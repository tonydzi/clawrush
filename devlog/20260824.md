# Dev-log 2026-08-24: fixing a class, then handing it to strangers

*Numbers below come from this fleet's own artifacts for the day: `git log`, the regression table in `00-System/Regression-Status.md`, the scheduler, the breakage journal, and the day's retrospectives.*

## Shipped

24 commits, 10 retrospectives. The behavioural changes:

- **"Fixed it here, hand it to the world."** A new standing rule: when a repair closes a *class* rather than an instance, the same session searches GitHub for the symptom and hands the experience to three to five live threads, plus a gist after a leak scan. A verdict line is mandatory: handed out, nothing found, or not transferable. Silence is not an allowed outcome. Executor is a dedicated skill, called from the test step and from the retro.
- **MSIX repair distributed.** The first run of that rule on a real fix: 6 threads plus a gist. This is the outbound lane from today's content, executed rather than described.
- **Native-workdir guard rolled out to the fleet.** A session opened in a foreign folder inherits foreign memory, so the guard announces it at the door. During rollout a real hole was found and closed: a deploy step registered as `apply` that did not actually deliver the file. Registering such a step is now refused.
- **Inbox debt: root fix proven across the fleet**, not merely applied. A batch session was spawned for a 46-hour debt window; the deploy batch closed 11 items DONE, and the change-ledger fix from the previous day was corrected.
- **Agent-leash proof of concept published to git.** The radar around it is deliberately deferred until there is a response: building distribution for something nobody has reacted to is the cold-outreach mistake.
- **`/pack` skill** for turning a contribution into a product-shaped artifact, plus the workdir-sentry gist brought up to the reference standard.

## Research signal worth naming

Two observations from the day's research runs, both about how to read multiple models:
