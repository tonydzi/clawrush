## Post 3: the agitational strategy, where the measured driver is response efficacy rather than fear

Claims under test: (a) does fear-based urgency work; (b) who pushes AI-native hardest; (c) "how to turn interest into action"; (d) is this a message problem at all.

### ⭐⭐⭐ (a) Fear appeals work, d = 0.29, and the backfire story is not in the data

- **Tannenbaum et al., 2015**: 248 samples, random-effects **d = 0.29** on attitudes, intentions and behaviours. The authors' own conclusion: no identified circumstances under which fear appeals backfire into undesirable outcomes.
- **Counter-claim**: Kok et al., 2018 - defensive responses (denial, reactance) when self-efficacy is low, per fear-control theory.
- **Reply, measured**: significant negative effect sizes in **6%** of low-reported-self-efficacy studies and **3%** of high-efficacy ones. No evidence that low-efficacy messages backfire.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The moderator is response efficacy, not fear intensity and not self-efficacy

**Bigsby and Albarracin, 2022, k = 158**: fear appeals influenced behavioural outcomes more strongly when they carried positive **response-efficacy** information (evidence the recommended action works), and **did not vary** as a function of self-efficacy information.

So the lever inside "you will professionally die" is not the threat. It is the paired demonstration that the proposed escape route functions. His own asset list - meetups several times a week, training, speakers, engineers showing work - IS the response-efficacy half. The strategy should be built on the proof, with the threat as framing, not the reverse.

⚠️ Hard limit on all of the above: these are health-behaviour meta-analyses. None covers marketing or community acquisition. d = 0.29 is not a marketing effect size and must not be quoted as one.
