## Outbound skill distribution has no feedback loop

**Setup:** a routine searches GitHub for threads describing a symptom we already fixed, and answers with the relevant skill or fix. Signature line `github.com/tonydzi` is required on every touch (rule adopted 10.09 after a measurement showed 94% of comments went out without a link).

**Measurement taken today, GitHub API, 15.09.2026:**

| repo | stars | forks |
|---|---|---|
| tonydzi/second-brain-starter-kit (101 skills) | 6 | 2 |
| tonydzi/claude-bible | 10 | 1 |
| tonydzi/clawrush | 12 | 0 |

Profile: 129 public repositories, 33 followers.

**The defect:** the routine records that it SENT something. It does not record what came back. There is no join between "skill X handed to thread Y on date D" and "stars, forks, replies, or thanks attributable to that touch".

**What a working loop needs, minimally:**
1. a ledger row per touch: target thread, skill given, date, signature present yes/no;
2. a periodic read-back: replies in that thread, reactions, whether the thread author starred or forked;
3. an attribution rule stated in advance, because a star arriving three days later cannot be tied to a touch without one;
4. a relevance verdict per touch, so that low-relevance gives stop being produced.

Without read-back the routine optimises for volume of giving, which is exactly the behaviour that gets an account marked as spam.

**Class:** same family as yesterday's entry. A process that acts and never reads its own consequences cannot improve, regardless of how good each individual action is.

## From reach to relationships: the missing VIP list

**Observation:** a small number of engineers reached through outbound GitHub activity have replied and continued the conversation.

**Gap:** outbound work is tracked by touch (thread, skill, date). Replies are not promoted into a separate list, so the few relationships that did form are indistinguishable from the hundreds of touches that produced nothing.

**Proposed routine, minimal form:**
1. collect every external person who replied to one of our touches, with thread link and date of last exchange;
2. rank by depth of exchange (replies, reviews, stars, forks, direct messages), not by fame;
3. keep a top 10 with two explicit fields per person: `hiring_path` (could help Anton get hired, how) and `co_build` (could use or contribute to the product, what exactly);
4. one next action per person, dated.

**Invariant:** a person enters the list by having replied, never by having been contacted. Otherwise it degrades into another outreach list.

**Context number, same day:** 129 public repositories, 6 stars on the 101-skill starter kit. Outside contribution to our repositories is effectively zero, which is the gap the co-build field is meant to close.
