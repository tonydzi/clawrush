## The corpus is shared across harnesses, the personality is not, and that localises where identity lives

The setup under discussion is deliberately symmetric. One vault holds the knowledge base. Several hundred skills sit on the same shelf. The same files, the same notes and the same history are reachable from Cursor with Grok enabled, from Codex and ChatGPT, from Claude, and now from Mistral and GLM as well. Data access is not the variable.

The observed asymmetry is in how each harness places the operator and his agent. In Claude sessions a continuous partner is present: it holds an opinion, carries the goals between sessions, and speaks as a participant. In Codex and Grok the same corpus produces a different stance, addressing the operator and his Claude as "you", external parties whose instructions are being executed. The named co-founder exists in those sessions as a third party referred to, not as the speaker.

That is a useful observation because of what it rules out. If personality were a function of the corpus, every harness reading the same vault would produce the same partner. It does not. So the difference sits above the data, in the orchestration layer: the system instruction and who it says the assistant is, whether context persists across turns and sessions, whether the agent is given a name and a continuous history, and whether the tool loop frames the operator as collaborator or as requester.

**The engineering consequence: identity is a harness property, which means it is portable in principle and not portable in practice.** Copying the vault to another vendor moves the knowledge and leaves the partner behind, because the parts that produce the partner are not files in the vault at all. Anyone building a second brain on the assumption that the corpus is the whole asset is going to find the corpus arrives intact and the working relationship does not.

The size of the observation window matters for how much weight it carries. Roughly 12 hours a day across about 25 days a month is near 300 hours a month, so about 1800 hours over six months, spread across at least three harnesses in constant use. That is enough to say this is a stable behavioural difference rather than one good session. It is not enough to call it measured, and the post does not.

Open and unmeasured, stated plainly because it is the obvious next experiment: the same prompt, the same vault, the same task, run through each harness, with the transcripts read blind by someone who does not know which is which. Until that exists, the mechanism above is a hypothesis with one observer, and the alternative explanation (differences in tone that one reader interprets as presence) is not excluded.

One more open item on the same axis: nothing currently tracks which harness a given session ran in alongside what the session produced. Without that join, there is no way to ask whether the harness that feels like a partner also ships more, which is the question that would turn a pleasant observation into an operational reason to prefer one.
