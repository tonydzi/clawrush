### ⭐⭐ The dependency did not disappear, it moved

The post's thesis is that the hostage relationship is solved: instead of depending on unreliable staff, you depend on synthetic assistants who do not sulk, do not need raises and do not have to be fired.

## Post: slow machines are a gift, and the bottleneck is the architecture

### ⭐ Recall result: this post answers a question he asked 9 days ago

Case `the-second-brain-needs-maintenance`, Facebook post of **26 October**: *"my pain of the day is that the machines drag badly when I have 10, 20, 30 sessions... the computer is super powerful, 128GB of RAM, and it drags badly. I do not know what to blame. I am looking for what is dragging."*

Today: *"constant reindexing of my second brain is eating resources... the problem is not the power of my machines, it is the architecture."*

So this is not a repeat, it is a **closure**. Nine days between the question and the answer, and the answer names a specific process rather than a feeling. Worth marking because this week's other briefs mostly restated themselves; this one resolved.

⚠️ Two numbers to flag, neither of them an error on his side. The 26 October post says **128GB**, today's says **120GB** for the same Mac Studio. And today's says **8 computers** while the node registry holds **6**: the registry is the one that is behind, because the Mac Studio (bought 27 October) and the Mac Pro were never added to it. On the machine count he is right and our own instrument is stale.

The distilled layers the second brain was built for (concepts, insights, bridges) come to **3,350 files, about 1.3%**. Raw conversation dumps are **46.3%**. So "the architecture is crooked" is exactly right, and the shape of the crookedness is now specific: the index is built over a quarter of a million files of which roughly one in eighty is distillate.

⚠️ Caveat: this is this node's copy and the counting excludes the bus and version archives, so it is a measurement of what sits under the vault root rather than a definitive index manifest. The proportion is the point, not the last digit.

### ⚠️ We already have the rule this violates

Our own standing note says it plainly: only signal goes into the vault and RAG, not raw material, and distillation beats volume. That rule exists and 46% of the vault is raw conversation exports. So the failure is not an unknown architectural question; it is a known rule with no enforcement, which is the same class as the outreach ledger that recorded 34 days of silence and told nobody.

### What follows from the numbers rather than from the feeling

His conclusion, that buying more machines only postpones the reckoning, is correct and the table is why: adding RAM multiplies the speed at which you walk 260,435 files, it does not reduce the count. The two levers that do are both selection, not power:

1. stop indexing what is not distillate (46.3% of the file count is one folder)
2. make reindexing incremental rather than constant, so cost scales with change rather than with corpus size

Neither needs a faster machine and neither needs new research.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. Decide what `01-Conversations` is for. 120,464 files and 1.3 GB of raw exports are either source material that should sit outside the index, or they are the brain, and right now they are treated as both.
2. Put the vault-only-signal rule behind something that can say no, since a rule with no gate is indistinguishable from no rule.
3. Add the Mac Studio and the Mac Pro to the node registry. He counted 8, the registry says 6, and on this one the registry is wrong.
4. Reconcile 120GB against 128GB across the two posts, since both figures are his and a reader comparing them will notice.
5. Measure reindex cost per run before optimizing it. This entry measures the corpus, not the process, and the process is what he says is eating the machine.

## Post 4: monitoring on every machine first, Screenpipe as the first suspect

### ⭐ Recall result: sixth post of this class in sixteen days, first one with an experiment

Cases in the chain, by Facebook publication date: `six-computers-in-one-swarm` 21 Oct, `forty-sessions-and-128-gigs-still-crawl` 22 Oct, `the-second-brain-needs-maintenance` 26 Oct ("I do not know what to blame"), `screenpipe-is-letting-me-down` 30 Oct, `slow-machines-are-a-gift` 4 Nov 11:55 UTC, this one 4 Nov 15:30 UTC. Five of the six propose a cause by reasoning. This one proposes a falsifiable test: stop Screenpipe on one machine, measure whether the symptom persists.

### The request is genuinely unbuilt, which is why the previous five had to guess

Routine register: **264** entries. Matching process/perf/monitor/cpu/load: **7**, and six of those are sync, transport, downloads or Screenpipe harvest. The only one touching processes is `Proc Patrol`, whose register description is "kills known zombies from a list", which is a kill list, not an instrument. No resource monitoring exists on any fleet node. The two performance investigations on record were both manual, both dated 7 July 2026, both on Windows machines (hub + ZBook laptop).

### ⚠️ Both prior investigations found a different root, and neither was Screenpipe

- **ZBook laptop, 7 July:** root was Claude Desktop left open for days. One instance at 31 hours uptime held **324** child processes and 18.5GB; **28** accumulated `telegram-mcp/main.py` copies at ~200MB each totalled 5.4GB. Hardware was not starved: 79.7GB RAM, CPU ~6%, GPU ~1%.
- **Hub, 7 July to 8 July:** hardware not starved either (128GB RAM at ~19%, i7-14700F at ~10%). Findings: 8 orphaned powershell processes, and `TextInputHost` burning **57 hours of CPU across 3 days** of uptime (known Windows looping bug, a full core wasted). The note closes with a standing instruction: *check TextInputHost first at any future slowdown.*
- Neither investigation implicated Screenpipe. Both implicated accumulation of our own long-running processes, which is step 2 of his plan, not step 3.

### ⚠️ Step order inverts the experiment

Step 1 installs monitoring; step 3 stops Screenpipe completely. Stopping before a baseline exists yields one bit (symptom gone / not gone) and discards the measurement that identifies the actual consumer. Correct order for the same cost: monitor for a few hours, capture the baseline, stop one component, monitor again. Without the baseline, a negative result ("still slow after stopping Screenpipe") leaves the next suspect unranked, and the series returns to reasoning.

### Cost of the stop, with the boundary named

- Screenpipe free tier retains history for **24 hours**; `raw_sql` returns 403 `history_access_limited`.
- Harvest is 2x/day on the **hub**: `Screenpipe-Vault-Harvest-Night` 03:40 + `Screenpipe-Vault-Harvest-Day` 15:40, via `_imports/screenpipe/sp_harvest.py`. Raw JSONL to `_originals/screenpipe/`, daily note to `01-Conversations/ambient-screenpipe/sp-DATE.md`. A gap over 24h is an unrecoverable hole; the script emits an alarm line to `~/.screenpipe/claude-pipeline/sp_log.jsonl`.
- Downstream: alpha miner `_imports/alpha/screenpipe_scan.py` (0 LLM detector, ledger `screenpipe_ledger.db`) feeding `/alpha-judge screenpipe`. Ten `screenpipe-*` skills on a shelf of **279**.
- ⭐ Those routines run on the hub, not on the Mac Pro. Stopping Screenpipe on the Mac Pro does **not** break the pipeline. The exposure is steps 7 and 8, where the audit reaches the Hub and "remove old and unused software" meets a component that is neither.

### ⭐ Prior art on distrusting this tool: its health endpoint lied for 5.4 days

Hub, 30 September: the `Callisto7200` capture stream died `(disconnected)` ~200ms after start while `/health` reported `active (last activity: 0s ago)` continuously, because "last activity" there is the age of the last *start*, not of the last data. Measured dead window **25 Sep 12:27 to 30 Sep**, i.e. **5.4 days**, during which the Default Communications Device was not reaching the database as a separate channel. Fixes: per-channel watchdog `screenpipe_pulse.py`, transcription engine switched from Parakeet to `whisper-large-v3-turbo`, upstream bug report filed. Gotchas file for all agents: `docs/screenpipe-gotchas.md`.

### ⚠️ Two debts this post surfaces

- **The DR he commissioned in public on 30 October is not in the register.** `_DR-Registry.md` holds **667** orders; the two Screenpipe entries are `DR26-08-30-HUB-01-1446` (parked: named consumer never built) and `DR26-09-15-HUB-01-1111` (applied: screen capture vs audio). The newest order of any kind is dated **2 October**, which is **33 days** with none. The removal in steps 5 and 6 is therefore scheduled ahead of the comparison he asked for.
- **`task-2026-09-15-screenpipe-capture-watchdog-and-screen-digest`: state open, P1, owner claude, 50 days.** It is the task that would have caught this class, created from the DR that *was* applied.

### ⭐ Registry stale, author right

`fleet_nodes.json` lists **6** nodes: FLEET-ANCHOR (linux VPS), A-2022BAYAREA (hub), ZBOOKG8-2023PAL, MacBook-Anton, MyOwnPC-Natalia, MacBook-Ruslana. The **Mac Pro** is absent, as is the Mac Studio bought 27 October. The machine this plan starts from is unknown to the fleet, and "roll out across the whole fleet" resolves to six machines excluding it.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. Build the monitor before stopping anything: baseline, stop, re-measure. One instrument closes a sixteen-day loop of guessing.
2. Check TextInputHost and accumulated Claude Desktop/MCP processes first, per the 7 July standing instruction, since that is where both prior roots were found.
