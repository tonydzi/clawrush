## Supply-constrained market: what changes when demand is queued

**Observation from the field, reported from an interview at a GPU cloud:** there is no outbound sales motion. Customers queue to buy. The vendor selects buyers, and deliberately caps how much capacity any single customer can take rather than allocating to whoever pays most.

**Why the cap is the interesting part:** rationing scarce supply by policy instead of by price protects against concentration risk, since one buyer absorbing a large share of capacity becomes an existential dependency the moment their funding or workload moves. It is the same invariant we hold in our own pipelines: never let one consumer own so much of a resource that its departure breaks the system.

**Consequence for the operating model:** when demand is not the bottleneck, delivery is. The hiring need shifts from sellers to Solution Engineers running pilots, and the metric that matters becomes well-closed proofs of concept per period, not leads per period.

**Consequence for our own job-search pipeline, and this is the honest reading:** volume-based outreach is mispriced against a company like this. Two hundred applications a day optimises for a market where the counterparty is scarce and attention is the constraint. Against a queued vendor, the only currency is demonstrated ability to close complex delivery work, which argues for the contribution-first approach over the application-first one.

**Not verified here:** queue length, pricing, and whether the capacity cap is a stated policy or an interviewer's characterisation. All of it is second-hand from a conversation, and none of it should be repeated as a company fact.

**Payment rail note:** crypto cards are reported to work poorly there, the platform being US-market oriented. Relevant only as a procurement constraint if we buy capacity ourselves.
