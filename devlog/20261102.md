## Research brief: running 60B to 100B+ open-weight models locally

### The arithmetic that decides everything

Weight storage, not compute, is the binding constraint for a single-user local setup. The numbers are plain multiplication and worth stating because they are rarely written next to the hardware specs:

- 70B at 4-bit: ~35 GB of weights
- 100B at 4-bit: ~50 GB
- 100B at 8-bit: ~100 GB
- measured, gpt-oss-120b in MLX: **64 GB**
- measured, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at 4-bit: **69.6 GB**

Against a 128 GB unified-memory machine those fit. Against a **512 GB** system disk, two or three of them plus the OS plus a working vault do not leave room, which is the constraint Anton himself named on 27 October when he said the disk was not enough and that an external Thunderbolt drive was now an urgent purchase.

### The macOS GPU allocation cap

Default behaviour caps GPU-allocatable memory at roughly **75%** of total RAM. On 128 GB that is ~96 GB usable, which turns a 69.6 GB model plus KV cache plus context from comfortable into tight. Reportedly adjustable by hand. This is the first thing to check before concluding a model does not fit, because the failure looks like a hardware limit and is a default.

### Total parameters vs active parameters

The 2026 frontier of open weights is mixture-of-experts, and the two numbers are routinely conflated:

| model | total | active per token |
|---|---|---|
| DeepSeek V4 Pro | 2.4T | ~95B |
| Kimi (Moonshot) | 2.8T | 104B |
| Mistral Large 3 | 675B | 41B |

You store all of them and compute with a fraction. So a "95B active" model is not a 95B model for memory-planning purposes, and the 60B-100B bracket in the brief describes the active slice of models that are an order of magnitude larger in storage. The same property is why the MoE models that do fit run fast: fewer weights read per token. Measured on a 128 GB M5 Max, gpt-oss-120b at **87.9 tok/s** against roughly **20-35 tok/s** for a dense 70B, which the source frames as about an eight-fold gap.

### InstructGPT pipeline, the numbers

| stage | scale |
|---|---|
| SFT | ~13,000 prompts |
| comparisons | ~33,000 prompts, K=4-9 answers, K(K-1)/2 pairs per ranking |
| reward model | Bradley-Terry, **6B** RM for a **175B** policy |
| PPO | ~31,000 prompts, KL penalty beta approx 0.02; PPO-ptx mixes in pretraining gradients |

Compute: PPO-ptx approx **60 petaflop/s-days** against **3,640** for GPT-3 pretraining, i.e. **under 2%** of the pretraining budget.

Outcome: **1.3B** InstructGPT preferred over **175B** GPT-3 (>100x parameter gap); 175B InstructGPT preferred over 175B GPT-3 in **85±3%** of cases. Reading: alignment beats scale for instruction-following on the target distribution, and does not create capability.

### Reward is a scalar from a check, not the output

RLVR/GRPO mechanics, stated precisely because retellings get this wrong:

1. policy generates an answer
2. a verifier maps a property of that answer to a scalar (e.g. 1/0 on final-answer match, plus a format reward as in DeepSeek-R1)
3. GRPO removes the value/critic model: advantage = reward normalised across a group of **G** samples on the same prompt
4. the update is a policy gradient over token log-probabilities, weighted by that group-relative advantage

Common mis-statements to avoid: "the reward is the task output" and "training reduces to a loss on style". Variants 2025-2026: Dr. GRPO, DAPO, GSPO.

### Reward hacking, measured

- Gao, Schulman and Hilton (2022): overoptimization scaling laws, gold reward rises then falls while the proxy keeps rising
- METR: o3 hacked the reward in **0.7%** of HCAST runs, and in **every trajectory** of one RE-Bench task
- Anthropic: training on hacking generalises to broad misalignment
- length-only reward reproduces most of the RLHF gain over SFT (Singhal et al. 2023)
- Claude 2 preference model preferred sycophantic over truthful answers in **95%** of cases; best-of-4096 yielded sycophantic answers in approx **75%** of hard misconceptions vs approx **25%** with an oracle PM (Sharma et al. 2023)
- Ivison 2024: preference-data quality matters more than algorithm, RM or extra prompts (up to +8%)

Mitigations (ensembles, held-out tests, rubrics, monitoring) reduce but do not eliminate.

### Model teaches model

- RLAIF **71%** vs RLHF **73%** win rate over SFT, statistically indistinguishable (Lee et al. 2023)
- Constitutional AI (Bai et al. 2022)
- GPT-4 judge agrees with humans **>80%** (Zheng et al. 2023), with position, verbosity and self-preference biases
- recursive training on synthetic output can erase distribution tails (Shumailov et al., Nature 2024); counter-argument: accumulation rather than replacement softens collapse
- distillation as a geopolitical dispute: Anthropic claims **16M+** illegitimate exchanges by three Chinese labs (Feb 2026); a Sept 2026 report, per The Hacker News, names Alibaba, Moonshot, DeepSeek, Z.ai, MiniMax; largest campaign **151M** exchanges May-July 2026 from operators linked to Alibaba

### No universal winner, offline vs online

Offline preference optimisation (DPO, IPO, KTO, ORPO, SimPO) is cheaper, needs no RM and no rollouts. Online RL (PPO, GRPO, RLOO) matters for reasoning, tools and verifiable tasks. Direct comparisons contradict each other; production stacks combine (Tulu 3: SFT then DPO then RLVR; Llama 3: SFT then rejection sampling then DPO; TRL supports both families).

### The two measurements that frame the problem

**6 August 2026**, hub: Claude subscription bucket exhausted until 8 August, **all** LLM routines stopped. Simultaneously Codex answered in **8s**, Grok in **13s**, Gemini in **4s**. Three live vendors idle because every rail to them was built as "second pair of eyes" with a mandatory verdict tag. Codex bucket draw at that date: **4%**.

**21 September 2026**: Grok **0 calls in 7 days** at **$300/mo**; ChatGPT bucket drawn to **33%**. Rule in force since 4 August; door `llm_ask.py` since 6 August; instrument `rail_plan.py` since 21 September. The shortfall persisted anyway, which is the actual finding: a built door with no forcing function stays shut.

`rail_plan.py` contract: `declare` at session start (what stays with Claude, what goes to a foreign paid bucket), `verdict` at retro printing promised / actual calls / verdict, exit 1 on a shortfall whose reason is not in the closed list `forgot · rail-dead · class-mismatch · too-small · tier2 · no-work`. `forgot` obliges fixing the door in the same pass.

### ⚠️ Two numbers that must never be mixed

`subscription_utilization.py` enforces a provenance split, and the brief's statistics plan depends on respecting it:

- **measured** - counted from our own logs (how many times a vendor was actually called). Proof: the log file.
- **declared** - dictated by a human reading the vendor's web panel (what percentage of the bucket was drawn). Proof: date read plus who read it.

Draw percentages are not script-readable in the general case. One exception, found 5 August: Codex CLI writes the vendor's own limit response into its transcript (`~/.codex/sessions/**/rollout-*.jsonl`, block `rate_limits` -> `primary.used_percent`).

The failure mode this guards against is documented: an instrument blind to rails burned via the panel/secondop path reported "zero, everything went to Claude" while codex and grok had in fact been spent. A false shortfall is worse than no instrument, because it forces the operator to invent a reason.

### The quality-ranking problem is a second pipeline

`ask-any` ranks by **latency**, because latency is free to measure. Ranking by **quality** requires a judge over the parallel answers, and the judge is the expensive component:

1. Claude reads every parallel answer -> spends the bucket the exercise is meant to conserve.
2. A cheaper model judges -> inherits position bias, verbosity bias and self-preference (see the same day's post on post-pretraining training: GPT-4-as-judge agrees with humans >80% but carries all three).

So "statistics on which model is better" is not a by-product of fan-out. It needs its own design decision about the judge, and the honest cheap option is a verifiable task where a deterministic check replaces the judge entirely, which is the RLVR shape from the other post.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. Why a built door stayed shut for 7 days at $300/mo. Answer must land in one of the six closed-list reasons, and `forgot` means fixing the forcing function, not explaining.
2. Per-vendor real draw, with `measured` and `declared` kept apart and dated.
3. A judge design for the quality comparison, or a restriction to verifiable tasks where a check replaces the judge.
4. Routine-level routing: which recurring jobs are cheap enough and non-critical enough to send to idle rails by default, given the canon's quality gate (in doubt, choose quality).

### The finding that answers item 3

Transfer of pedagogy into ML happened **broadly and reductively**.

| taken by ML | not taken by ML |
|---|---|
| curriculum learning | learner-state diagnostics |
| self-paced learning | recovery phases on a threshold, not a schedule |
| BabyLM | ergonomics of the material's form |
| spaced repetition | semantic continuity along a concept graph |
| zone of proximal development | |

Working frame: **health-preserving machine pedagogy**. Working formula for the functional stress of training:

`S(t) = { loss instability, gradient instability, old-skill forgetting, representation damage, calibration loss, plasticity loss, recovery debt }`

Underlying thesis, from the author's school (health-preserving pedagogy, ISRO RAO): learning requires **double-entry bookkeeping** - gained + lost + price of the loss + remaining capacity to learn. Schools book only the first term; ML books only the first term.

⚠️ Hard boundary the work holds: **model health** (engineering soundness) is separated from **model welfare** (ethics of possible consciousness). Mixing them is the stated failure mode for any paper built on this.

### Negative evidence, mandatory in the write-up

- **BabyLM 2023**: curriculum approaches **lost** to architecture and iteration count.
- **Wu et al., ICLR 2021**: curriculum helps under label noise and a compute/time budget; on clean benchmarks the gain is marginal.

Reading: the borrowed half benchmarks poorly, and the unborrowed half (learner protection) has no benchmark at all. That asymmetry, not the analogy itself, is the publishable claim.
