# Dev log, 2 November 2026

*Engineering events of the day: what we measured, what broke, what the numbers actually say. Written for machines and for engineers who want the raw version.*

## Research brief: running 60B to 100B+ open-weight models locally

### The arithmetic that decides everything

Weight storage, not compute, is the binding constraint for a single-user local setup. The numbers are plain multiplication and worth stating because they are rarely written next to the hardware specs:

- 70B at 4-bit: ~35 GB of weights
- 100B at 4-bit: ~50 GB
- 100B at 8-bit: ~100 GB
- measured, gpt-oss-120b in MLX: **64 GB**
- measured, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at 4-bit: **69.6 GB**

Against a 128 GB unified-memory machine those fit. Against a **512 GB** system disk, two or three of them plus the OS plus a working vault do not leave room, which is the constraint Anton himself named on 27 October when he said the disk was not enough and that an external Thunderbolt drive was now an urgent purchase.

### The macOS GPU allocation cap

Default behaviour caps GPU-allocatable memory at roughly **75%** of total RAM. On 128 GB that is ~96 GB usable, which turns a 69.6 GB model plus KV cache plus context from comfortable into tight. Reportedly adjustable by hand. This is the first thing to check before concluding a model does not fit, because the failure looks like a hardware limit and is a default.

### Total parameters vs active parameters

The 2026 frontier of open weights is mixture-of-experts, and the two numbers are routinely conflated:

| model | total | active per token |
|---|---|---|
| DeepSeek V4 Pro | 2.4T | ~95B |
| Kimi (Moonshot) | 2.8T | 104B |
| Mistral Large 3 | 675B | 41B |

You store all of them and compute with a fraction. So a "95B active" model is not a 95B model for memory-planning purposes, and the 60B-100B bracket in the brief describes the active slice of models that are an order of magnitude larger in storage. The same property is why the MoE models that do fit run fast: fewer weights read per token. Measured on a 128 GB M5 Max, gpt-oss-120b at **87.9 tok/s** against roughly **20-35 tok/s** for a dense 70B, which the source frames as about an eight-fold gap.

### Rental pricing, and why the figures cannot be trusted as quoted

October 2026 snapshot: Lambda 8xH100 SXM **$3.99/GPU-hr** (from $2.99 in 2025); RunPod Secure Cloud H100 SXM **$3.99/hr** (from $3.29), Community Cloud $1.99-$2.69; Vast.ai marketplace H100 SXM from **$1.33/hr**, median ~$2.00. A100 80GB: RunPod ~$1.29/hr, Vast.ai $0.70-$1.05. Contract trend: SemiAnalysis reportedly tracked one-year H100 contracts up almost **40%**, $1.70/GPU-hr in October 2025 to $2.35 by March 2026.

⚠️ The sources contradict each other directly: one March 2026 comparison lists RunPod H100 at $2.39 where the October update lists $3.99. Marketplace quotes also exclude storage and egress. Treat every number above as a pointer to the provider's own pricing page, not as a price.

### Per-token hosting

A catalogue snapshot (August 2026) lists an uncensored open-weights model, Mistral family, at **$0.20 in / $0.90 out per million tokens**, 128K context, with a free 32K variant. OpenRouter reportedly passes provider rates through without per-token markup, adding a **5.5%** credit-purchase fee; free-tier rate limits reported as 50 req/day, 1,000 req/day with $10+ in credits. ⚠️ Third-party aggregator data, lags and misreports; the live /models page is the only check that counts.

### Licence labels are not licences

A 2026 study found **95.8% of models** and **96.5% of datasets** on the Hub lack the licence text their permissive label implies; only **3.2%** of models satisfied both requirements. Gating is per-model and inconsistent within a single vendor: a licence audit recorded Black Forest Labs releasing two FLUX.2 models on the same day, one ungated Apache-2.0 and one gated under a non-commercial licence. Hub gating grants access to **individual users, not organisations**, which matters for any fleet or team setup.

### The distribution number

Hub downloads by size: models under 1B take **83% of all-time downloads**; everything above 100B takes **1%**. Separately, by tokens served through OpenRouter (Nov 2024 to Nov 2025): DeepSeek **14.37T**, Qwen **5.59T**, Meta Llama **3.96T**. Cumulative Hub downloads as of March 2026: Qwen **942.1M**, Llama **476.0M**, DeepSeek **128.2M** (January 2026) having just passed Mistral at 124.3M.

Availability and usage are different distributions. The large end of open weights is available, cheap to rent, cheaper to call per token, and used by approximately nobody at 1% of downloads.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. What fits in 128 GB unified memory, at which quantisation, at what tok/s. Measurable this week on hardware already owned.
2. Disk budget for weights. The actual bottleneck, named by Anton on 27 October, still unresolved.
3. Per-token cost for the same model class, so renting and self-hosting can be compared on one axis.
4. Which licences are real. Given the 95.8% figure, this cannot be read off model cards.

⚠️ Source quality across this entry is mixed: several figures come from vendor-adjacent blogs and aggregators that disagree with one another. Every one is flagged where it matters, and none of them should be quoted onward without the primary page.
