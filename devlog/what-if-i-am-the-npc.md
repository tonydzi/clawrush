# Devlog: Writing “What If I Am the NPC?” Without Turning a Metaphor Into a Fact

## Source tension

The source post combines four different questions:

1. Could AI become conscious?
2. Do humans have free will?
3. Are humans comparable to biological AGI?
4. What happens to consciousness after death?

They are related, but none answers the others automatically. The writing task was to preserve Anton's emotional question while preventing one analogy from being presented as proof of another.

## Editorial rule

The central sentence was treated as a thought experiment:

> What if I am the NPC?

The longread explicitly separates literal and metaphorical readings. There is no evidence offered that people are externally controlled non-player characters. The metaphor is retained because it gives a modern shape to the older problem of agency under biological and social constraints.

## Claims kept separate

The article does not collapse these distinctions:

- thought exists vs. free will exists;
- behavior is general-purpose vs. subjective experience exists;
- a process has causes vs. a person has no meaningful agency;
- consciousness depends on the body vs. consciousness ends at death;
- another person behaves predictably vs. another person lacks inner experience.

Each pair requires an additional argument. The source post raises the bridge; the longread does not pretend the bridge has been proven.

## Structure

The final structure moves through seven layers:

1. the NPC metaphor;
2. the limited conclusion of the cogito;
3. biological and social causes;
4. determinist, libertarian, and compatibilist answers;
5. the biological-AGI analogy;
6. the ethical danger of dehumanizing others;
7. the practical value of reflection even inside a causal system.

The final section on death is intentionally short. The source asks the question, but neither the NPC metaphor nor current AI behavior supplies evidence about post-mortem consciousness.

## Language constraints

The longread avoids presenting any of these as established facts:

- that an AI system is conscious;
- that humans lack free will;
- that consciousness survives death;
- that consciousness ends at death;
- that other people are NPCs;
- that biological and artificial intelligence have the same inner life.

The piece also avoids diagnosing the author. The source is written as philosophical uncertainty, not as a report of immediate danger or loss of contact with reality.

## Reusable test for similar posts

For each metaphysical statement, ask:

1. Is this an observation, analogy, hypothesis, or conclusion?
2. What evidence would distinguish it from alternatives?
3. Does the conclusion contain more certainty than the source supports?
4. Could the framing reduce other people to objects?
5. What practical claim remains valid if the metaphysics is unresolved?

For this post, the practical residue is strong: people do not choose every impulse, but reflection, environment design, and habit formation can still alter future behavior.
