## Always-loaded instruction files: research the field before restructuring

**Request as stated:** a deep research run on how other developers optimise always-loaded agent instruction files, specifically CLAUDE.md and MEMORY.md. Explicit constraint: apply nothing yet, return options.

**Named search surfaces:** GitHub, general developer forums, Reddit. Named artifact classes: instruction files for Claude, Gemini, Grok and Cursor (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md and equivalents). MEMORY.md is called out separately, and the distinction is the useful part: it is to be studied as *memory logic in a project*, not as a document that happens to be long.

**Why this file class deserves a research step at all, and this is the transferable bit:** an always-loaded file is charged on every run. Its structure is a standing cost, not a one-time cost, so the usual method of trimming it by feel optimises the wrong variable: it reduces characters, which is visible, rather than reducing the per-run tax of a bad structure, which is not. Reading how others solved it is cheap compared to discovering a bad restructure through three weeks of degraded sessions.

**Sequencing, stated as a constraint rather than a preference:** research → options side by side → comparison with the current approach → only then any change. The owner's own line names the risk honestly: he optimises these files himself today and may not be doing it right. A self-assessed method is the weakest kind of evidence about itself.

**Outbound half of the task:** after the survey, check who has published on the topic on GitHub, and contribute our own approach back as a skill, a process or another shareable form, aimed at people working on the same problem. Reading the field creates the obligation to publish into it.

**Not measured:** everything. No token counts, no before-and-after on file size, no claim that our current structure is good or bad. The honest state is an open research request with the application step deliberately blocked.
