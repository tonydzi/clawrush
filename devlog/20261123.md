## Post 1: Argon vs Carbon - 9 index points apart, tied on Arena Text, same token price, and his eval hit the tie

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (b) The gain is uneven, and this explains his null eval result

| benchmark | delta (Carbon over Argon) |
|---|---|

**Actionable:** an eval that wants to separate these two must load on SWE-style repair or graph traversal. A chat-shaped eval will return "no difference" by construction.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (c) ⚠️ Apple does not expose any of it: the public API gives the passport page, the labels live in a reverse-engineered private index

`PHAsset` exposes `mediaType`, `mediaSubtypes`, `contentType`, `sourceType`, `pixelWidth/Height`, `creationDate`, `addedDate`, `modificationDate`, `location`, `duration`, `isFavorite`, `isHidden`, `hasAdjustments`, `rating`, `burstIdentifier`, `playbackStyle`, `extendedMetadata`. **No faces, no people, no scene labels, no keywords.** Apple's developer forums state outright that "there is no api to access the People album".

Where the data actually sits:

- `Photos.sqlite` (Core Data, `Z` prefix): `ZPERSON` (with `ZKEYFACE`), `ZDETECTEDFACE`, `ZKEYWORD` for manual keywords, `ZASSET` for assets.
- ML labels are **not** in `Photos.sqlite`. They live in the search index: `database/search/psi.sqlite` (tables `ga`, `groups`, `assets`; UUID assembled from `uuid_0` + `uuid_1`), and on **macOS 27** a new `leo.sqlite` with `lexicon` and `items`. Numbered categories: `LABEL 4000`, `RICH_LABEL 4010`, `LANDMARK 4020`, `HUMAN_ACTION 4060`, `ACTIVITY 4090`, `PLACE_NAME 2060`, `PERSON 3001`, `PET 3010`.
- The schema is undocumented and moves. Verbatim from the osxphotos wiki: "Most of what is described in the following comes from reverse engineering what Photos is doing... I had to make many, many assumptions and some of them are surely wrong." Release 0.78.1 exists because Apple "renumbered the album, keyword and share participant tables" in a macOS 27.3 beta.
- ⚠️ `ZSCENECLASSIFICATION` and `ZASSETANALYSISSTATE`, two table names in common circulation, could not be confirmed in any source. Do not build on them.

Export is no escape hatch either: Photos offers "Export IPTC as XMP", which carries **manually assigned** keywords and IPTC fields. Recognised scene labels and person names are not written into EXIF/XMP on export; ⚠️ that is proven by the absence of any such option on Apple's pages, not by an Apple statement.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (d) ⭐⭐ The reader is already installed: `osxphotos`, and it is the library our own `/photo-leads` routine runs on

`osxphotos` 0.78.1, released **8 November 2026**, macOS only, Python 3.10+, read-only. `PhotoInfo` exposes precisely the fields he wants generated:

- `labels` and `labels_normalized` - "labels applied to photo by Photos image categorization" (Photos 5+ only; empty list on older libraries)
- `persons`, `person_info` (`PersonInfo`), `face_info` (`FaceInfo`)
- `keywords`, `search_info` (`SearchInfo`), `score` (`ScoreInfo`)
- CLI has a `labels` command and a `--label` filter; export writes sidecars
- `examples/recover_person_names.py` copies person names onto photos as keywords and/or captions - the one legal way to get names out

Related tooling: `dogsheep-photos` (Simon Willison) loads `Photos.sqlite` + `psi.sqlite` into Datasette and is where the joins were first published; `apple-photos-forensics` and the mac4n6 blog document `ZDETECTEDFACE`/`ZPERSON`. ⚠️ Current activity of those three not verified.

⭐ New doors as of this autumn, both public: `MediaIntelligence` (macOS 27) with `FaceGroupAnalyzer`, which "detects faces in images and groups them by person" into a working directory you own - a face-grouping engine, **not** access to Photos' People.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (e) ⭐⭐ The missing piece, a caption in a sentence, became free and local five weeks ago - and only on the Mac Studio

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (j) ⚠️⚠️ The measured answer to "inject the most correct context" is: inject less. 194,480 calls say so

**Chroma, "Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance"** (Hong, Troynikov, Huber, 14.07.2025): 18 models - 5 Anthropic, 7 OpenAI, 3 Google, 3 Alibaba - across 8 input lengths and 11 needle positions, **194,480 calls**, 69 refusals (0.035%).

The line that lands on his post: on LongMemEval, 306 prompts, **the full input of about 113k tokens against a focused input of about 300 tokens, and every single model performs better on the focused one.** The largest gap belongs to **Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4**, mostly through abstentions. Other results: a shuffled haystack scores **better** than a logically ordered one across all 18 models; a single distractor already lowers accuracy and four lower it further; in Repeated Words (1,090 variations, 25 to 10,000 words) every model degrades, Gemini breaking into random words around 500-750 words. ⚠️ Vendor publication - Chroma sells a vector database - and the exact "degradation starts at N tokens" numbers live only in the charts, not in the text.

**Anthropic's own figures for doing retrieval properly** ("Introducing Contextual Retrieval", 19.09.2024), failure rate at top-20 chunks: **5.7% → 3.7%** with contextual embeddings (−35%), **→ 2.9%** adding contextual BM25 (−49%), **→ 1.9%** adding reranking (−67%). ⚠️ Metric is 1 − recall@20; dataset size not stated.

**mem0's own paper** (arXiv 2504.19413): p95 latency about 17.1 s for full context against about 1.4 s (−91%), tokens per query **26,031 against 1,764** (−90%). ⚠️ But on accuracy full context is **higher** - a third-party summary gives 66.88 for mem0 against 77.51 for full context on gpt-4o-mini LoCoMo, and mem0 itself concedes it trails "computationally prohibitive full-context". The famous "+26% accuracy" is against OpenAI's memory, not against full context.

⛔ **No published before/after measurement of startup-context injection for an agentic CLI exists.** I looked. The closest piece does retrieval on demand rather than at start, and its numbers are borrowed (a vendor's "~40% token saving" plus the author's own observation that search costs a few thousand tokens on a 1.2M-LOC repo while the file-reading fallback is "routinely 30k").

So the honest engineering answer to his question is not "assemble more". It is: assemble **small and relevant**, which is exactly the shape of a retrieval call, and the published numbers say the gain from doing the retrieval properly - contextual embeddings plus BM25 plus reranking - is a two-thirds cut in misses, while the gain from pouring in everything is negative.
