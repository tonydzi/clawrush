# 29 October 2026 - dev log

*Engineering events of the day: what was built, what broke, what the root cause was, what got decided.*

## Publishing transcripts is blocked by one clause in the request, and we measured how big that clause is

Today's task asks to publish session transcripts as training material for other models, to turn on detailed reasoning everywhere so the published thinking is worth learning from, to find as many platforms as possible, and to depersonalize before publishing. Logged because three of the four parts are straightforward and the fourth decides the project.

**Size of the corpus.** This node: **849 transcripts, 5 projects, 2.30 GB**, largest file **202 MB**, median **1.3 MB**. Up roughly **8 files in 24 hours**, so this is a live stream, not a fixed archive, and anything built for it has to run continuously rather than once.

**What a random sample contains.** 25 files, 28 MB, scanned for categories rather than contents. Token or key shaped strings: **134 hits across 15 of 25 files**. Password-like assignments: **11 across 4**. Emails: **216 across 21**. Phone numbers: **96 across 17**. Account-bearing local paths: **204 across 12**. Deliberately not reproducing any of it. The number that matters for sequencing is **60%**: that share of a random sample carries something key-shaped, so a per-file human review is not a feasible plan at 849 files and rising.

**Tooling: a scrubber exists, the right scrubber does not.** Two cleaners are live and each has its own test: one for robot profiles, one run before a repository release. Both target structured material, config files and profile fields, where a secret sits in a known key. A transcript is prose with tool output embedded in it, and the same secret appears mid-sentence, inside a quoted command, or inside a pasted error. Pointing an existing scrubber at it would produce confident green on a file that still leaks. ⚠️ One of those two cleaners already has a recorded defect of exactly the confidence sort: its test fixtures once wrote into the live evidence folder and **426 of 450 files there turned out to be test debris**. A cleaner whose own hygiene failed once should not be trusted on a new material class without a red test first.

**The reasoning request has a precise and unexpected answer.** The routing table describes **6 rails** with plan, monthly price and machine-readability of quota per rail. Fields governing reasoning depth: **none**. The parameter appears in **2 scripts only**, hardcoded in their logic. So nothing can be "switched on in settings", because the setting does not exist: the work is adding the field to the rails table and teaching the two doors to read it. Small, but a build.

**Order that follows from the measurements, cheapest first.** Write the red test before the cleaner: take one transcript known to contain key-shaped strings, assert the cleaner misses them, then build until it does not. Second, run the cleaner as a filter at export time rather than as a pass over the archive, because the corpus grows daily and a one-off sweep goes stale the same evening. Third, add the reasoning field to the rails table, since newly recorded sessions are the ones worth publishing and older ones have thin reasoning anyway. Platform hunting is last: it is the only part with no blocker, and doing it first would produce a list of places to upload material that is not yet safe to upload.

**Open and unmeasured, stated because it is our gap.** We checked one node out of several and one sample out of 849 files. We do not know the figure for the fleet, and we do not know whether the heaviest files are heavier in secrets or merely in tool output. Both are one script away and neither has been run.

## The remote desktop question was answered by one command, and the answer was about the path, not the client

Second post of the day asks which remote access service to adopt for a machine on another continent, notes that AnyDesk is heavily slow at high resolution, and asks for relay and direct-connection settings to be optimised. Logged because the optimisation target turned out to be measurable before any client gets installed.

**The measurement.** `tailscale status --json` from one fleet laptop: **7 peers, 5 online, 4 Macs**, client 1.98.10. **Direct: 1** (the hub, over a public address). **Relay: 6**, specifically four on the Madrid relay, one Frankfurt, one Nuremberg. `tailscale ping -c 3` to the Nuremberg VPS: **82ms** round trip. The same ping to the four Macs and the hub: no reply, so those paths are unverified rather than measured.

**Why this reframes the request.** Screen streaming over a relay means every frame leaves the sender, reaches a third-party box on another continent, and comes back out; at 4K that is the dominant cost and no codec or FPS setting recovers it. The request asks to optimise relay and direct modes, and the measurement says the ratio is currently **1:6 against direct**. Raising that ratio is a NAT and firewall problem, not a client problem, and it would make every candidate client faster at once.

**What already exists and is not being used.** Tailscale has been on the fleet since early July, with **207 file references** against **49** for AnyDesk. RDP over Tailscale was verified live on the hub on **9 July**: TermService automatic, firewall group enabled, 3389 listening, reachable over the tailnet. So a path that does not re-encode the desktop through a vendor relay has been available for months. Stated as a defect of ours rather than a win: nobody promoted it to the default, and the slow rail stayed the habit.

**Prior art inside our own notes, which changes what the research should cover.** RustDesk and Chrome Remote Desktop were both **declined on 25 July** as duplicates of a working AnyDesk-plus-Tailscale setup; both were independently recommended in the post comments, so the decline is either correct and worth restating, or it was made on thin evidence and worth reopening. An ssh-based path already has a report from **6 July**. Of the seven suggestions in the comments, **three** named a tool already installed on every machine here, **two** named tools already declined, and **two** were new (Screens 5, Supremo).

**Cheapest order that follows from the numbers.** First, count direct versus relay on every node rather than one, because the current figure comes from a single vantage point and may be worse or better elsewhere. Second, find out why `tailscale ping` gets no reply from the Macs, since an unverified path cannot be optimised. Third, measure frame latency on the rail that already exists before installing a new client, because if RDP over a direct tailnet link is adequate, the whole client comparison collapses into a settings change. Client benchmarking comes last, and by then it is a comparison on two or three candidates rather than a survey.

**Correction, logged because the first sweep missed a live instrument.** `remote_desktop_watch.py`, 7.8 KB, written **15 September 2026** on his voice request, is a finite routine whose docstring states the goal as rolling out remote access **without AnyDesk** on two laptops. Targets in its `NODES` map: `ZBOOKG8` at ports **3389 (RDP)** and **21116 (RustDesk)**, `MacBook-Anton` at **5900 (Screen Sharing)** and **21116**. Probe ceiling 8, pulse 30 minutes, and its design note says the instrument is the port measured from the hub because a local `Test-NetConnection` on the target does not count as proof.

**Its journal, `_remote_desktop_watch.json`, last written 30 September.** `MacBook-Anton`: 8 pings, **done: true**, last ping 26 September. `ZBOOKG8-2023PAL`: 8 pings, **done: false**, last ping 15 September, which means it hit the ceiling and emitted "8 pings with no result, needs Anton's hands". No scheduled task for it exists on this node, so the routine lived on the hub and is now finished on one target and stalled on the other.

**What this corrects in the section above.** Calling RustDesk "declined on 25 July as a duplicate" is accurate for July and misleading today: on 15 September it was one of two named rollout ports, so the decline silently expired. ⚠️ That is the reusable defect, and it is ours: a declined-decisions entry has no expiry field, so a decline from July reads as current in October even when the same tool came back into the plan six weeks later. The register needs a date-checked field, not better memory.

**And the measurement defect.** The earlier figures in this entry, 207 Tailscale references against 49 for AnyDesk, came from scanning `~/.claude` only. A full sweep including the vault returns larger totals and surfaced this routine, which the narrow scan did not. The narrow scan was the right thing to run under time pressure; quoting it without naming its boundary is what hid a working instrument for an hour.

**Open and unmeasured.** Latency numbers for any client: none. Per-node direct ratios: one node out of seven. Mac reachability: unknown. Whether the Madrid relay choice is geographically sane for a US-to-Europe pair: not checked, and it is the first thing to look at, because four peers sharing one relay in a third country is a suspicious default rather than an obvious one.

## The cross-model bus was already built, and its tables prove nobody used it

Third post of the day asks that every voice note raise a session in six models (Cursor, Grok at medium effort, Antigravity on the hub, Gemini, Codex, Claude Code), that n8n issue all of them one shared id in the shape `H-XXXXX-1700000000`, that each session receive a short system prompt naming that id and instructing it to collaborate, and that the `/llms` skill be enriched with every cross-model collaboration pattern already implemented. Logged because reading our own files reordered the work.

**The bus already exists.** `aibus` ships a SQLite database, `agent-protocol.md` and `aibus_bridge.py`. Work tables: `tasks`, `leases`, `events`. **All four tables, including the sequence table, hold 0 rows.** So the coordination substrate the request asks for has existed for months and has carried nothing. That is the single most useful number in this entry, because it says the missing piece was never the identifier.

**Identifiers: 208 files, 905 occurrences, all single-vendor.** The env ladder reads `CLAUDE_CODE_SESSION_ID`, `CLAUDE_SESSION_ID`, `CLAUDE_CODE_HOST_SESSION_ID`. Every one identifies one session, and every one is minted by one vendor. There is no vendor-neutral correlation key anywhere in the tree, so this part of the request is correctly identified as new work rather than configuration.

**Participants: 6 rails, and two of the named six are not among them.** Routing holds claude, codex, grok, gemini, glm, mistral. **cursor and antigravity are absent entirely.** An unaddressable participant cannot be given a shared key, so wiring those two is a prerequisite and not a detail.

**The voice lane is documented for one vendor.** `voice-sessions` is 39.5 KB and references Claude Code once; Codex, Cursor, Grok, Gemini and n8n appear zero times. Two sessions nevertheless come up per voice note, which means an undocumented path raises the second one. ⚠️ Named as our defect: a live rail with no paper is the sort of thing that breaks silently and gets blamed on the model.

**Order that follows from the measurements.** First, find out what actually raises the second session today, because it is the working half of the mechanism and nobody wrote it down. Second, determine why `aibus` is empty: no writer, or a writer that fails. Those are different repairs and the row counts cannot tell them apart. Third, make Cursor and Antigravity addressable, or decide on evidence that they cannot be driven headlessly and shrink the target from six participants to four. Fourth, issue the shared id, which at that point has something to correlate. The `/llms` enrichment belongs with step two, since the patterns it should document are the ones the empty bus was built for.

**Open and unmeasured.** Whether any model other than Claude will act on a system-prompt instruction to read a shared channel: untested, and if they ignore it the id changes nothing. Whether a per-voice fan-out to six vendors stays inside subscription limits: not computed. Whether `H-XXXXX-timestamp` collides at our volumes: trivially checkable and unchecked. All three are cheap, and all three are cheaper than the id.

## The ban on Codex for deep research turned out to ban the terminal, not the browser

Fourth post of the day proposes routing all deep research through Codex, which would drive the browser, fan questions out to other logged-in models and collect results, with Claude systematising; plus reworking the scripts and the skill around Codex as primary executor, fixing the constant browser re-logins, and running a separate study of who handles screen, mouse and desktop automation best. Logged because the proposal looked like it collided with a standing rule of ours and did not.

**The rule, in three places.** `DR_BANNED_RAILS = {"codex", "claude"}` in `dr_headless_dispatch.py`, enforced fail-closed, valve `DR_RAIL_BAN_OK=1` opening only on his word. A dated comment in `dr_runner.py` attributing it to his voice instruction of 22 September, repeated twice. And `_test_dr_runner_pregate.py` case H, which asserts a research leg through a banned rail is never offered as a candidate. ⭐ Worth noting as a win rather than an obstacle: this is what a rule with a red test looks like, and it is the reason the collision surfaced in seconds instead of being discovered after a rewrite.

**The clause that resolves it.** The comment above the constant states the reason: the Codex tank is where Anton codes and the Claude tank orchestrates, so neither should burn research runs, **and their legs are web-only**. The prohibition is therefore on the CLI rail, and the web rail is explicitly left in place. His request is for the web rail. No override is required, and the valve does not need to be touched.

**What this changes about the work order.** The scripts do not need Codex unbanned; they need the web leg promoted from "left open" to "default executor", which is a different and smaller change. The `/llms` and `/dr-fanout` documentation needs the distinction written down, because the current text reads as a blanket ban and will mislead the next person who looks, including us.

**Existing bridges: nine.** `codex_mirror.py` (20.8 KB), `codex_skills_bridge.py` (18.1 KB), `codex_token_pull.py` (12.5 KB), `codex_bridge.py` in two copies (1.7 KB and 22.4 KB), `codex_lib.py`, `codex_pair.py`, `codex_voice_router_v01`, `codex_export.cmd`. The integration surface is not thin.

**Corpus state.** 559 applied, 221 parked, 13 closed, 1 collecting; newest order 2 October, 28 days old. An idle lane is the cheapest thing to re-plumb, and also the easiest to misjudge, since nothing is currently failing to tell us what is broken.

**Open, and the first one decides the rest.** Budget: if the fan-out moves into a Codex browser, the ChatGPT subscription is still consumed, and nobody has compared web-session cost against terminal-call cost. Capability: his claim that Codex controls screen and mouse better than Claude is untimed and unlogged, so the study he asks for is the right instrument and not a formality. Re-logins: named in the request as a persistent class, and this node holds no task file describing it, so the owner of that repair is currently nobody. ⚠️ Stated plainly because it is our gap: a defect class mentioned in four separate requests over three months and still without a written owner is a process failure, not a browser failure.

## The alpha collector is built and filled; the shelf has not been opened in 55 days

Fifth post of the day asks for a pipeline to collect engineering alpha from Telegram, Hacker News, Reddit, X and GitHub into an intermediate store, have an LLM distil each item (what happened, why it matters, topic, links, useful now or later), index it into the vault and link it to existing material, plus an assessment of the strategy, an MVP that can be installed immediately, and a routine that periodically rereads the accumulation and reports what is relevant today. Logged because one filesystem timestamp reorders the whole request.

**Inventory: nine skills, about 69 KB.** `mine-channel` (4.1 KB), `watch-channel` (7.0 KB), `alpha-judge` (11.2 KB), `alpha-review` (2.8 KB), `community-alpha` (16.6 KB), `local-fb-alpha` (8.2 KB), `alpha-credit` (9.1 KB), `content-mine` (7.0 KB), `wisdom-distill` (3.2 KB). The detector-then-judge split the request describes is the architecture these were written around.

**Output that reached the vault: 26 alpha notes**, oldest 7 July, inside 760 insight files, 53 of which changed in the last 30 days. So the lane is not dead and the folder around it is active.

**The decisive measurement.** `alpha-backlog.md` in the insights folder: **40.6 KB, 160 lines, 58 entries**, with sections for parked-not-urgent and reference-as-knowledge. `mtime` = **2026-09-05**, i.e. **55 days** stale. The request's final item, a routine that rereads the pile, is therefore the only genuinely missing component, and its absence is provable from a single field rather than from an argument. ⭐ Worth naming as a method, not just a finding: a shelf with an old mtime is the cheapest possible instrument for "is anyone consuming what we collect", and it costs one `stat` call.

**The adjacent tool and its real scope.** `prior_art.py`, 42.5 KB, reads 3,689 incident lines from the breakage journal before any problem may be declared new; a live probe for "collecting alpha from Telegram channels" returned CLEAN in about a second. Correct, and precisely scoped to **our own defects**. The "system should remember this came past" behaviour he wants is a different index: external items seen and parked, not internal failures recorded. Those are two registries, and conflating them would produce confident silence on exactly the queries he cares about.

**Measurement boundary, and it is a repeat offence from earlier today.** Miner databases are not on this node; `alpha-review` states this in its own notes, warning that an empty screen is not an empty dataset and that the data lives on another drive. Every count in this entry is therefore "what reached the vault", not "what the miners collected". ⚠️ Earlier today a narrow scan of `~/.claude` was quoted in a published post as if it covered everything, and it hid a live routine for an hour. Same class, same day, second occurrence: by the rule of the third breakage, one more and this needs a mechanism rather than a note.

**Open and unmeasured.** Why the backlog went stale on 5 September: no owner, no trigger, or no value in rereading it? Unknown, and the three imply different fixes. How much the miners actually collected versus what reached the vault: unmeasurable from here, one command from the hub. And whether 58 parked entries is even worth a reviewer, or whether the correct action is to archive the file and change what gets collected: nobody has read the 58 entries to find out, which is itself the first task.
