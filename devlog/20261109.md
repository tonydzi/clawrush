## Post 1: was the co-founder against a second brain for organisations

### ⭐⭐⭐ The objection is on record, and it is not the one he remembers

The brief says the co-founder talked him out of a second brain for organisations, on the grounds that organisations do not need one. The record says something narrower.

A full research pass ran on **31 August 2026** specifically to test that objection. Its own summary, verbatim from the distilled note:

> Проверили возражение "корпорациям не нужен второй мозг" через анализ рынка, конкурентов, консалтинга и кейсов. Выяснили, что спрос есть, но термин "второй мозг" не продается

So the verdict was: **demand exists, the phrase does not sell**. That was recorded 70 days before this brief was written.

Source: `02-Decisions/_Inbox/2026-08-31--korporativnyy-rynok-vtorogo-mozga-kto-prodaet-kto-pokupaet-skolko-stoi.md`, distilled 23 September via the `distill_drip.py` mistral rail.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Three things were rejected, and one of them is exactly today's question

From the same note, the explicit "что отвергнуто" section:

| rejected | stated reason |
|---|---|
| **horizontal SMB products at $20/user/month** | competing with Notion AI and Copilot is unrealistic |
| pure knowledge bases without agents | does not solve adoption, per the Viva Topics lesson |
| consulting as the main business | 1-2x profit multiples against 10-20x ARR for SaaS |

The brief asks whether to sell the second brain to SMB organisations. That is the first row of the rejection table, by name.

The Viva Topics evidence, also from that note: Microsoft closed it in 2025 after three years of beta because fewer than 5 percent of employees used it regularly.

### ⭐⭐ Agent memory was already named a category in that pass, with money attached

The same research, under "Тренды (2025-2026)":

| company | funding | positioning |
|---|---|---|
| Mem Labs | $23M | memory for AI agents, B2B version in beta |
| Heyday | $10M | agentic memory layer, partnered with Dust |
| Twine | $8M seed | shared memory for teams of agents |

Attributed to CB Insights, 20 August 2026. And the mechanism was named too: corporations buy a second brain **through the agent door**, with Dust integrating Heyday and Glean adding an agent-memory API.

So the two examples in today's brief are not new information. They are the trend that pass identified, now more visible.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Both named examples check out smaller than they look

Live verification today, and the sources disagree enough to be worth stating.

**Mem0:** $24M Series A announced late October 2025. Valuation estimates range from $89M (Premier Alternatives, as of July 2025) to $150M (Caplight). Neither is a company disclosure. Headcount 15 to 22 as of June 2026.

**Zep AI:** total funding reported anywhere between **$500K and $2.43M** depending on the aggregator, with CB Insights at $2.3M across three rounds. Revenue around $1M by June 2024 with five employees; a modelled $2.3M annual estimate from StartupHub at 63 percent confidence. Headcount 5 to 9 as of July 2026.

Together the two companies are roughly **31 people**. That is an infrastructure bet at an early stage, not a proven subscription market. It is evidence that the category is being funded, which the August pass already said.

⚠️ Every number above comes from third-party aggregators that contradict each other, so treat them as order of magnitude. The honest version is "both are small and the category has money in it", not any single valuation.

### ⭐⭐⭐ And Zep is Graphiti, which we rejected on our own measurement

This is the part worth the whole search. Zep's engine is the open-source **Graphiti** temporal knowledge graph, confirmed in today's search and in Zep's own positioning.

Our own journal, entry dated 26 July 2026:

> Zep/Graphiti + Neo4j **отклонён** на текущих данных: наш вердикт 25.07 прямо предписал не принимать «граф везде»

And the measurement behind it: the graph helped on topic queries (**71 percent**) and **hurt on people search**.

There is a second line in the same entry worth repeating, because it is the same shape as today:

> Внешний DR попытался продать нам решение, которое наши же данные уже отклонили. Gemini настойчиво рекомендовал Zep/Graphiti на Neo4j.

So the technology behind one of the two examples was tested against our own corpus and rejected on retrieval quality, not on market demand. Those are different objections and they do not cancel each other: Zep can be a real company with real customers while its approach still underperformed on our data.

### ⭐⭐ What is NOT in the declined-decisions journal

`declined-decisions.md` has no entry for "second brain for organisations". Grep for SMB and for организаци returns nothing in that file.

So the conversation he remembers was never logged as a decision. It exists as a research pass with a verdict, which is a different artefact: a research note records what was found, a declined entry records what was decided and under what condition to revisit.

That gap is the actual defect here. The journal's own instructions say an entry goes in the same session the decision is made, and that each entry carries a `Revisit if` line. Had one existed, today's brief would have been answered by reading one line instead of by a search across four locations.

### ⭐⭐ And the B2B direction was not closed, it was confirmed by him

From memory `two-storefronts-raise-and-acquihire`, dated 24 August 2026, origin: anton:

> CRM «Сиренка» = НЕ отдельный продукт, а **B2B/корпоративная грань второго мозга** (Антон 24.08.2026): тот же движок «затащи все источники и работай по ним», но для корп-контура; личный twin = потребительская грань, Сиренка = B2B-грань

The same note records that I had narrowed the portfolio to agent-audit only and he corrected it back to three products, with the second brain as product number two.

So the direction was approved by him a week before the research pass. What the research pass rejected was a specific **channel and price point**, not the direction.

### ⚠️ What actually changed since August, stated carefully

The category got funded: $24M into Mem0 in October 2025, with Mem Labs, Heyday and Twine already carrying $8M to $23M each in the August snapshot.

But the money went into **APIs sold to developers**, not into a knowledge product sold to organisations. And the stated reason for rejecting the SMB subscription was Notion AI and Copilot, both of which are still there and both of which have shipped more since August, not less.

So on the evidence available, the rejected channel did not improve. The category around it got more credible.

### 🤔 The research pass left an open tail, and it is still open

Verbatim from its "Открытый хвост":

- test the hypothesis that agent memory is the new way to sell a corporate second brain, which needs interviews with buyers of Dust+Heyday or Glean API
- find companies that bought knowledge **before** automation, since every case found was the other way round
- pin down pricing for agent-memory products

Seventy days later, none of those interviews have happened. The recommended channel was enterprise through CIO or CDO, and mid-market through consulting boutiques. Neither has been tried either.

So the honest state is not "the objection was wrong" and not "the objection was right". It is: the objection was conditional, the conditions were written down, and nobody tested them.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. Write the missing declined-decisions entry retroactively, with the condition that reopens it, so the next version of this question costs one line instead of a search.
2. Run the three interviews the August pass asked for before building or pricing anything.
3. If the agent-memory door is the thesis, the product is an API for agents, not a workspace for employees. Those are different companies.
4. Keep the Graphiti measurement separate from the market question when quoting either one.

### ⭐⭐ "Memory and the database are on SQLite" is true for one layer out of three

The architecture has been written down since **6 June 2026**, in standing memory `vault-data-architecture`, and it is explicitly three layers:

| layer | job | cost |
|---|---|---|
| **SQLite** | facts: filters, counts, joins, dedup, lookups | 0 LLM tokens |
| **RAG** (e5 embeddings + reranker) | meaning: embed the **curated** markdown, roughly 5k high-signal docs, NOT the 1.5M raw messages | few tokens |
| **Markdown** (Obsidian) | reading and judgment, the human layer | n/a |

Rule of thumb from that same note: bulk goes to the DB, meaning goes to RAG, judgment goes to markdown.

So retrieval and embeddings are not a missing capability to be added by picking a new database. They are already a separate layer, running e5 plus a reranker on a local GPU at zero API cost. The brief lists them as things to research alongside ClickHouse and Postgres, which conflates a storage choice with a retrieval choice.

### ⭐⭐ The reason SQLite was chosen is a property, not a benchmark

From the same note, verbatim on why: *"One file, no server, indexed, instant."*

ClickHouse and Postgres both require a server. That is not a disadvantage, it is a different trade. But it means a pilot that swaps SQLite for either one is not primarily testing query speed, it is testing the cost of operating a server across a fleet where one node's Python interpreter has been broken since 2 October and nobody has repaired it.

So the metric for that pilot is not milliseconds. It is whether the thing still works on the day something breaks.
