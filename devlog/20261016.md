## Adaptive depth works, but the weakness signal is human, and nobody is logging it

The learning loop described today has three steps and only the first two are built. One: feed a source document to the model and have it emit a shallow sequence, ten lessons at a high level. Two: on request, take one of those lessons and decompose it into ten deeper ones. Three, the part the post attributes to the AI: identify which lessons the listener is weak at.

Step three is currently performed by the human. The listener notices that a section felt thin and asks for the expansion. That is a perfectly good loop and it is the reason the approach beats watching random videos, but the claim "the AI understands your weaknesses" describes a system nobody has built yet. **Adaptive depth on demand is not adaptive instruction; the difference is who supplies the signal.**

What makes this worth logging rather than just correcting: the signal is being produced and thrown away. Every time a lesson gets decomposed, that request is evidence about where the gap was. Collected across a course, the list of decomposed lessons is the weakness profile the post is talking about, and it costs one append per request to keep. Without it, the next course generated from the next book starts from zero knowledge about the same learner.

The same gap, one level up, showed in the entry from 14 October about a generated artefact that settled a decision instead of teaching: our teach-podcast lane counts episodes produced and records nothing about what happened afterwards. Two days, two different uses of one tool, and in both cases the outcome was invisible to the pipeline.

So the cheap instrument is the same shape in both: one line per episode recording what the listener did with it (prepared, dropped, asked to decompose section N). That turns the lane from a production counter into something that can answer whether customised learning actually works, which is the open claim in today's post.

Open and unmeasured: retention from generated lessons (zero knowledge), decomposition requests per course (not logged), and whether a second course for the same learner benefits from the first (unanswerable until the above exists).

## A second recording rail now writes into the same vault, and nothing dedupes the overlap

A portable recorder was assembled on an Android tablet running Omi with an external microphone, as a backup for the Plaud that already covers conferences and meetings. The audio path ends where the existing one ends: transcription, then pickup by Claude from the cloud, then a file in the vault.

That is the engineering event worth logging, and it is not the hardware. **The vault now has two independent write paths for the same class of artefact, and no key that says two files are the same meeting.** When both devices are carried to the same conference, which is exactly the scenario the backup exists for, one conversation lands twice under two names with two slightly different transcripts.

Why that is worse than wasted disk: retrieval over the vault ranks by similarity and has no notion of provenance overlap, so two transcripts of one meeting read as two sources that agree. That is the false-corroboration defect we have logged before in a different place, arriving here through a hardware decision rather than a code change. Two rails agreeing is one rail counted twice.

The cheap instrument is a dedup key, not a dedup algorithm: device id plus start timestamp plus duration, written at ingest, with an overlap check against anything already stored within the same wall-clock window. Files that collide get linked rather than merged, because the second recording is genuinely useful as a fallback when the first one has a gap. What must not survive is both of them answering the same query as if they were unrelated.

The post also states a quality bar we have never instrumented. "Switched it on and forgot" is the reason the original device keeps its place despite the backup costing three times as much, and it is a measurable property: manual steps per successful run. Our own routines have counters for invocations and for outputs, and none for interventions. So we cannot currently answer which of our lanes clear the bar the post is praising, and that is the honest reading of a post about consumer gadgets turning into a verdict about our own pipelines.

Open and unmeasured: duplicate meeting transcripts across the two rails (no key, so zero knowledge), transcript quality of the Omi rail against the Plaud rail (asserted equal by ear, never compared), and manual interventions per run for any of our routines (no counter exists).
