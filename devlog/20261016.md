## Adaptive depth works, but the weakness signal is human, and nobody is logging it

The learning loop described today has three steps and only the first two are built. One: feed a source document to the model and have it emit a shallow sequence, ten lessons at a high level. Two: on request, take one of those lessons and decompose it into ten deeper ones. Three, the part the post attributes to the AI: identify which lessons the listener is weak at.

Step three is currently performed by the human. The listener notices that a section felt thin and asks for the expansion. That is a perfectly good loop and it is the reason the approach beats watching random videos, but the claim "the AI understands your weaknesses" describes a system nobody has built yet. **Adaptive depth on demand is not adaptive instruction; the difference is who supplies the signal.**

What makes this worth logging rather than just correcting: the signal is being produced and thrown away. Every time a lesson gets decomposed, that request is evidence about where the gap was. Collected across a course, the list of decomposed lessons is the weakness profile the post is talking about, and it costs one append per request to keep. Without it, the next course generated from the next book starts from zero knowledge about the same learner.

The same gap, one level up, showed in the entry from 14 October about a generated artefact that settled a decision instead of teaching: our teach-podcast lane counts episodes produced and records nothing about what happened afterwards. Two days, two different uses of one tool, and in both cases the outcome was invisible to the pipeline.

So the cheap instrument is the same shape in both: one line per episode recording what the listener did with it (prepared, dropped, asked to decompose section N). That turns the lane from a production counter into something that can answer whether customised learning actually works, which is the open claim in today's post.

Open and unmeasured: retention from generated lessons (zero knowledge), decomposition requests per course (not logged), and whether a second course for the same learner benefits from the first (unanswerable until the above exists).
