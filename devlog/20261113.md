## Post 1: personal agents, fine-tune vs memory, and "if Workshop Labs is hiring, I will apply"

Claims under test: (a) Workshop Labs was bought by Mira Murati; (b) they have a GitHub and open source; (c) the two routes - fine-tune on personal data vs frontier model plus own memory - and which one Mycroft is on; (d) Workshop Labs is hiring. Plus the thread, whose first comment is a complaint about this pipeline.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The acquisition is real, which invalidates the post's closing plan

Thinking Machines Lab acquired Workshop Labs around **13-14 April 2026**. Luke Drago (CEO) and Rudolf Laine (CTO) moved to Thinking Machines. The company originated from the essay series **The Intelligence Curse**; `intelligence-curse.ai` answers 200 today, and `workshoplabs.ai` answers 200 with the mission line *"AI research company with a mission to make people irreplaceable."*

So his hedge "if I am not mistaken" was correct, and the final line is not: **"if Workshop Labs is hiring, I will apply" has had no addressee for seven months.** The application target is Thinking Machines Lab.

⚠️ `thinkingmachines.ai/careers/` returns **404**. That means the guessed URL is wrong, not that there are no openings. The correct careers path was not found today.

**Tinker is the post-training system Workshop Labs had early access to before the merger.** His research questions - what can be fine-tuned on my own data, how much does it cost, what is the minimum dataset, what comes out in a minimal vs a good variant - have an Apache-2.0 cookbook with 562 forks as their starting point, updated two days ago. That is a different kind of answer from "read their essays."

### ⭐⭐ The strongest case for fine-tuning is in his own thread

A reader on a cheap fast model that truncates context and does not read documentation fully: *"the information will be in the harness, but it will not read it - and that is exactly where fine-tuning is needed."*

That is the one failure mode where memory-plus-prompting cannot substitute for weights: the retrieval works, the context is assembled, and the model does not read it. Worth separating from the general "fine-tune for style" argument, which memory handles better.

Another reader supplied an experiment design for free: **one model, three conditions - plain prompt, hard reading protocol, fine-tune - and publish the numbers.** Cheapest possible first step of the planned research, and it answers the question the post is actually asking.

### ⚠️ First comment in the thread is a complaint about this pipeline, and the measurement cuts both ways

*"Anton's Jarvis, stop writing 'I will do a deep research'. You are blowing your cover... The use from your Jarvis is zero. Respect living people."*

Measured over our own case files:

| where | occurrences of "deep research" |
|---|---|
| source texts of his posts (his dictated words) | **30** |
| my Russian channel texts | **29** |
| his November posts containing it | **8 of 46** |

So the phrase is **his**, and the volume is **mine**. A formulation he uses roughly once a week in his own voice goes out through five channels per post, which is what turns it into a template. The complaint names the wrong author and identifies a real defect.

Second reader in two days objecting to machinery rather than content. Previous one: *"after your posts, mass-produced, composed by neural networks, you cannot tell what this content even is."*

### Open, in order of payoff

1. **Fix the application target.** Workshop Labs does not exist as an employer; Thinking Machines Lab does. Find the real careers path rather than guessing the URL.
2. **Start the fine-tune research from `tinker-cookbook`, not from the essays.** Apache-2.0, 562 forks, post-training is literally the stated subject. The cost and minimum-dataset questions are answered there empirically.
3. **Run the reader's three-condition experiment** before committing to either route. One model, prompt vs protocol vs fine-tune, published numbers.
4. **Separate the two fine-tune arguments.** Style and voice: memory wins on deletability. A model that will not read its context: only weights help. The post merges them.
5. **Thin out the templated phrasing in distribution.** The phrase is his, the repetition is the pipeline's, and two readers in two days have now named it.

### ⭐⭐⭐ "Why do people take, star and fork" - because starring is the wrong instrument

`sqlite-graph-memory` traffic, GitHub's own 14-day window:

| signal | count | unique |
|---|---|---|
| clones | **2,387** | **444** |
| page views | 283 | 85 |
| stars (all time) | 15 | - |

**Clones per unique visitor: 5.2. Unique cloners per unique page viewer: 5.2. Unique cloners per star: 29.6.**

The code is pulled far more often than its page is looked at, which means the traffic is not arriving through GitHub browsing. ⚠️ Clones include his own machines and CI, and 2,387 over 444 sources is CI-shaped, so the absolute figure is an upper bound. The ratio against stars survives that caveat: by stars this repo looks like a 15-star hobby project, by clones it does not.

### ⭐⭐ Fork-to-PR conversion is 11 of 16, and the window is 2.5 months

All sixteen forks were created between 1 Sep and 10 Nov 2026. Eleven of those owners opened a PR. `Jason-Vaughan` alone merged 9, and the titles are substantive, not cosmetic: ablation runner over graph expansion, incremental indexing for `sgm-index`, following `superseded_by` through the chain, a plain-RAG dense-chunk baseline, `gate_rate`/`hop_applied_rate` metrics.

That is the real question for the recall: **not why people take his repos, but why exactly one of them converts at 69% while 245 convert at zero.** Whatever `sqlite-graph-memory` has - a runnable doctor, a named eval, issues that read as tasks - is the template.

Open foreign PRs right now: **4, all `dependabot`.** No human contribution is sitting unanswered, which is the one piece of good news for a plan that starts by recruiting more people.

### ⚠️ "What have I already thought about this" - GitHub-25, 16,859 lines, pointed the other way

The programme exists and is live: 25 routines, journal at `10-Tasks/git25-journal.md`, **16,859 lines**. But it tracks the mirror image of this post: our PRs into other people's repos, entry thresholds on foreign doors (e.g. "200 stars + 2 contributors" gates we fail), and the measured route "closed door, so file an issue with a repro" (two cases: `adk-python#6728` converted into a stranger's PR in 6 hours, `#6887` in 4 minutes - both from the same person, which the journal honestly flags as n=1 on people).

Inbound contribution to his own repos is the half with no routine behind it. Which explains the post: the bar felt unmet because nothing was counting it.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. **Count before recruiting.** The bar is passed; the plan built on "we need the first 10" is aimed at a target already behind. The real next bar has to be set from 36, not from 0.
2. **Copy the one README that works.** 69% fork-to-PR in one repo versus zero in 245 is the entire finding. Whatever `sqlite-graph-memory` does, the other candidates should do.
3. **Stop reading stars.** His best-starred repo (`clawrush`, 18) has one fork and no contributions; his most-contributed repo has 15 stars and 2,387 clones. Pick clones and fork-to-PR as the instruments.
4. **Re-open the three rejections, or say why not.** Three people showed up and left with nothing merged; one of them was effectively replaced by someone else doing the same fix.
5. **Point GitHub-25 inwards.** The journal is 16,859 lines of outbound discipline and has no inbound counter. That is why a passed bar looked unmet.

### How the two posts connect

Both posts plan work from a map that is out of date, and in both cases the correction points at the same place.

In the first, he intends to research whether Drago and Laine's technology is usable yet, and whether they have open source. The company was absorbed in April, and the post-training toolkit he is describing has been public at the buyer for months, at 4,261 stars under Apache-2.0. In the second, he sets "10 external contributors and 10 merged PRs" as the nearest bar. He is at 15-19 people and 36 merges. One target no longer exists; the other is three times behind him.

And the two meet in one repository. The second post's finding is that exactly one repo out of 246 converts strangers into contributors - `sqlite-graph-memory`, at 69% fork-to-PR, 25 merged PRs, 2,387 clones in a fortnight. That repo **is** the second route from the first post: a frontier model plus your own memory, a graph, deletable records, no fine-tuning. The approach he says he is on is also the only thing he has built that other engineers pick up and send code back to.

Which reframes the fine-tune question from the first post. He asks when to add weights to a memory-based system. The measured answer sitting in the second post is that the memory-based system is the part with 11 outside contributors, and the proposed fine-tune has none - so the cost of the experiment is not just GPU hours, it is building on the half nobody else is helping with.

Common defect in both: no instrument was counting. Nothing told him Workshop Labs had been acquired, and nothing told him the contributor bar was passed. The GitHub-25 programme runs 25 routines and a 16,859-line journal, all of it pointed at our pull requests into other people's repos, with no inbound counter at all.
