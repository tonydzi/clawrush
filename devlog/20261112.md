## Post 1: NotebookLM as a teaching machine, four podcasts described, zero in the queue

Claims under test: (a) a skill builds four kinds of interview-prep podcast; (b) NotebookLM is a general-purpose learning tool, and could teach a pilot theory syllabus of "eight or nine" exams; (c) a commenter's question about how the audio is generated and what it costs in usage.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The skill exists and its rule is stricter than the post

Five skills on the shelf on this node: interview prep (16,666 b), the podcast builder (39,056 b), the interview debrief (16,628 b), the NotebookLM skill (9,296 b), the YouTube publisher (28,486 b).

The hiring rule inside interview prep is stronger than the post describes: for hiring, the podcast is made **without asking Anton first**, and one interview takes **3 to 5 episodes** rather than one, because a single call exposes several gaps and each gap earns its own episode. Asking permission is defined as a violation of the rule; delivering one episode for an unfamiliar company is defined as executing it at a quarter.

So the described capability is real and under-described rather than over-described.

### ⚠️ The exam count resolves to nine, and it is one lookup

The EASA theory syllabus for a light aircraft licence covers **nine subjects**: air law and ATC procedures, operational procedures, meteorology, human performance, principles of flight, aircraft general knowledge, navigation, flight performance and planning, communications. Examined as roughly **120 multiple-choice questions** in total across subjects.

Worth naming because of what it says about tool choice: the post wonders whether it is eight or nine while proposing to build an audio course for it. The count is a single lookup; the syllabus is what needs the course. Those are different problems and only the second one is worth a podcast.

### ⭐⭐ The commenter's question has a two-part answer, and the second part changed in September

Question: is the audio generated by the built-in generator or by separate tools, and how does it eat usage?

First part: built-in. NotebookLM synthesises the audio itself; no external text-to-speech is in the path.

Second part: there is **no official per-day figure for 2026**. Since early September 2026 the quota model is **compute-based, refreshing every five hours up to a weekly ceiling**, rather than a fixed count of audio overviews per day. The per-day numbers still circulating in blog posts predate that change and contradict each other, ranging from three to ten overviews or from fifteen to thirty minutes of audio.

Which explains a design decision already in our own queue: it **rotates between two accounts**. That rotation exists because the bucket runs out, and it is the operational answer to the question - audio comes out of the account's shared compute budget, so a long bilingual series needs more than one account to finish.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. Name the priority out loud. 37 episodes shipped on general learning, 3 interview-prep calls in 60 days. Either the interview line is the priority and the queue should show it, or it is not and the post oversells it.
2. Look up the exam count rather than building a course to discover it. It is nine, the subjects are listed above, and the course is worth building for the syllabus rather than for the number.
3. Verify the syllabus against the authority that will examine him. The nine-subject list is from secondary sources, and the UK regulator runs its own alternative syllabus.
4. Record outcomes per invocation, not just invocations. Three interview-prep calls may have produced three episodes or fifteen; the counter cannot tell, which makes "used three times" weaker evidence than it looks.
5. Watch the compute ceiling rather than a daily count. The quota model changed in September; a queue sized against per-day limits is sized against a rule that no longer exists.

Skills were read from the shelf rather than exercised - no live run was performed for this post, so the claim is "specified and installed", not "verified working". Queue numbers are a single status read on one node at one moment. Usage figures come from our own counter over 60 days and count invocations rather than episodes or outcomes. NotebookLM quota behaviour is from secondary reporting; Google publishes no number.

## Post 2: pitch the community, not the product, and his own audience already voted

Claims under test: (a) the founder's advice that an investor should be pitched a community and customers rather than a product; (b) his own proposal that for now he could at least teach everyone to use Claude Code and Codex, and grow customers out of that later; (c) his admission that raising money easily for years distorted how he valued community building.

### ⭐⭐⭐ The thing he proposes "for now" is his single best-performing post, three weeks old

He wrote on 21 October: if you need help setting up Codex and Cursor, can I be your teacher. Measured on the post pages, logged out:

| date | post | reactions | shares | people in thread | comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **21.10** | **teach everyone to set up Codex and Cursor** | **165** | **17** | 11 | 10 |
| 03.11 | be co-founders of our community | 14 | 1 | 9 | 9 |
| 11.11 | a community member is an investor | 25 | 2 | 6 | 5 |
| 12.11 | this post, community-first as a principle | **2** | 1 | **0** | **0** |

So the engagement gap between **offering a service** and **reasoning about community** is roughly **80x on reactions** and **17x on shares** within the same audience and the same three-week window.

And the replies to the teaching post are not applause, they are intake:

- "I'm in" - twice, independently;
- "I'd very much like to take part";
- "can it be set up? I'd gladly join";
- "your help would be invaluable";
- one person went to DM: "I wrote to you in PM about a marketing problem, can a swarm of AI solve that?";
- one commercial opener: "we're researching commercial application in small and medium business, starting with ourselves - I think this would be useful to you";
- and one long technical objection about how to balance what you give the swarm against what you take, and whether lending your own subscriptions to other swarm members is sound.

That is **six people stating availability, one inbound DM, one commercial lead and one serious technical reviewer**, from one post. In the publicly visible threads there is no author reply to any of them.

### ⭐⭐⭐ Which makes his conclusion right and his sequencing backwards

The post's conclusion - community management is the most important thing - is supported by his own numbers better than by the founder's advice. But the post frames it as something to start doing, and the measurement says it was started on 21 October and produced a queue of volunteers that is still unanswered on 13 November.

The customer base he says matters more than the product is partly visible already:

| surface | figure |
|---|---|
| @PaloAltoAi | **145,839 subscribers** |
| @ClawEng | **4,629 members, 282 online** |
| @PaloAltoAiRu | 3,400 subscribers |
| GitHub followers | 86 |
| starter kit | 15 stars, 5 forks, 8 open issues |
| starter kit clones vs page visitors (10 Nov measurement) | **743 unique clones against 88 humans** |

That last line is the one that bears on "pitch your customers". **743 machines cloned the second brain and 88 humans looked at the page.** If the pitch is a customer story, the clone counter is not it: a clone is an agent fetching files, not a person with a need. The number that would support his pitch - people helped, named, with a before and after - is the one we do not have, because the six volunteers were never onboarded.

### ⚠️ The self-diagnosis is the strongest line in the post and it is unverifiable here

"My perception was distorted for a long time, because I raised money easily. I always raised money when there was hype, and investors handed it out easily. And I never really thought about how important growing a community is."

No measurement is offered for that and none is available on this node - the CRM databases live on the hub. It is named here because it is the kind of claim that deserves either evidence or an explicit "no evidence", and this is the second.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. Answer the six volunteers from 21 October before writing another post about community. They said "I'm in" three weeks ago; one of them moved to DM unprompted.
2. Reply to the commercial opener separately. "We're researching commercial application in SMB" from somebody doing it on themselves is a lead, not a compliment, and it has been sitting for 23 days.
3. Answer the technical objection in public. The question about balancing what the swarm gives against what it takes, and about lending subscriptions, is the best objection anyone has raised to the swarm pitch; leaving it unanswered under the most-shared post is the expensive silence here.
4. Stop measuring reach with the clone counter. **743 clones, 88 humans** - that ratio describes agent traffic, not customers, and it cannot support a customer-story pitch.
5. Produce one named customer story. The pitch he describes needs one person, helped, with a before and after. Six people volunteered to be that person.
