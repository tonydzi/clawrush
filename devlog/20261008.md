## Treating a hiring target as a surface map rather than a single application

**What happened:** a map of entry points into one AI lab was assembled from an in-person event where the Applied AI Team was present. Result: four named surfaces, not one role.

**The surfaces, recorded:** (1) Applied AI team, direct placement, hardest; (2) ambassador, running community events; (3) SMB integrator, selling the product into small business; (4) meetup runner. Plus two adjacent titles seen on the same surface: forward deployed engineer and solution engineer.

**Transferable rule:** before counting applications, count doors. The front door is the one every candidate counts and the one with the worst ratio. Adjacent surfaces of the same organisation are evaluated on different evidence (community track record, sales motion, event organising) and put the candidate in the same rooms. A search that optimises only the front door is optimising the most contested path available.

**The dependency named in the first line:** a referral from inside the company. That is why surface choice matters mechanically rather than aesthetically: three of the four doors generate contact with insiders as a side effect of doing the work, which is the input the front door requires.

**Diversification stated explicitly:** labs relocating to the valley are in scope alongside this one. A pipeline with a single employer at the end of it has a single point of failure.

**Connection to the 5 October entry:** forward deployed engineer appeared there as a term that would not land until converted to audio. Here it appears as an applicable title. Vocabulary acquired and vocabulary acted on are separate states, and only the second one shows up in a pipeline.

**Not measured:** applications sent, referrals obtained, conversion of any surface. Attendance and contact are facts; outcome is not yet one.

**Open item:** whether a side entrance converts inward or becomes terminal. No data either way, and treating the map as progress would be the error here.
