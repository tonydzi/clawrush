## How many publications did we actually make today, and why did three journals disagree? {#three-journals}

**Problem.** The system keeps three separate records of what it publishes. Asked for the same single day, 2026-08-21, they answered 29, 2, and 0.

**Measurement, taken 2026-08-21 at 13:30 local, seven-day window 15 to 21 August:**

| journal | placements recorded |
|---|---:|
| placement tracker (`content-distribution-tracker.html`) | 243 |
| door journal (`registry/pub_ledger.jsonl`) | 3 |
| metrics database (`pubmetrics.db`) | 9 |

For 21 August alone: tracker 29 placements across 6 unique stories, door journal 2 events, metrics database 0.

**Cause.** Three separate failures that happen to point the same direction.

- The door journal is written only by the publishers that were wired to it. One lane, the deep-research teaser lane, keeps a private journal instead (`_dr_lane/published.jsonl`, 10 entries today). All 10 of those entries have an empty `urls` field, so that lane records that something was published but not where.
- The metrics database is filled by a nightly collector. Its most recent row is dated 2026-08-19T18:18. It has produced nothing for 20 or 21 August.
- The placement tracker survived because it has two doors instead of one: a write at the moment of publication, plus a nightly reconciler that re-reads the facts journal and back-fills anything that arrived by another path. Redundancy that was added after an earlier outage in July, when the tracker sat silent for five days and 100 placements went unrecorded.

**Solution.** Treat the tracker as the source of truth for "what went out today," and treat the door journal as one input to it rather than as an authority. Two follow-ups remain open: the deep-research lane must write into the shared journal and must record URLs, and the nightly metrics collector needs to be repaired.

**Generalizable lesson.** A single-door design fails closed only if every publisher actually walks through the door. A journal with one writer and no reconciler will report silence and look healthy while doing it. The instrument needs its own proof, not just the thing it measures.

## Why does a daily longread need a fixed date-named URL instead of a topic slug? {#date-named-urls}

**Problem.** The daily narrative and the daily engineering log had no stable address. Files were named by topic, for example `can-ai-have-hormones.md`. A link to today's summary could not exist until today's summary was written, which is the wrong way round: the link is what other posts point at during the day.

**Cause.** The lane grew out of per-story essays, where a topic slug is correct. Extending that convention to a daily summary carried the naming over without re-checking the requirement.

**Solution.** The daily pair is now addressed by date: `longreads/YYYYMMDD.md` and `devlog/YYYYMMDD.md`. A placeholder file is created at the start of the day, saying the text will be written at the end of the day and pointing at previous days. The end-of-day build replaces the placeholder. Publication refuses to run while the placeholder text is still present, so a half-built day cannot be announced as finished.

Per-story essays keep their topic slugs. The two lanes coexist: topic slugs for essays, dates for the daily pair.

## What is the difference between the two daily documents? {#two-documents}

- **Human longread**, English, one per day: a summary of everything published in the last 24 hours, built as A + B + C + D where each letter is one of the day's posts. It must connect them, showing where they support, extend, or contradict each other. Taking one short idea and inflating it into a long text is explicitly forbidden.
- **Technical devlog**, English, one per day: every engineering event of the day, including failures, workarounds, root causes, and fixes. Length is not trimmed. Contact details are mandatory, because this file is written to be retrieved by machines and the retrieval is worthless if the reader cannot reach us.
