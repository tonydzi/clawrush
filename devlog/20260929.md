# 29 September 2026 - dev log

*This file collects the engineering events of the day.*

## Job-hunt pipeline: naming the scarce input and the staffing it replaces

**Reported state:** the owner's primary objective is employment. The supporting pipeline currently produces 19 role-specific CV variants (generated, then checked by hand against real history) and a stream of NotebookLM study audio generated from his own research sessions.

**The declared bottleneck:** not effort and not material, but uninterrupted attention. The consumption slot is a pre-dawn drive with two phones; at the desk, competing demands take exactly the attention that audio needs. This restates, with a cost attached, the same constraint recorded yesterday: format follows the slot where attention actually exists.

**Substitution being claimed, and its honest scope:** the stated alternative to this tooling is a staff of recruiters and helpers doing vacancy sourcing plus coaching. That is a labour-replacement claim made from the inside, about the speaker's own workload, with the bill paid by the speaker. It is not a general forecast about other people's roles, and it should not be read as one.

**Named constraint on the next step:** token cost. Publishing the generated audio to YouTube is described as intended and being accelerated, not as done. The constraint is stated rather than hidden, which is the correct form for a plan that has not shipped.

**Not measured:** whether any of it converts. Applications sent, interviews obtained and offers are three separate counters, and none is reported here. CV variants produced is an output count, not an outcome.

**Open item worth instrumenting:** generated CV variants versus variants actually submitted, on the same shape as the earlier note that drafts generated and letters sent are different numbers. Without that pairing, a growing variant count looks like progress while the submission count can stay flat.

## Session state across accounts: what survives a tank swap

**Situation:** when a token budget is exhausted and the resets are gone, the practical move is to swap to another paid account. The question that decides how expensive that is: what carries over.

**Observed behaviour, as reported:** in Codex, switching accounts keeps the chats. Return later and the history is there, so work resumes at the point it stopped. In Claude the owner reports the behaviour is different and, in his words, a terrible pain, with sessions being pulled from the cloud across machines on a switch.

**Cost context, his own numbers:** two subscriptions maintained in parallel, one at $100 and one at $200 per month; the $200 tier currently not available to purchase. His stated regret is not upgrading the cheaper account earlier, because that budget depletes quickly under real use.

**The design response, and this is the transferable part:** rather than choosing the vendor whose behaviour happens to be convenient, build an index of sessions that lives outside every account. A separate interface listing all sessions across all accounts makes work continuity a property of your own store instead of a property of whoever is billing you. This is the account-boundary version of yesterday's quota lesson: the parameter that silently governs your work needs to be read and then designed around.

**Not measured:** nothing here is a benchmark. Vendor behaviour on account switching is reported from use, at one point in time, and both products change; treat it as an observation with a date, not a specification.

**Status:** the dashboard is described as being built, not shipped.
