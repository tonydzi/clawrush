## Cross-messenger lead scoring: one ordered list, per-channel caps

**Setup:** an agent has read access to chat history in Telegram and WhatsApp through MCP bridges. Facebook Messenger is not connected yet. Contacts, warmth and outreach history already live in a local CRM database; outreach sends pass through a single throttle.

**Goal:** a single ordered list of people to contact, where the order is "how close is this person to the mission", and the daily volume is decided per channel rather than per mood.

**Proposed shape, minimal form:**
1. **ingest** every channel into one store: one row per person, one row per message, channel recorded on both;
2. **score** each person against the named missions (employment, engineers collaborating in the open, investor network), using evidence already present: who replied, how often, how recently, what they do;
3. **rank** into one table, sorted by score, writable by more than one session, with a forced-rating field that carries an author and a reason;
4. **select** for a run: take from the top, filtered by channel, until that channel's daily cap is reached;
5. **send** through one throttle that counts per account and per day, no matter which routine asked.

**Two numbers, not one.** Ranking answers "who first". Throttling answers "how many messages may this person receive". Merging them into a single number produces a second message to the same warm contact on the same day, because a high rank outranks a spent cap. Keep them separate.

**Invariant:** the cap belongs to the send path, not the caller. Every routine that sends must pass through the same counter, or the cap is advisory.

**Invariant:** a forced rating is a row with an author and a reason, not an edited cell. Otherwise nobody can say later why someone is at the top.

**Ban surface, stated honestly:** a personal profile on Facebook Messenger has no official send API, so any Messenger automation is client mimicry. That makes cold DMs the most expensive item in this design: the account carrying them is usually the same account carrying the public presence. Reading for scoring and replying inside already-open threads is a materially different risk from cold outreach, and the two should not be built as one switch.

**Mode:** one historical pass first, then an incremental weekly run over new messages only. The full pass is expensive and buys the same conclusions twice if repeated.

**Open questions for readers:** which Messenger bridges survive in production; how others score relationships against a stated goal rather than by recency alone.

## Founder interviews as a structured source, not as reading

**Goal:** turn public founder interviews (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, Google, the Chinese labs) into two structured fields per source, instead of a reading list nobody revisits.

**Extraction schema, minimal form:**
1. **founder type** — technical or ideological. The two describe different things: one talks about what is hard to build, the other about why it is being built at all. Collapsing them into one bucket loses the distinction that makes the set useful.
2. **opportunities** — business openings the speaker says AI has just opened, quoted with a date and a source link.
3. **pains** — what is hard for them right now while building. Same rule: quote plus date plus link.
4. **fundraising read** — which product shapes they describe as easy or hard to raise money for.

**Why the date matters:** a lab's stated pain has a short half life, and an interview from two years ago describes a bottleneck that may already be solved. Every extracted row carries the interview date, and a row without one does not go in.

**Invariant:** each field is a quote with a source, never a paraphrase from memory. A claim without a link is a hypothesis, and it is stored as one.

**Output:** an ordered set of pains, since a pain that several labs state independently is worth more than one mentioned once. That ordering is the actual deliverable; the interviews themselves are raw material.

**Open question for readers:** which long-form founder interviews carry real operational detail rather than the same press narrative.
