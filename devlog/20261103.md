### Safety: what is established vs what is not

| area | status | key figures |
|---|---|---|
| passive safety | **established** | Model Y 2025 IIHS TSP+; Model 3 TSP; Cybertruck TSP+ (built after Apr 2025); Euro NCAP 2025 Model 3 five stars (90/93/89/87%) |
| in-service reliability (DE) | **established for cohort** | TÜV Report 2026, age 2-3 yrs: group avg **6.5%** significant defects; Model 3 **13.1%**; Model Y **17.3%** (worst in group). Named: brake discs, suspension. Mileage >50,000 km, above many peers |
| fires | **emerging** | Tesla claims 1 event per ~**140M** miles vs ~**17M** for the US fleet (~8x). Not causal: younger fleet, global vs mixed-US comparison, no individual data published (Plug In America) |
| doors / egress | **investigation, not rate** | NHTSA **PE25010**, Model Y 2021, **16** reports as of 2025-10-27 of exterior handle loss at low 12V; in 4, parents broke glass to reach a child. Three distinct mechanisms must not be merged |
| Autopilot (historic) | **established** | NTSB on 2016 Williston and 2018 Mountain View: over-trust, weak attention monitoring. Recall **23V838** (Dec 2023), **2,031,220** cars, Tesla conceded insufficient "prominence and scope". EA22002 closed Apr 2024; **RQ24009** examines 20 post-remedy crashes |
| FSD (Supervised) | **SAE Level 2, open probes** | RDW (NL) approved EU version 2026-04-10 as driver-controlled assistance after 3,000+ test hours, 1,000+ runs, 1.8M km. **PE25012**: 58 incidents, 14 crashes, 23 injuries, population **2,882,566** (red lights, wrong-way). **EA26002**: 9 crashes, 1 fatal, population **3,203,754** (degraded visibility) |

⚠️ **Denominator trap, stated explicitly in the report:** both FSD investigation populations are counted in **vehicles, not miles**. No crash rate can be computed from them, and any derived "crashes per million miles" would be fabricated.

⚠️ **Causal claim status:** Tesla's "N times safer with FSD" is **association only**. Confounds listed: driver self-selection, road composition, vehicle age, weather, non-comparable endpoints, no independent VIN-level audit, no intervention data. Reuters 2026-05-28: **10 of 11** researchers surveyed found the key comparisons problematic. Confidence in any multiplier: low.

### Robotaxi vs Waymo: same question, different disclosure

Tesla, as of 2026-10-18: ~**3.1M** autonomous miles reported (own counter displayed 3.95M), **zero** events across three selected categories (injury on first contact, own airbag deployment, pedestrians/cyclists). ⚠️ Selected categories are not total collisions: WIRED counted **17** SGO events July 2025 - March 2026, **at least 2** with remote operators driving. California: Tesla listed under testing-with-a-driver only. NHTSA opened a Cybercab self-certification audit query 2026-09-04.

Waymo, as of June 2026: **271.3M** rider-only miles; **0.67** injury crashes per million miles vs **3.77** human benchmark; **0.01** serious vs 0.21; **0.29** airbag deployments vs 1.61.

Both corporate and observational. Waymo discloses more granularity. Comparison is only valid within similar ODD and identical endpoints and denominators.

### What employee and union evidence can and cannot support

⚠️ Explicit in the report: employee testimony (anonymous Reuters data labelers, Krupski) and union conflicts (IG Metall, IF Metall) are **not evidence of vehicle defects**. They are evidence about working conditions and labelling practice. The brief proposes building the investigation on employee forums, so this boundary must be stated in the episode itself, not just held in editing.

### Litigation status: no single verdict

Tesla won the 2023 California cases. The Walter Huang settlement (2024) is not a judicial ruling. Florida, August 2025: jury awarded **$243M** over a 2019 crash; 2026-02-20 the judge declined to overturn.

### ⭐ Newer than the report: Consumer Reports 2026

Not in the 30 October report because it postdates part of it. CR 2026 brand rankings: Tesla enters the **top 10 for the first time**, **9th** on reliability with a score of **50**, survey covering ~**380,000** vehicles; only the Cybertruck scored below average. Counterweight: CR's **2025** used-car study ranked Tesla **last of 26 brands**.

Consequence for the brief: the premise ("people write that Teslas are not the most reliable") holds for the used fleet and is weakening for new cars. The answerable question is per-model and per-age, not per-brand.

### Electrification: the counterfactual, and why it cannot be a number

Verdict, high confidence: **interaction hypothesis**. Neither originator nor passenger.

Pre-Tesla independent trajectories: CARB ZEV mandate **1990**; GM EV1, **1,117** built; Prius **1997**; Li-ion commercial **1991**; Nissan Leaf battery plan drawn before Model S; BMW Megacity/i3 announced **2009**; BYD F3DM **2008**; Chinese NEV subsidies **2009-2010**.

Structural scale: by 2025 China sold **>13M** EVs (~**55%** of its new cars); Chinese OEMs ~**60%** of world EV sales; China produced ~**70%** of EVs and **>80%** of battery cells. Pack cost **$1,415/kWh** (2008) to **$139/kWh** (2023), ~**90%** decline (DOE).

Necessity test (fails for Tesla): Vietnam/VinFast **175,099** EVs in 2025; Thailand led by BYD and MG with Model Y tenth; Indonesia with SAIC and BYD **>60%** of cumulative BEV sales. US is the exception where Tesla's share exceeded half of EV sales in 2021.

Tesla's identifiable channels: premium desirability of BEVs; battery + power + own Supercharger network (6 stations Sept 2012, 380 by end 2014); early OTA/software-centric architecture; competitive pressure; Model 3 **325,000** reservations in one week, 2016; NACS adopted as a regional standard (GM, Ford, SAE J3400).

Best contemporaneous evidence of pressure: Diess (VW), August 2017. ⚠️ Confounded by Dieselgate ($2B settlement, Electrify America) and CO2 regulation. For BMW, Nissan, GM, Ford: little direct evidence Tesla caused the original electrification decision.

⚠️ Myths corrected: the patent pledge is **conditional** good faith, not a licence or public-domain release, and citation counts do not prove use (academic support: emerging only). Profitability was late: Roadster loss-making 2008; gross margin 8.5% in 2009; first fully profitable year **2020** at **$721M**; DOE loan **$465M** (2010) bridged the valley of death.

For PHEV and conventional HEV, Tesla's role is secondary (Prius 1997, F3DM 2008). The broader the definition of "electrification", the weaker the "Tesla caused it" thesis.

### Open, in order of payoff

1. Episode design that keeps the four safety questions separate. The research is done; the compression risk is editorial.
2. Explicit non-causal labelling wherever an "N times safer" figure appears, with the denominator named (vehicles, not miles).
3. Robotaxi/Waymo comparison restricted to matched ODD, endpoints and mileage denominators, or dropped.
4. A stated boundary in the episode that employee and union testimony speaks to working conditions, not vehicle defects.
5. For the electrification episode, the report's recommended package: 18 primary sources, 35-45 minutes, verdict stated plainly, with sources published before or during the decisions cited rather than retrospective praise.

## Setup brief: Screens 5 server side across the fleet

### ⛔ Retraction of a false blocker

Earlier in this brief's handling I recorded a factual risk: "the hub runs Windows; Screens has no server side for Windows, so Screens will not reach it." **That claim is false and is withdrawn.**

Verified against the vendor's own documentation:

- **Screens Connect runs on Windows** and is the server-side component for non-macOS hosts.
- It **installs a VNC server (TightVNC)** during setup; the step can be deselected if a VNC server is already present.
- It **configures the Windows firewall** as part of installation.
- Minimum Windows version is stated inconsistently across store listings (Windows 7 SP1 or later in one, Windows 10 SP1 or later in another). ⚠️ Check the current listing before relying on either.

Class of error: a negative verdict delivered without running the check. A false positive ("this will work") surfaces at first test; a false negative removes a line of work and nobody tests what was ruled out.

Consequence: installing a remote-control server on a host outside the registry puts it outside every watchdog (`output_freshness`, node-health-beat, parity board), because those iterate over the registry.

### Protocol limits (VNC), which define the use case

- **No audio.** VNC does not carry sound. So this cannot be used to monitor runs by ear.
- **Clipboard on Windows: text and URLs only.** No files, no images via clipboard.
- **Secure connection on Windows requires a separate SSH server**, not bundled with Screens Connect.

Net: an interactive remote screen for hands-on work. Not a file transport, not a monitoring channel. Does not replace the shell for a fleet whose work is sessions and logs.

### Third path to an already-closed problem

- **Tailscale + ssh**: machine-to-machine, in production, no screen.
- **Remote-desktop research** (`DR26-07-23-HUB-01-1836`, AnyDesk/RustDesk/RDP/VNC): **parked**, verdict "superseded by reality: cross-machine access is closed by Tailscale plus ssh; a remote-desktop layer is not needed."
- **New order, 2026-10-29**: remote access for the Europe-to-US case, where AnyDesk was lagging badly (that brief was distributed on 3 November as a separate post).
- **Screens**: phone-in-hand. The only one of the three that answers "nobody is at the keyboard."

So the three do not overlap: no screen / desk-bound / phone. Worth stating in the setup note so the next audit does not park Screens for the same reason the earlier DR was parked.

### Known conflict, and a dated precedent for the class

Avast interferes with Screens Connect and requires an explicit exception.

Precedent on this very node, already paid for: **ExpressVPN silently broke Tailscale** by cutting external resolvers (`1.1.1.1` and `8.8.8.8` silent) while keeping its own at `100.64.100.1`, inside Tailscale's own CGNAT range, which the daemon refuses to use. Symptom was `tailscale up` failing with "no DNS fallback candidates remain" while the internet worked. Cure: split tunnelling with **`tailscaled.exe`** excluded (the `d` matters; `tailscale.exe` is only the CLI). Wasted paths, verified as non-fixes: service restart, public DNS on the Wi-Fi adapter, `--accept-dns=false`, hosts-file entries.

Rule derived: security software that rewrites network paths is the first suspect whenever a remote connection works on the LAN and dies from outside.

### Order of work

1. Enter **Mac Studio** and **Mac Pro** into `fleet_nodes.json` before any install, so the new surface is visible to the watchdogs.
2. Install **Screens Connect** on the hub; expect TightVNC to come with it unless deselected.
3. Add firewall and antivirus exceptions deliberately (Avast exception named), not after the first failure.
4. Decide explicitly: SSH server on Windows for the secured path, or rely on Tailscale as the transport and keep Screens on the tailnet address.
5. Test **from outside the home network**. A LAN-only test cannot distinguish a working setup from a working LAN, and this is the step that the parked DR's "it already works" verdict never had.

⚠️ Source note: fleet composition and the ExpressVPN precedent come from our own registry and breakage journal, both dated. The Screens Connect capabilities come from the vendor's documentation checked live today; the minimum-Windows-version discrepancy is theirs, not ours, and is flagged rather than resolved.
