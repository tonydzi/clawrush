## Research output to audio: converting the artifact to fit the consumption slot

**Pipeline reported:** research sessions and study material are passed to NotebookLM, which generates audio lessons; several are produced per day and consumed during drives.

**The constraint that drives the design:** at the desk, with 5 machines and 9 screens, audio does not get attention. In the car, it does and nothing else can. So the scarce resource is not compute or material, it is an attention slot with a fixed shape: audio only, roughly the length of a drive, no interaction possible.

**Design consequence, and this is the transferable part:** the output format of a pipeline should be chosen by where it will be consumed, not by what the generator natively emits. A research run emits text because that is cheap for the machine. Text competes for the exact attention slot that is already saturated. Converting it costs one step and moves it into an empty slot.

**Backlog signal, stated against ourselves:** hours of generated audio remain unplayed, because production runs daily and consumption is bounded by drive time. This is the same failure as any unread report: a generator with no rate limit tied to its consumer's capacity will always outrun it. The fix is not more generation, it is either a selection step ahead of generation or an explicit queue with a cap.

**Not measured:** retention. Nothing here shows that listening produced knowledge, and the post does not claim it. "Generated" and "listened" and "learned" are three different counters, and only the first one is currently instrumented.

**Distribution note:** the setup is public on the owner's GitHub, free to run locally or reuse, and sharing is offered openly.
