## Two agents, one working folder and one skills directory

**Result reported:** Claude Code and Codex run simultaneously against the same working folder, sharing a single skills directory rather than holding a copy each.

**Why one directory and not two copies:** the moment a skill is fixed in one copy, the copies diverge, and the two agents then disagree about how the work is done. One file, mounted into both tool paths, keeps a single source of truth. A skill written for one agent should therefore be written for any agent: steps that call our own scripts or CLIs work everywhere, while steps that depend on one vendor's connector need to be labelled as such so the other agent can say the rail is not available instead of inventing a workaround.

**What is not established here, stated plainly:**
1. no contention test. Two agents writing the same file at the same time is not addressed by a shared folder; a lease or a lock is a separate mechanism, and nothing in this setup provides one;
2. no benchmark. The claim is that they coexist, not that the combination is faster or better;
3. instruction files remain per-agent even when the skills are shared, so the shared layer is the skills, not the whole context.

**Open question asked publicly:** whether other people run the two side by side in one space, and what breaks for them when they do.

## Job-search automation: three nodes, 18 resume variants, and a constraint automation cannot move

**Current shape:** a daily routine runs on three machines: find new openings, apply. The application surface is 18 resume variants, one per role type, so targeting happens by selecting a variant rather than by rewriting a document per application.

**New segment added:** hedge funds, entered on a compensation argument rather than a technical one. Stated band for this experience level is 200-300k base, with the actual interest in bonus upside. Geography is concentrated in London and New York, which makes location the live question rather than the role itself.

**Provenance of the numbers, because they are not all the same kind of claim:**
1. 3 machines and 18 variants are our own counts, verifiable on disk;
2. the 200-300k band is the owner's reading after a deep research pass, so it is an estimate;
3. the several-million-a-year figure is second-hand from a podcast and is repeated as hearsay. It is not used as a basis for anything.

**The binding constraint is not throughput.** Yesterday's entry had job boards rate-limiting the sender, and the answer was to change channel. Today's limit is different in kind: four children, and a schedule of four office days a week with flights home is rejected outright. No pipeline change addresses this. What can be negotiated is the arrangement, with a week in office alternating with a week at home named as the candidate shape.

**Consequence for the tooling:** a filter on work arrangement belongs upstream, at the point where openings enter the queue, not downstream at the interview. Applying to roles whose schedule is already known to be unacceptable spends the very rate limit that yesterday's entry showed to be scarce.

**Not verified here:** whether hedge funds at this level negotiate alternating remote weeks at all. That is the open question the post itself raises.
