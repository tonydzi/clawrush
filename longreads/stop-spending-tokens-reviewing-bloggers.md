# Stop spending tokens reviewing bloggers

We had an idea in the team: reviewing and parodying other bloggers could make great content.

But why?

We already have a ridiculous amount of our own material. Products to build. Experiments to run. Bugs to break. Things we have learned the hard way and still have not documented properly. Why spend our tokens checking whether some blogger explained something correctly, whether their demo is real, or whether their functionality actually works?

That is just stupid.

Bloggers are doing their work. We do not need to become reviewers of reviewers. We can do that when we have nothing better left to do, which is not going to happen any time soon.

There is one important exception.

If somebody's work intersects with mine, then the comparison becomes useful. Suppose a blogger talks about building a second brain. I have spent enough time building one myself. I have working systems, failures, missing pieces, and scars. In that case I can explain what the blogger got right, what they discovered that I missed, and where they simply do not understand the problem yet.

That is not commentary for commentary's sake. It is a comparison grounded in actual work.

The difference matters. A generic reaction asks, “Is this blogger right?” A useful reaction asks, “Does this claim affect something we are building, and can our evidence improve the decision?”

If the answer is no, move on.

Attention is not free. Tokens are not free. Verification is definitely not free. Every hour spent auditing somebody else's content is an hour not spent testing our own product or publishing what we already know.

So the rule is simple: do not review other bloggers merely because they are there. Review an external claim only when it collides with our product, our research, our experience, or a decision we actually need to make.

Otherwise, why?
