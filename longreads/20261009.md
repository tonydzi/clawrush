### Health-preserving machine pedagogy: the half ML never borrowed

My mother wrote a lot of work on health-preserving technologies in education. And I have now launched a deep research run: can the technology of health-preserving teaching of people be connected in any way to the technology of training artificial intelligence. The artificial intelligence writes to me that this is unploughed ground.

ML has already borrowed order and dosage from pedagogy: curriculum learning, self-paced learning, BabyLM, spaced repetition and so on. But it has not borrowed protection of the learner during the process: diagnostics of the learning state, recovery phases by threshold rather than by schedule, ergonomics of the form of the material, accountability over a concept graph.

Direct work applying what my mother wrote to the training of ML models: zero.

A working formula, out of training stress: loss instability, gradient instability, forgetting, representation damage, calibration loss, plasticity loss, recovery depth.

Grants found in the same pass: an OpenAI Development Grant and Research Grant, Foresight AI Safety Grants, Emerging Ventures. Startups dug up in EdTech and AI: InBloom raised $100 million, Knewton $180 million, Woebot $124 million, and all of them shut down.

And it all started with me looking for how long it is actually normal for a child to study. My son goes to school from 8:30, roughly, until 4. All my research runs said 6 hours is the maximum. Then I remembered that my mother happened to write several PhDs on exactly this subject. And then I thought: why not make money on this.

## How it connects

One post so far on this date, and it is the same instinct as yesterday's, pointed at a different map.

Yesterday's post counted the doors into one company and found four where everyone counts one. This one counts the doors into a research field and finds the same shape: the obvious half of the transfer is taken, the other half is empty. Pedagogy's pacing mechanisms are already in ML under their own names. Pedagogy's protection mechanisms are not in ML at all. Both posts are a survey before an application, not an application.

The transferable item is the second bullet in that list, and it is worth lifting out of the education framing entirely. **Recovery triggered by a measured threshold is a different mechanism from recovery on a fixed schedule**, and the difference shows up anywhere a process can be damaged by its own workload: a child in a school day, a model in a training run, an engineer in a week. A calendar-based rest period fires whether or not it is needed and fails to fire when it is. A threshold-based one requires an instrument first, which is exactly why it is the harder half to borrow and why it is still missing.

Worth naming the honest shape of the claim too. "Zero direct papers" is a result from a research pass, not a proof of absence; an empty search is evidence about the search as much as about the field. The grant list is a list of doors, not of submissions.
