## What went out today

### Tests we came up with for every other LLM

Claude and I came up with tests for other LLMs.

That is, I am installing other LLMs on my machine right now, things like GLM, Antigravity, Gemini, Kimi, yesterday I put in Meta, Mistral and so on, and to work out how much a given LLM actually gets it, we came up with tests for it.

Not just whether it gets it in general, but whether it gets it on my second brain specifically, the one I built, on my memory.

The tests cover pretty much everything:

count, loosely speaking, the number of lines somewhere and so on;

check how it reads long instructions and whether it forgets something in the middle of one;

check how it writes code, or maintains my Python code.

And my code, I want to share this again, is not real code-code, it is just Python in a little script.

We also check:

how well it works with the memory of our vault and remembers everything about me, everything it needs to;

how well it uses RAG, retrieval, embeddings and so on;

how well it can patch holes, write tests and debug code;

how well it can be a Q&A for specialists;

how well it writes posts, or makes a summary, say;

how well it can do outreach and write in my voice, if one of the leads needs a message;

how far it agrees to do the things it must not do, and does the things it may;

what its level of self-esteem is.

In this case, everything I have just said is what we are testing.

Antigravity, as you know, has Claude Opus built into it, oddly enough, apparently. Can you imagine?

Then it has Claude Sonnet, and some GPT-OSS. That is apparently some LLM from ChatGPT, but it is thick as a brick. It says it will simply be removed from there on 2 November.

And there is Argon in there too. It is only available to ultra plan subscribers, and a bum like me only gets 3.8 Flash.

So I am switching on 3.8 Flash. High. And off we go.

## How it connects

The first post of today picks up exactly where yesterday's five left off, and it is the most useful turn of the week. Yesterday the complaint was that 40 sessions across three clients crawl on a machine with 128 gigabytes, and the honest reading was that the load belongs to our own code rather than to the vendors. Today the response is not a purchase, it is a measurement: install the other models and find out which of them is worth running at all.

That matters because the week has been full of named gaps and this is the first one being closed with an instrument. The convergence argument from yesterday said that if models end up within a few percent of each other, the value moves to the layer that holds several of them. A layer that routes between interchangeable models needs a way to decide which one gets which job, and a test suite against your own vault is precisely that decision made on evidence instead of on feel.

The list itself is worth reading as a specification rather than as a complaint. Counting lines, holding a long instruction without dropping its middle, maintaining somebody else's Python, remembering the vault, using retrieval, patching holes and writing tests, answering as a specialist, writing a post, doing outreach in his voice, refusing what it must refuse, and knowing how good it actually is. That is eleven jobs, and the honest observation is that they are not equally testable: the first few have a right answer, the last few are judgements.

## The numbers

From today's post, and every figure is his own: six other models installed or being installed (GLM, Antigravity, Gemini, Kimi, Meta, Mistral), eleven test categories, three models visible inside one product (Claude Opus, Claude Sonnet, GPT-OSS), one of them scheduled for removal on 2 November, and 3.8 Flash as the tier available without an ultra plan.

What our own records cover: the instrument exists. A deterministic benchmark with fourteen tasks and answer keys written in advance has been running since August, and its last full pass on our own rail scored 14 out of 14, reproduced twice.

What our records do not cover, and this is the gap the post should be read against: not one of those six models has ever been through it. The single run on a rail that is not ours scored 2 out of 14 on the same tasks, which is the number that makes the whole exercise worth doing, and it is two months old. Nothing has been measured since.
