## What went out today

### The company with a queue of customers waiting

I have now been through several interviews at companies worth more than a billion, unicorns already. One of them was Lambda, which provides GPU capacity and was hiring a Solution Engineer.

What genuinely surprised me was the answer to the question I always ask: how does sales find customers? They do not. There is a queue of customers waiting to buy something from them.

Picture it. I once worked in a highly competitive market, where you look for customers, talk them into buying, and a pile of vendors fight over every client. Here it is the other way round. They choose whom to sell to, and they even try not to oversell to any one customer, try not to ship too much capacity into a single buyer.

The role follows from that: a Solution Engineer for pilots and proofs of concept. To have longer relationships with customers they need as many well-closed pilots as possible, so the customer has a clear understanding of what he needs and what Lambda can give him.

I am going to try Lambda for myself now, though they handle crypto cards poorly, being oriented toward the American market.

And I could not imagine how vast this market is: machine learning, selling graphics cards and capacity. The volume is insane. A gigantic number of companies are desperate for compute and ready to pay for it. A100 and B200 cards, if you need to train your models, that is all at Lambda.

It is not certain they will hire me, because I have no cloud experience. But I do have gigantic experience with difficult customers. Who knows. I keep looking.

## How it connects

One post so far today, and it inverts the premise of everything written here this week.

The job-search posts of the last two days were about throughput on our side: two hundred applications a day, eighteen resume variants, three machines running the routine, and job boards throttling the sender. All of that assumes the scarce thing is attention from the other side.

This post meets a company where attention is not scarce at all: demand is queued, and the constraint has moved to delivery. That changes what an applicant is selling. Against a queue, volume of outreach is worthless and evidence of closing complex work well is the whole pitch, which is also why the tactic from yesterday, arriving as a pull request instead of a form submission, points in the right direction.
