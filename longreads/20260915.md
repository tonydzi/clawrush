## What went out today

### Giving skills away, and finally counting what came back

When my Claude builds something useful and a skill falls out of it, I try to give it to the world. I have a routine for that. It goes to GitHub, finds people who hit roughly the same pain I already fixed, and hands them the skill.

The reason is not charity. I want to be noticed in the engineering world, and I want my signature on everything I give.

A skill is only a crumb of what I actually built: a second brain, coordination between several Claudes running on different machines, and the machinery around it. The skills are the part small enough to hand to a stranger. For the rest, the door is my GitHub or a direct message.

Today I asked the uncomfortable question. Does any of it work?

I checked. The starter kit that holds 101 of those skills has **6 stars and 2 forks**. The whole profile: **129 public repositories, 33 followers**.

So giving is not the result. The result is a count: stars on exactly the skills that were handed out, where they went, how relevant each one was to the thread it landed in, and whether a single person came back to say thanks.

Without that count, a routine that gives things away is indistinguishable from a routine that spams, just with better intentions.

## How it connects

Yesterday's post asked whether tomorrow's routine inherits a fix made today. Today's post asks the next question down the same line: once a routine gives its work to the world, does it ever learn whether the gift landed? Both are the same gap. A routine that acts without reading back its own consequences will keep doing the same thing forever, confidently.
