## What went out today

### Twenty years of remote work, and the office is still an aquarium

Work remotely. Honestly, somewhere from the age of 25, for the last 20 years, I have been working remotely. Even before remote work existed at all in principle, I started working remotely, back when I was an employee. I was my own boss, the manager of small regional warehouses, branches and so on.

And I can say that, honestly, it was fun, because working in a big office, in my personal opinion, is working in a big aquarium where everyone keeps getting in your way. They call you out for a smoke and so on, they call you to lunch. Very inefficient.

Basically, what always helped me was that I work remotely: you work at a large company, in some rich city, in a rich country, and you live wherever you like.

And running around San Francisco in the mornings, I was thinking that it would be nice to set all of this up the same way. Working remotely and living in your own village beats going to the office 4 days a week. Who the hell knows how I will negotiate it, but it seems I have a chance to agree either on full remote, or a week in the office and 3 weeks at home. In short, a deja vu, a certain deja vu, because I have been through this already.

To work effectively, I believe you have to work in silence, in complete silence, and better still without food. Not to mention without a smoke break. No smoking, no food, nobody, just you and the machine, the computer.

But at the same time I understood for sure that I can work effectively enough even from a phone, the main thing is to have internet.

## How it connects

Two claims sit in this post and they are not the same kind of claim. The first is a fact about a working life: twenty years of remote work starting at 25, running small regional branches before remote work had a name. That is biography, nothing in this repository measures it, and it is stated here as his own account. The second is a forecast about a negotiation that has not happened: full remote, or one week in the office against three at home. There is no offer on the table, no employer named, and no written terms anywhere in our records. It is a hope with a shape, and it is worth separating from the twenty years, because the twenty years are evidence and the hope is not.

What makes the post more than a preference is the mechanism underneath it. The complaint about the office is not about commuting or buildings, it is about interruption: a smoke break, a lunch call, someone at your desk. Each of those is cheap for the person who initiates it and expensive for the person who was mid-task, and an open-plan office is a system that makes initiating cheap. The stated cure is the strongest possible version of the opposite, silence with nothing else in it, which is the same argument this repository keeps running into from the machine side. Long work needs an uninterrupted window, and anything that can break the window will.

The honest counterweight, and he states it himself in the last line: he can work well enough from a phone if there is internet. That undercuts the maximalist version of his own rule. If a phone on a street is sufficient, then the requirement was never total silence, it was the absence of people who can interrupt. Those are different conditions, and conflating them is how a preference turns into a ritual.

## The numbers

One post on this date.

Twenty years working remotely, starting at 25, his own account and unverified here. Four days a week in the office as the alternative he is weighing. One week in the office against three at home as the compromise he thinks is reachable, stated as a chance, not an agreement.

Not measured, and it is the number that would settle the argument: how many uninterrupted hours a day he actually gets in either arrangement. Nothing on our side logs that. The claim that a remote day beats an office day is plausible, consistent with twenty years of practice, and entirely untimed.
