# 27 August 2026: Three Posts, One Elephant, and a Watchdog With No Memory

*Three posts went out today, and they turn out to be the same idea approached from three sides: work that never finishes in one heroic session, and what you have to build instead.*

## What went out today

**Mining alpha out of paid VIP groups.** I pay real money for membership in some closed investor groups on Telegram, Discord and WhatsApp. A paid group returns nothing by itself: the only value is the people you actually meet and what you learn there. So the plan is to parse every member into the CRM together with everything they have written over the whole time, split them into talkers and quiet ones, and summarise both. Context matters when you summarise: if someone wrote "yes yes", you need the question they were answering.

The hard rule attached to it: when the AI and I write replies, we use only what is in my own vault. Not the whole internet. We cannot answer what we do not know, and we are not a search engine.

**My whole YouTube watch history into the vault.** Titles, links, descriptions, transcripts where they still exist. Knowing what someone watched over the years tells you more about them than asking them directly. But this is not a task, it is a routine: a long job that keeps tripping over rate limits, bans and my own availability has to be eaten in pieces. Backfill the history first, then take a small bite every day.

**A watchdog that did not know the past.** The third post is the failure that ties the other two together, and it is worth reading as the counterweight to them.

## How they connect

The first two posts are both plans to consume something enormous: thousands of messages across paid groups, years of watch history. Neither can be done in one session, and both would quietly die if you tried.

That is why the phrasing matters: **a finite routine must know when it is done, switch itself off and report.** Otherwise it either runs forever on an empty queue, or stops silently and nobody notices for weeks. The third post is exactly the case where the second half of that sentence was missing.
