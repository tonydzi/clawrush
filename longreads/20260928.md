## What went out today

### A PhD in teaching people does not transfer to training LLMs

I have a PhD in teaching engineers. Honestly, it has been of no use to me except as a checkbox: everyone can see it is there. Though I am very wrong to put it that way, because I should thank my mother for helping me get it. She motivated me and helped in every way she could, and she has several more PhDs of her own, all connected to teaching.

The catch is what the degree is actually about. It is about teaching people, not about teaching artificial intelligence.

So, about learning. Right now I want to understand whether the way people learn can be extrapolated to the way LLMs are trained. I have ordered several research runs on this question. We will see, maybe something can be glued together.

### Cursor limits are monthly, not weekly: 28% gone in 2 days

I kept looking at it and seeing that I was using Grok on Extra High in Cursor fairly safely, and the limits kept not running out, and not running out. I was genuinely shocked.

And then it hit me: the limits here turn out to be MONTHLY. I had already burned through 28% of the monthly limit in 2 days.

Only now did I understand that the limits in Cursor are monthly, not weekly. Well, damn. Okay, no big deal. Cursor turns out to be not cheap either.

## How it connects

Two posts today, and both are about assumptions that were never checked against the thing itself.

The first sits behind the post from two days ago about turning research output into podcasts for the car. That one was about a delivery format; this one goes a level down and asks whether the underlying model of learning is even the same thing for a person and for a model. A human learner has motivation, fatigue and a drive to school; a language model has data, an objective and a gradient. The two are described with the same verb and share almost nothing mechanically. The transferable move is the discipline of not assuming transfer: holding a credential in one domain is the most comfortable possible reason to apply its methods to a neighbouring domain without checking.

The second is the same failure in miniature and with a receipt attached. A quota was paced against an assumed weekly window when the real window was monthly, and the error only became visible because the limit refused to run out. Both posts land on the same instruction: read the actual parameter before planning against it. One is about a research programme, the other about a subscription, and in both cases the assumption was comfortable and wrong in the same direction.

## The numbers

Two posts today. The first carries no measurements and claims none: the only quantities are a degree that exists and several research runs ordered and not yet returned. Nothing there asserts that human pedagogy maps onto model training; the post is the question, posed before the answer exists.

The second carries one real figure, read off the vendor's own meter: 28% of a monthly limit consumed in 2 days on Grok at Extra High reasoning inside Cursor. Extrapolating it to a full month would be dishonest, since two days of heavy use are not a month of average use, so it is left as what it is: a consumption rate that was invisible until the reset window was read correctly.
