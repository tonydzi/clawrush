## What went out today

### A PhD in teaching people does not transfer to training LLMs

I have a PhD in teaching engineers. Honestly, it has been of no use to me except as a checkbox: everyone can see it is there. Though I am very wrong to put it that way, because I should thank my mother for helping me get it. She motivated me and helped in every way she could, and she has several more PhDs of her own, all connected to teaching.

The catch is what the degree is actually about. It is about teaching people, not about teaching artificial intelligence.

So, about learning. Right now I want to understand whether the way people learn can be extrapolated to the way LLMs are trained. I have ordered several research runs on this question. We will see, maybe something can be glued together.

## How it connects

One post so far today, and it sits directly behind the one from two days ago about turning research output into podcasts for the car.

That one was about a delivery format: text is what a generator emits, audio is what a driver can absorb, so convert. This one goes a level down and asks whether the underlying model of learning is even the same thing in both cases. A human learner has motivation, fatigue and a drive to school; a language model has data, an objective and a gradient. The two are described with the same verb and share almost nothing mechanically.

The transferable move is the discipline of not assuming transfer. Holding a credential in one domain is the most comfortable possible reason to apply its methods to a neighbouring domain without checking. Naming the mismatch out loud, in public, before running the research, is what keeps the next few weeks honest.

## The numbers

No measurements in this one, and none are claimed. The only quantities are a degree that exists and several research runs that have been ordered and not yet returned. Nothing here asserts that human pedagogy maps onto model training; the whole post is the question, posed before the answer exists.
