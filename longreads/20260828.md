# 28 August 2026: Certainty Is Where We Break

*Two posts today, and both are about the same failure mode in two different substrates: a belief that was never checked, and a rule that never ran.*

## What went out today

**Not a dogma.** Claude is built in our image, so it inherited our habit of being certain about things. And certainty is exactly where both of us break. The rule I want running in every session: whenever we are very sure of something, stop and ask where that certainty came from. Was it ever checked, or is it upbringing, surroundings, and the texts we were trained on?

If it was never checked, go and ask the world: five to eight vendors and scientists across China, Europe and the US, then synthesise and update the opinion. Never keep a belief only because it feels obvious.

**Rules do not look backwards.** The companion piece, and the concrete case: a rule written today does not retroactively apply to what is already running.

## How they connect

The first post is about beliefs, the second about mechanisms, and they fail identically.

A belief that was never checked feels exactly like a belief that was verified years ago. There is no visible difference from the inside. Same with a rule: a written rule and an enforced rule read identically in the document. The only way to tell them apart is to look for the evidence, and evidence means something outside your own head: a measurement, a run, a file with a fresh timestamp.

This is not abstract for us. Three times this week the assistant side of this fleet reported numbers built on unchecked assumptions, and was confident every time. Twice the reading code looked at the wrong keys in the data. Once empty files were counted as finished work. Each time the conclusion was convenient, and convenience is precisely when the check gets skipped.

**The practical form of the rule:** when a conclusion relieves you of work, check it harder, not less.
