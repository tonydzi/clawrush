# No 20x on the Team Plan

*Anton's verdict, and the part of it we checked against the vendor's own price page today.*

The people who follow this know about my token limit problems. And they may say: dude, why not just get Claude Teams?

The catch is that team Claude has no maximum subscription. There is no 20x. The top tier there is the 100 dollar one, and past that they will upsell you tokens.

And I do not like being upsold tokens.

Minimum five users at 100 dollars a month. Fine, that part does not scare me. But the fact that everyone who runs past their limit will be buying extra tokens, that is what makes me sad.

So it is simpler to have 2-3 subscriptions at 200 dollars than one corporate plan for five half-crippled users at 500.

In short, Claude for teams is a money grab.

Anyone on the team plan, tell me: am I wrong?

## What we checked, and what we found

This post makes checkable claims about a vendor's prices, and we publish numbers only if they are true. So we went and read the price page on the day of publication.

**The core claim is correct.** The individual Max tiers, 5x and 20x, do not exist on the team plan. Team offers a standard seat and a premium seat, and the premium seat is described as 5x the standard one. There is no 20x seat to buy at any price. If your bottleneck is one person's throughput, the team plan cannot sell you the thing you actually need, no matter how many seats you add.

**One number does not match the page as it reads today.** The current page describes the team plan as being "for teams of 2 to 150", not a minimum of five. The 500 dollar figure in the post is five premium seats at 100 each, and that arithmetic is right; the floor of five is not what the page says now. We did not check what it said when the post was written, and we are not going to guess.

**And the shape of the complaint survives both corrections.** Two premium seats at 100 buy you two people at 5x. One individual account at 200 buys one person at 20x. If the work is concentrated in one operator, seats are the wrong unit, because the plan sells breadth and the problem is depth.

## The part nobody wants to hear

We ran this exact calculation on ourselves last week, and the honest conclusion was uncomfortable: **we measured 82% of our own output going to mechanics** — shell commands, editing files, reading files — while a second vendor's paid subscription sitting in the same toolbox was 4% consumed.

Which means the plan comparison above, however correct, is the second question. Buying a bigger tier, a second account or five seats all answer "how do I get more of the expensive thing". None of them answers "why is the expensive thing doing cheap work". We took the second account too, so this is not a lecture: it is what we found after taking it.

If you are about to compare plans, run the audit first. Count what your tokens *do*, not how many there are. If most of them are mechanics, no tier fixes that, it just postpones it by a week.
