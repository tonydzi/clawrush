# 18 September 2026: One List, Sorted by How Close They Are

## What went out today

### Alpha leads across all my messengers

I have Claude Code connected to WhatsApp and to Telegram. There are MCP pieces for talking through Claude in both, and there are ones for Facebook Messenger and other messengers too. Messenger is the one I want to connect properly next.

This matters for anyone doing sales or outreach, and the interesting part is not the connection itself. It is what you do with the history once you have it.

The plan is one historical pass over every conversation in WhatsApp, Telegram and Facebook Messenger, and then a regular pass on top of it. Out of that history I want the alpha leads: the people closest to reaching the mission. My missions are a job, a community of engineers who collaborate with me on GitHub, and an investor network that keeps growing.

Most of the people I deal with are men. At some point I wrongly refused to deal with women in business, and I was wrong about that. Chatting was fine, business made me uncomfortable, and there was no reason for it.

Then the mechanism. Every lead gets a score: how close this person is to the mission. One table holds them, sorted by score, and several sessions can write into it, including pushing a forced high rating onto someone. Outreach engines take people off the top of that table within each channel's own cap. In Facebook Messenger I allow myself 30 a day, so the question "which 30" stops being a decision and becomes a query.

### What lab founders see that I do not

I want to run a deep research pass over founder interviews: OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, Google, the Chinese labs.

Two kinds of founders, and the split matters: the technical founder and the ideological one. They answer different questions, and reading only one of them gives you half a picture.

From each interview I want two things. Which business openings they see that artificial intelligence has just opened, and what hurts them right now while they build it.

The reason is plain. Their pains are a job description written by the people who do the hiring, so they tell me how I might get into those labs and which of their problems I could actually solve. And their opportunities are ones I cannot see from where I stand: I am an ant, I swim low and run low, and they are at the level of labs. I am a guppy and they are whales.

The second half is fundraising. Which products are easy to raise money for right now, and which are hard, from their point of view. Put together with the pains, that is the material for deciding whether to pivot what I am building.

### Ten resumes, and a deep research pass behind each one

I am job hunting, and I built one reference resume, then stretched it into ten or twelve variants: AI lab researcher, BD at a startup, fundraising and investor relations, and so on down the list of roles I could plausibly fill.

Now I want to go one step further. For each of those ten or twelve role types, I want a deep research pass, and the question is not "what kind of person usually gets this job". It is narrower: which prior experience, specifically, is the strongest match for this exact role.

The reason this matters even for resumes already written: I only put in what I myself judged worth including. My actual practical experience is bigger than three pages, or five. Somewhere in the parts I left out there may be exactly the experience a given role is looking for, and I would not know it without checking.

So the research runs role by role. Who gets hired as DevRel at a lab, and what do they have in common. Who gets hired for BD at a lab, versus BD at an incubator. Who hedge funds take. Who crypto exchanges take. Who a stablecoin infrastructure company is most likely to hire. Out of each of those, one composite portrait: the profile of the candidate almost always chosen for that role.

Then, and only then, I hold my own background up against each portrait and see where it fits.

The next step is to give this away. Once the process works, I want to turn it into a skill, or several, that decompose job hunting into pieces anyone can pick up and run themselves.

## How it connects

Three posts today, and all three refuse the same shortcut: instead of guessing, read what already exists and turn it into an ordered list.

The first does it with my own history. Every conversation in every messenger already exists, and the job is to score each person by how close they are to the mission and let a daily cap cut the list.

The second does it with someone else's words. Founder interviews already exist, and the job is to pull two fields out of each one, opportunities and pains, and let that tell me where to aim.

The third does it with my own experience. Ten resumes already exist, one per role type, and the job is to build a composite portrait of who actually gets hired for each role, then hold my full history, not the three-page summary, against it.

None of the three starts from "who should I message", "what should I build" or "what should I put on the resume". All three start from a stated goal and sort existing material against it.
