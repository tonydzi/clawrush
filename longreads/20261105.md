### Find and compare every second-brain and agent-memory product, Zep.ai included

Anton wants a recall first and then an additional deep research on products for the "second brain", the "second memory", agent memory. He wants to find and compare products like: a second brain or second memory for individuals; shared memory between agents; corporate knowledge bases that unite the knowledge of every employee; a "second brain" for a company; individual employee memory that can merge with corporate memory; SaaS and open-source tiers; products similar to Mem0; and ZEB.AI must be in the comparison. He wants to understand who is building second brains now, how an employee's second brain merges with the company's, which solutions exist for corporations and for individuals, how agent memory is structured, and how Zep.ai differs from the alternatives. And on Zep.ai specifically: pull up any podcasts already made, if transcripts exist, and extract the knowledge from them to describe Zep.ai more precisely.

**The recall he asks for first lands on something, and it is more than a mention.** A radar of memory and PKM products exists in the vault, dated **28 July**, covering **24** artefacts. The products in it are exactly the ones in his brief, with star counts measured rather than quoted: **`mem0ai/mem0` 61,881**, "a universal memory layer for agents"; **`topoteretes/cognee` 29,484**, an open-source AI memory platform with a graph and an ingest pipeline; **`getzep/graphiti` 29,265**, a temporal knowledge graph for agents; **`supermemoryai/supermemory` 28,659**, a memory API pushed daily; **`letta-ai/letta` 23,992**, the stateful-agent platform formerly known as MemGPT.

⭐ **And `getzep/graphiti` is Zep.ai.** The GitHub organisation is literally `getzep`; Graphiti is the temporal graph engine their product is built on. So the item he marks as a must-have for the comparison is already in our own radar under its engineering name, and the radar also noticed what it is good at: a temporal knowledge graph for agents.

⭐⭐ **It goes further than reading about them. We have two comments filed in Zep's repository**, on issues `#1645` and `#1381`, one of them described in our own notes as "our threshold measurement plus the counterexample that killed it". The company he wants compared is one we have already talked to in public, in their issue tracker, about their own thresholds.

⚠️ **But the axes are wrong for what he needs now, and that is the honest gap.** The July radar compares these products by **contribution doors**: whether a pull request from outside has a chance, how old the open queue is, how many authors get merged. Mem0 is marked red because "there are no doors on our topic", Letta red for a queue whose median age is 198 days. Those are the axes of someone looking for a way in, not of someone choosing or positioning a product. His brief asks about SaaS against open source, corporate against individual, and how an employee's memory merges with the company's, and the radar answers none of that. So the recall gives him the player list and the measured sizes for free, and the research he is ordering has a narrower job than it looks: take the known 24 and grade them on the product axes.

⚠️ **The podcasts he asks to pull up do not exist.** The teaching-podcast queue holds **49** episodes, 37 done and 12 waiting, and not one of them is about Zep, Mem0 or agent memory. The queue runs with account rotation and is alive; the subject has simply never been queued. ⭐ The fix is cheap and it is the same machine: the radar note plus the two Zep issues are enough source material for one episode, which would then be available the next time this question comes round.

⭐ **One thing worth adding to the comparison that his list leaves out: us.** The second brain in this brief is a product we have already built and published. The public starter kit holds **235** skills with **14** stars and **5** forks, and the thing underneath it is a working personal memory with retrieval over a vault of a quarter of a million files. On his own axes, individual memory that merges into something larger, we are an entry in the table rather than only the buyer reading it.

⭐ **And one of these products has already changed our code.** A research order in the register notes bitemporal columns taken from Graphiti into our SQLite, alongside FTS5 and RRF, BGE-M3 with a reranker, sqlite-vec and personalised PageRank from HippoRAG, and records that it "influenced the code the same day", along with adopting the term "second memory". So the architectural idea he wants compared is one we have already borrowed from Zep, by name, in our own storage layer.

⚠️ Two notes for the record. The brief spells the product two ways, `ZEB.AI` in the list and `Zep.ai` twice in the body; the live product is Zep, with Graphiti as its open-source engine, and his text stays as he typed it. And the star counts above are from 28 July, which is **101 days** ago; they moved once already, the previous report said mem0 had about 48k and the measurement found 61,881, so any comparison starts by re-measuring rather than by quoting.

### A set of dashboards worth having, and the count of the ones we already have

Anton will do a deep research and wants to think through a good set of dashboards. His co-founder will analyse his logs and sessions, say the last hundred, and work out which dashboards are genuinely needed to see quickly what broke, what failed and what is running normally. He wants to see which dashboards already exist and how useful they are, which should be improved and which rebuilt from scratch, which should be assembled locally on individual machines and which across the whole fleet, and he wants them comfortable for him, for an ordinary person and for the AI itself. He also wants to look at what dashboards other teams doing similar things build, what is alike, what differs and what is worth borrowing. The primary research goes to Codex, on the hypothesis that it works better with a browser and gathers information; Claude will check the result and bring it to quality, and if Codex does badly Claude redoes it all.

**The inventory answers his first question immediately, and the answer is a number.** The vault holds **520** HTML dashboards. Updated within the last day: **7**. Between one day and three months old: **278**. Older than three months: **235**. Median age: **86 days**. ⚠️ And the shape of that distribution is the finding, not the total: the buckets for "within a week" and "within a month" are both **zero**. Nothing decayed gradually. The whole set stopped at once.

⚠️⚠️ **The date it stopped is the same date as everything else this week.** Through all of October, dashboards were rebuilt on exactly three days: the 1st (4 files), the **2nd (39 files)** and the 3rd (5 files). After 3 October, not one until 6 November, and the seven November files are built on a different machine, the laptop, by the session writing this. On 2 October at about 20:12 the hub lost its Python standard library and has not run a script since. So the 513 stale dashboards are not a design problem or a usefulness problem. They are the same outage, seen from a fourth angle.

⭐ **What is not broken: the shop window.** The public dashboard site answers **HTTP 200** and holds **392** published files. Links handed to anyone still open; what they show is the state of 2 October. That distinction matters for his brief, because "which are useful" and "which are reachable" have different answers right now.

⚠️⚠️ **And the one correction that changes his plan rather than confirming it: new dashboards are not HTML any more.** The standing rule on this is specific. A new dashboard is a Google Sheets workbook, built through our sheets rail, with access granted by name; HTML is not built for anything new. A request to modify an old one means migrating that one into a workbook, with the HTML left as an archive and no mass migration. The published site stays alive for what is already on it. So his three categories map cleanly but not the way the words suggest: **"improve" means migrate that dashboard to a workbook**, **"rebuild from scratch" means build it as a workbook**, and the 513 stale HTML files are an archive rather than a backlog. Secrets and anything CRM stay out of both, which is why 52 CRM panels were deliberately held back from the public site.

⭐ **The axis he names third is the one we already run daily.** "Comfortable for me, for an ordinary person and for the AI itself" is exactly the split between the human longread and the machine dev-log that goes out under every post: one file written for a person, one written for an agent to read. That pattern transfers to dashboards without inventing anything, and it is the part of his brief with the most evidence behind it.

⚠️ **On giving the primary research to Codex, two things, and both are his own decisions rather than my opinion.** First, this is the second time the hypothesis appears: it was the subject of its own post on 30 October, where it was offered as a guess to be tested; here it arrives as a decision. Second, our own research dispatcher refuses Codex for deep research fail-closed, because of his instruction of **22 September** that the Codex bucket is for his coding and the Claude bucket is for orchestration. The valve exists and can be opened deliberately, but as things stand today the pipeline will decline the order, and that is worth knowing before the order is placed rather than after.

**So the research he is ordering has a smaller job than it looks, and a clear order of operations.** The inventory is already measured, including the uncomfortable half. What genuinely needs outside input is his fourth question, what other teams build and what to borrow, because that is the only part the vault cannot answer. The first three are answered by the numbers above plus one decision: which of the seven living dashboards deserve to be workbooks, and in what order.

### Is there already a podcast about the terms I keep hearing, and what does provenance mean

Anton is checking whether he already has an episode about the terms and acronyms that come up in interviews and in current work. If not, he will make one. The example he gives is a word he heard recently: provenance. He also wants to work out which terms he has never decoded although they appear constantly in work, interviews, discussions and tasks. Podcast as usual, preferably in Russian but with the English terms and their explanations.

Three answers came back, and one of them is a file he created himself.

**No, there is no glossary episode.** The YouTube queue holds **132** rows and the curriculum **159**, and not one of them is an episode about terms or acronyms as a subject. Zero.

**But the format has already been built once, for one branch.** Lesson **H9** in the curriculum is titled "The market vocabulary in one trip" and lists its own contents: alpha/beta, long/short, gross/net, leverage, Sharpe, drawdown, factors, liquidity, execution, alternative data, overfitting. Eleven terms with a stated purpose, tied to the hedge-fund branch of his CV. Two more episodes, **V32** and **V35**, name vocabulary as the reason they were commissioned rather than as their content: one says the camp "sets the vocabulary people will use when they talk to me in interviews".

So the curriculum already treats vocabulary as load-bearing. It just never gave it an episode.

⭐⭐ **And there is a glossary. He started it on 15 July.** `00-System/_Glossary.md`, "The clan glossary: terms and abbreviations", **14 terms**: ACK, Tier-2, QQQ, the two Telegram channel numbers, bus, heartbeat, DR, CC, Cowork, ELI5, AK-47, hub, anchor. Next to it sits `_Glossary-Anton-Speech.md`, **53 rows** and 20KB of voice mishearings and his own jargon, with a published dashboard.

⚠️ Both were last touched on **14 August**. That is **87 days** without a new term, in a period where he has been going through interviews and reading unfamiliar codebases weekly.

⚠️ **One honest distinction, because it decides what to build.** The existing glossary is about *our* vocabulary: our channel numbers, our principles, our machine names. What he is asking about is *the industry's* vocabulary, the words other people use at him in an interview. Those are two different artefacts with the same shape, and the second one does not exist. So the answer is not "you already have it", it is "you have the format, the file pattern and a dormant file, and the industry half was never started".

⭐ **On provenance, since he asked what it means.** It is the origin and chain of custody of a thing: where this came from, through what hands, through what transformations. The word comes from art dealing, where a painting's provenance is the documented list of its owners, and in engineering it carries over almost unchanged: for a piece of data it is the record of what produced it and what it was derived from.

⚠️⚠️ **He has been enforcing it for months under that exact name.** The word appears in **466** files across his configuration and tooling. It appears **four times in his own standing rules**, including the name of a lint he runs on every commit, `code_provenance_lint.py`, and the name of a rule about CRM records, "crm-lead-full-provenance". His NotebookLM index describes pulling artefacts back into the vault "as Derived notes with provenance".

And the line that makes this worth writing down: the word stands in the **frontmatter of his own glossary file**, in a field called `provenance`, recording who asked for the file and where its definitions came from.

So the answer to "what does provenance mean" is: it is the thing his system already demands of every artefact, including the glossary where the word would be defined.

⭐ Same shape as the three briefs before it today, and by now that is the finding rather than a coincidence. The knowledge exists: in H9, in a file from 15 July, in 466 files of his own canon. What is missing is anything that connects a word heard in a meeting to the rule already running in the repository. And the candidate list for the episode is therefore mechanical rather than creative: sweep the canon and the notes for terms used without ever being defined, and provenance is simply the first one he happened to notice.
