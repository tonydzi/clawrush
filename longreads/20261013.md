# 13 October 2026: When Building Gets Cheap, the Room Becomes the Asset

*This file collects the posts of the day and the connections between them. More posts from this date are appended here as they go out.*

## What went out today

### I sat in rooms with AI engineers and understood every third word

I have been to various closed gatherings where AI engineers come together, the kind who look like hippies. Their salaries are 500 to 700K. Maybe even more.

I listened to what they were saying and realised that I do not understand English at all. Their English I do not understand at all.

So what I have understood now is that I need to listen very hard to various AI podcasts in English, to start properly getting it.

Because when an engineer talks about how to configure PyTorch, or about how he trains that LLM of his, I only catch every third word.

Very, very urgently needs fixing.

### Harnesses are everywhere, and one of them just raised

I was at a YC event where one project, which had raised money and pivoted several times, was presenting a harness for agents. It lets you plug a lot of different LLMs into one application.

I have seen plenty of harnesses like that in my time. Everyone here is starting to use Muse, then Grok the bot, then dots from ChatGPT, and there was some Instinct too, made by a woman I saw on GitHub with a Slavic surname.

There are enough harnesses around, and the interesting part is that a harness collects money. Very surprising. I would have thought money is hard to raise on a harness. A harness is not the kind of thing you should be funding.

And I still have the task of using the BB harness properly, to understand how good it is. Although here in the Valley I also found a lot of other fun harnesses with very, very nice UI, the kind it is a pleasure to start using faster.

### One brain across all my machines, and it found a password I lost

I am experimenting with Claude and Codex and Cursor, installed at once on many computers, mine and my colleagues'. And we all share one brain. Which is to say, we all know what the other person knows. If I started some piece of work, I can pick it up, and so can my colleagues, and that is fun.

The second brain also ended up holding my entire WhatsApp history, my whole Telegram, every Telegram message, all my mail, all my notes from my whole life, my entire browser history, everything I watched on YouTube and so on. And you have no idea how much easier life became.

If you are in ordinary ChatGPT or Claude, or even if you use Claude Code or Codex or something else, and you ask a question, then your context is, loosely speaking, only from that session. Or maybe you attached some folders and it will try to find something in those same folders. But all of that works very badly.

So I lost some password to Mail.ru. I had not been in Russia for 15 years. I wrote to them asking to let me recover it, and got complete silence back. Then I talked my problem over with my Claude, and he says: dude, do you remember you gave your employee your login and password back in 2018? And it is a working password, you somehow forgot about it. I said, no way. You found in my Telegram from 2018 that I once gave someone a password, and checked that it still works? I was ready to kiss my agent.

Being able to remember everything and, most importantly, to search it fast, that is the key. Underneath it is RAG, retrieval and embeddings, plus indexing and search. You can use the thing with colleagues or on your own. But you need the maximum subscription, around 200 bucks, and then you will appreciate how good it is.

### If anyone can build it, the scarce thing is the room

In my Facebook feed I see a lot of this: open source will take everything over and the vibe coders will vibe-code it all. There will be free open source resources, which have already taken over the Valley, and people just come in from India on Chinese LLMs.

And honestly, I think there is something to that thesis. Because even yours truly, who had not picked up a keyboard in a long time, learned to vibe-code, built himself a second brain and so on. By the way, if anyone needs it explained how to do all of this properly in Claude and actually have your own co-founder-assistant, write to me in the comments.

So the vibe coders will take it all. And you will take it too, by the way, whoever is reading this. You are also a vibe coder who will contribute to this progress.

But, however, in the Valley software still raises something. Investors give money for a harness. So it turns out a harness is a shovel, right, for a vibe coder. That is, in a gold rush you sell shovels, and so harnesses collect the money. What else collects? I need to think about that, of course. But the fact that soon you will be able to create any software at all with a single voice message, that is a dead certainty.

I am just curious. And here is the question for my Claude: where should one move?

Well, the safest thing in a situation like this is to look for a job, which is what I am doing. But my second idea is not only to look for a job, but also to do something offline. Like, what could it be, making pizza, or one of my dreams, selling kombucha in the Valley. But there is something else that is cool, everything connected with people.

Look, the most valuable thing right now is human attention, community and so on. In the Valley there are a lot of different people, Indians and Chinese and Russians, who have their own community, who organise meetups and so on. The smartest thing is to organise meetups not for engineers, imagine that, but for CFOs, COOs, CEOs and so on. I have a lot of good acquaintances who do this, for example Murray Newland. He has become a real guru at it. And he makes, I think, 20 to 30K a month there, because sponsors need events like that.

Say you are raising a Series A or Series B, you need money, angels. Well, there you go, a dinner was organised and the money was collected. Or say you sell a product for accountants, you need CFOs. So through him an evening like that was organised and you got users for your product.

So, in short, what I am saying right now is both soft skill and hard skill. If making the product is no longer the problem, selling it is still the problem. And having, owning an audience that can buy your product, or rather owning an audience, any audience that hangs on your every word, that is a good thing.

So if I have not been hired yet, I need to build a community. I would not say my posts let me gather a community, but something is still coming of it. Right now my main task is to get users to take to that harness, not-harness, that I made.

And by the way, since we are here, if you read to the end. I am thinking now that one could start making friends with women on Facebook. Because women are usually the most grateful users. The most loyal, devoted and so on. Not the guys who are here today and somewhere else tomorrow. So if you come in through teaching and teach women to use Claude, or ChatGPT, Codex, Grok and all the rest, then I think we get a large number of loyal users. And yes, a focus on women seems like it could be very useful.

## How it connects

Four posts on this date, and the first three are about knowledge that neither reading nor memory hands you. The first two are data you only get by being in the room. The third is data you only get by keeping every room you were ever in, and being able to search it. The fourth asks what happens to the value of that room once the building part is free, and lands on the same answer from the other side.

The written side of this field was never the problem. Papers, docs, release notes, repo issues, model cards: all of that is read every day without friction, and reading comprehension is where everyone assumes their language stands. **Then you sit in a room where the same material is spoken at native pace with domain slang on top, and the real number turns out to be one word in three.**

That gap is structural rather than personal. Written technical English arrives sorted: punctuation marks the clauses, the terms are spelled the way they are indexed, and you set the speed. Spoken technical English in a room of practitioners arrives with none of that, plus compression nobody writes down. "Configure PyTorch" is said as one slurred token; a model name becomes a two-syllable nickname; half the sentence is implied by context you do not yet share.

The practical consequence is about where the salary number sits. 500 to 700K is the going rate in that room, and the people earning it are not doing anything unreadable, they are saying it out loud faster than you can parse. The repair is cheap and boring: hours of spoken domain audio, not more reading.

The second post is the same room, different instrument. Sitting at a YC event and listening to a funded project pitch a harness, next to a personal memory of having seen many harnesses already, produces a judgement that no market report would hand you: **the category is crowded, the barrier is low, and yet it raises.** Muse, Grok as a bot, dots from ChatGPT, Instinct from one developer on GitHub, plus a stack of others in the Valley with better UI than ours, and a funded team pitching one more.

Both posts are reports from inside a room, and they point opposite ways about the same presence. In the first, being there revealed a deficit in himself. In the second, being there revealed a mispricing in the market, or at least a judgement that it is one. The instrument is the same and it is the only one that reads either signal: attendance.

Worth separating the two claims in the second post, because the post keeps them separate and the distinction matters. "A harness raises money" is an observed fact from an event. "A harness is not the kind of thing you should fund" is an opinion about whether that fact should hold, offered as surprise rather than analysis. Mixing those two is how a single conference talk turns into a market thesis.

The third post turns the same problem inward. A room makes a deficit audible; a corpus makes one searchable. The password story is the cleanest demonstration of why: the answer existed, it had existed since 2018, it was sitting in a Telegram thread the whole time, and for six or seven years it was functionally gone because nothing could find it. **The capability that mattered was not storage, it was retrieval over everything at once.** A folder attached to a chat session cannot do that, which is the whole complaint in the middle of the post.

Put the three together and they describe the same deficit from three sides. Reading papers daily does not teach you to parse a room, so you sit with a third of the words. Reading market reports does not tell you that a crowded low-barrier category still raises, so you need a chair at the event. And reading your own archive is not possible at human scale, so what you lived through is unavailable until an index makes it addressable. In each case the stored or published version of the knowledge exists and is useless, and what closes the gap is a different instrument: attendance for the first two, retrieval for the third.

Worth naming the shared-brain half separately, because it is a different claim from the memory half. One brain across many machines means a colleague's session can be picked up mid-task, and that is a coordination property, not a recall property. The post states it as working practice rather than as a measured result, and it is the part of the setup with the least evidence attached in the text.

The fourth post is the market version of the first three, and the chain through it is tight. If software can be produced from a single voice message, the production step stops being the moat. What stays scarce is distribution: an audience that will listen when you have something to show. And the first two posts of the day are exactly reports on how that scarce thing is acquired, by sitting in rooms where people talk, where the salary numbers get said out loud and where a funded team pitches its harness. **The shovel observation and the room observation are the same observation from two ends: when building is cheap, access to buyers is the asset.**

The shovel framing deserves a note, because it is sharper than it looks. In a gold rush you sell shovels, and the harness is a shovel for the vibe coder, which explains why a crowded low-barrier category still raises money. That is the same raise the second post of the day found surprising. Put the two together and the surprise resolves: the funding is not a bet that one harness wins on features, it is a bet on being the toll booth while everyone else digs. Whether that bet is correct is a separate question and neither post answers it.

Worth separating observation from plan again, as with the harness post. Observed: investors fund harnesses; a known acquaintance runs meetups for executives rather than engineers and earns, by his own estimate relayed here, 20 to 30K a month from sponsors. Plan, not result: build a community, get users onto the harness he built, possibly reach an audience through teaching. The plan carries one honest line about itself, that his posts do not yet gather a community, and the honesty matters because every other part of it is still a hypothesis.

## The numbers

Four posts on this date.

Stated: salaries of 500 to 700K in the rooms visited, "maybe even more"; comprehension of roughly every third word when an engineer speaks about PyTorch configuration or training a model.

Reported as heard, not verified: the salary band comes from conversation in those rooms, not from an offer letter or a compensation table. It is in the post as what was said there.

The honest figure is the comprehension one. One word in three is an estimate made from the inside of the problem, which is the only place it can be made from, and it is the number that decides what to do next.

Not measured: reading comprehension on the same material, which is almost certainly far higher, and the size of the gap between the two. Nobody tracks that delta, which is exactly why it goes unnoticed until a room makes it audible.

The second post carries a count rather than a measurement. Named harnesses: Muse, Grok as a bot, dots from ChatGPT, Instinct, the YC project presenting, the BB harness, plus "a lot of other" ones in the Valley. That is six named and an unnamed remainder, which is the whole argument about crowding, and it is a count from memory and attendance rather than from any registry.

Not verified: that the presenting project raised on the harness specifically rather than on an earlier product before the pivots, and the attribution of Instinct to a single developer, which comes from having seen a GitHub profile.

Not measured anywhere: whether any of these harnesses is actually used after being installed. Six names is a count of launches, not of retained users, and the post's own open task, learning to use the BB harness properly, is the same gap stated from the inside.

The third post carries the day's only hard numbers about the system itself. Stated: 15 years since the last time he was in Russia; the password was handed to an employee in 2018; the subscription that makes the setup worth it costs around 200 dollars; the stack underneath is RAG, retrieval and embeddings plus indexing and search. The corpora named are WhatsApp in full, all of Telegram, all mail, a lifetime of notes, the whole browser history and YouTube watch history.

Verified by the event itself: the password worked. That is the one claim in the post with a hard test behind it, because recovery either succeeds or it does not, and it succeeded.

Not measured: how often the archive is actually queried. One spectacular hit is not a hit rate, and the post is honest enough to be a story about one retrieval rather than a claim about many. The shared-brain side has no number at all attached to it, not a count of machines, not a count of handed-over tasks.

Also not measured anywhere in this file: whether any of today's three instruments is used after being set up. The harness post ends on the same open task, and the brain post ends on the same silence.

The fourth post carries one third-party number and it is relayed as an estimate: 20 to 30K a month earned from sponsored executive meetups, stated as "I think" about an acquaintance's business rather than as a figure anyone audited. Alongside it, two funding stages named as examples (Series A, Series B) and one prediction with no date attached: any software from a single voice message.

Not verified, and flagged as such: the acquaintance's monthly revenue. It is hearsay about someone else's income, offered to show that the market for such events exists, and that is the weight it should carry.

Not measured on our own side, which is the uncomfortable half: the post's stated main task is to get users onto the harness he built, and there is no counter anywhere for external users of it. Installs are not tracked, retention is not tracked, and the standing offer of a free seed to engineers has no attribution. So the task is named and the instrument for judging it does not exist.
