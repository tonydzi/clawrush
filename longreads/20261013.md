# 13 October 2026: Reading the Field Is Not Hearing the Field

## What went out today

### I sat in rooms with AI engineers and understood every third word

I have been to various closed gatherings where AI engineers come together, the kind who look like hippies. Their salaries are 500 to 700K. Maybe even more.

I listened to what they were saying and realised that I do not understand English at all. Their English I do not understand at all.

So what I have understood now is that I need to listen very hard to various AI podcasts in English, to start properly getting it.

Because when an engineer talks about how to configure PyTorch, or about how he trains that LLM of his, I only catch every third word.

Very, very urgently needs fixing.

## How it connects

One post so far on this date, and it names a gap that most people in this position never measure.

The written side of this field was never the problem. Papers, docs, release notes, repo issues, model cards: all of that is read every day without friction, and reading comprehension is where everyone assumes their language stands. **Then you sit in a room where the same material is spoken at native pace with domain slang on top, and the real number turns out to be one word in three.**

That gap is structural rather than personal. Written technical English arrives sorted: punctuation marks the clauses, the terms are spelled the way they are indexed, and you set the speed. Spoken technical English in a room of practitioners arrives with none of that, plus compression nobody writes down. "Configure PyTorch" is said as one slurred token; a model name becomes a two-syllable nickname; half the sentence is implied by context you do not yet share.

The practical consequence is about where the salary number sits. 500 to 700K is the going rate in that room, and the people earning it are not doing anything unreadable, they are saying it out loud faster than you can parse. The repair is cheap and boring: hours of spoken domain audio, not more reading.

## The numbers

One post so far on this date.

Stated: salaries of 500 to 700K in the rooms visited, "maybe even more"; comprehension of roughly every third word when an engineer speaks about PyTorch configuration or training a model.

Reported as heard, not verified: the salary band comes from conversation in those rooms, not from an offer letter or a compensation table. It is in the post as what was said there.

The honest figure is the comprehension one. One word in three is an estimate made from the inside of the problem, which is the only place it can be made from, and it is the number that decides what to do next.

Not measured: reading comprehension on the same material, which is almost certainly far higher, and the size of the gap between the two. Nobody tracks that delta, which is exactly why it goes unnoticed until a room makes it audible.
