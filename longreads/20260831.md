# 31 August 2026: A Routine That Stops Without a Sound

*A daily lane ran for two days, stopped, and stayed silent for eight more. Nothing alerted, because nothing was watching the right thing.*

Routine failures are starting to irritate me more and more.

I set up a lane that posts about my deep-research reports. Simple as it gets: collect the reports, publish them. It ran, and I stopped thinking about it.

Today I found out it had run for two days and stopped on 23 August. Eight days of nothing published, and I did not know. Not because I missed an alert - there was no alert. **A routine that failed to do its work goes quiet in exactly the same way as a routine that had nothing to do today.**

## Why nothing fired

We do have the instrument for this. It watches the *age of the output* and reports every morning: this job's last result is 2.9 days old, that one's is 18 days old. It works, it is loud, it names the file and the fix.

This lane was simply never registered in it.

So the failure is not "we have no monitoring." It is worse and more ordinary: **the monitor exists, and adding each new routine to it is a manual step that a human forgets.** Ten days later the lane is dead and the dashboard is green, because the dashboard was never asked about this lane.

## The shape of the fix

Not "be more careful when creating routines." That is a wish, not a mechanism.

1. **A routine is not born until its watchdog is born.** Registration in the freshness monitor is part of creating the job, not a follow-up task. If the registration step is skipped, the job does not get created.
2. **What is watched is the age of the output at the consumer**, not whether the scheduler fired. A job can start, return exit code 0, and produce nothing - we have measured exactly that on other lanes ("green tap, but no work landed").
3. **Silence needs a default meaning.** If the last publication is older than one day, that is an alarm, not an absence of news.

Today the lane was moved to another machine and given a journal it must write to on every run. The journal is the thing the watchdog will read, so the next stop announces itself within a day instead of within eight.

## The honest part

The instrument was there. The rule was there. What was missing is that neither of them is *forced* at the moment a new routine appears - and I am the step that forgets. Every routine I add without a watchdog is a small future outage with a delayed detonation, and I have a lot of routines.
