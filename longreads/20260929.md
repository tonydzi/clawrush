# 29 September 2026: The Scarce Thing Is Undistracted Hours

*This file collects the posts of the day and the connections between them.*

## What went out today

### Getting up at four: 19 CV variants and a drive full of podcasts

What motivation is there at all to get up at four in the morning? To achieve something.

On Sunday I got up at four, got behind the wheel and took two phones with me. I put on NotebookLM with all those podcasts my Claude generated for me, and off I go, listening to podcasts to understand more about how to find a job.

Right now my single biggest motivation is to find a job, and to find it faster I need to load as much information into my brain as possible. I have already made 19 kinds of my own CV. More precisely, Claude made them for me. Separate respect to Claude. Without Claude Code I would have needed a whole staff of recruiters or helpers who would feed me a certain number of vacancies and coach me on top of that.

So the motivation to get up at four is the wish to find a job. Claude helps me listen behind the wheel to podcasts it generated, so that I can teach myself. I am planning to put all of this on YouTube. I am saving tokens, but right now I am planning to do it sooner.

### Switching accounts in Codex keeps the chats, so I built a dashboard

Say your tokens in Codex have run out, and all those token resets have run out too, and you simply buy a new account. The convenient part is that when you switch accounts in Codex, all your chats are kept: switch, come back later, carry on.

My own mistake, with the numbers on it: I always had two accounts, one on the $100 subscription and one on the $200 one. I should have moved the $100 account up straight away instead of being greedy, because honestly that tank runs out very fast. And right now I cannot buy the x20 subscription at $200 at all, only the $100 one.

In Claude it does not work that way, and in short it is a terrible pain. So I am building my own dashboard with my own sessions, a separate interface where I see all my sessions across all my accounts, so I can carry them on regardless of which tank is paying.

## How it connects

Two posts today, and both are about a resource running out under you while the work is still going.

Yesterday established the parts separately: audio as the format that fits the only free attention slot, a checklist per role answered by a synthetic reference candidate, and a credential that does not transfer. This one states what all of that is being spent on and what it costs. The scarce input is named plainly and it is not effort: it is hours with nothing competing for them. Four in the morning and a drive are where those hours exist, so that is where the material goes.

The second half is the substitution being made explicit. Nineteen role-specific CVs, an ongoing stream of generated study audio and a coaching loop are, in the ordinary version of this, a staffed operation. Here they are one person plus tooling. That is a claim about labour replacement stated from the inside, by someone paying the token bill himself, which is a different thing from predicting it about other people's jobs.

The second post is that same bill hitting the other side. Tanks empty, accounts get swapped, and the question becomes what survives the swap. The vendor-specific detail is that in Codex the chats do; the general lesson is the one being built into a tool: work continuity should not be a property of an account. Note the shape, because it repeats: the fix is not to buy the bigger plan, it is to make the state independent of which plan is paying, so the next time a tank runs out nothing has to be reconstructed by hand. Yesterday's Cursor post found a quota whose reset window had never been read; this one finds the same class one level up, at the account boundary.

## The numbers

Two posts today. From the first: 19 CV variants produced, a 4 a.m. start, two phones in the car. From the second: two parallel subscriptions at $100 and $200 a month, with the $200 tier currently not purchasable at all. All of them are the owner's own counts, and the subscription prices are what he is paying, not a market survey.
