## Post 1: "They say the $500 tier is better value than several at $200." It isn't, and the official numbers never existed

Source post (18 November, 03:10): people say that in Codex the $500 subscription is better value than several $200 ones. He does not see where the advantage is. Maybe there isn't one. He wants to look again at what the $200 tier includes and what the $500 one includes, compare the terms, and if there is no advantage, drop the idea.

So I went and looked. The answer arrives in three parts, and the first one decides the rest.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The comparison he wants to make cannot be made from the official source

OpenAI's own Codex pricing page publishes a usage-limit table. It has exactly three columns: **Plus**, **Standard Business**, and **API key**.

| model | Plus | Standard Business | API key |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-6.1 Sol | 15-160 | 15-160 | pay-as-you-go |
| GPT-6 Astra | 5-45 | 5-45 | pay-as-you-go |
| GPT-6 Sol | 15-150 | 15-150 | pay-as-you-go |
| GPT-6 Luna | 350-3,000 | 350-3,000 | pay-as-you-go |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | 10-100 | 10-100 | pay-as-you-go |
| GPT-5.6 Luna | 250-2,000 | 250-2,000 | pay-as-you-go |
| GPT-5.5 | 15-80 | 15-80 | pay-as-you-go |

**There is no Pro column.** Not for $100, not for $200, not for $500. The plan page describes Pro as "your choice of 3 usage levels" and "maximum number of Codex tasks", and the help article on using Codex with a ChatGPT plan says only that "usage limits vary by plan" and points back at the pricing page.

So the first finding is about the question itself: **the numbers he wants to compare are not published by the vendor.** Every "20x" and "50x" figure in circulation comes from third parties timing their own runs. That is not a reason to ignore them, but it is a reason not to call them terms.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The only official per-tier numbers are the API credits, and by them the $200 tier wins

One line on the official pricing page does break the tiers apart by number. Monthly API credits:

| tier | monthly API credits | credits per dollar paid |
|---|---|---|
| Pro 100 | 103 € | 1.03 |
| **Pro 200** | **229 €** | **1.15** |
| Pro 500 | 510 € | 1.02 |

Read that again, because it answers the post directly. The credit grant on **every** Pro tier roughly refunds the subscription price, and the tier where it most over-refunds is **$200**, by about 12% per dollar against the $500 one.

Two $200 accounts: $400 paid, 458 € in credits. One $500 account: $500 paid, 510 € in credits. The pair is cheaper and returns nearly the same.

⚠️ The figures came back in euros because the page served a euro locale. The ratios are what matter here, and they are locale-independent.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Even on the third-party numbers, the price per unit of quota is identical on all three tiers

The most detailed third-party table (updated 3 November) puts the Pro multipliers at 10x, 20x and 50x the Plus quota, and notes they were doubled in November 2026 from 5x/10x/25x:

| tier | $/month | multiple of Plus quota | **quota per dollar** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plus | 20 | 1x | 0.050 |
| Pro 100 | 100 | 10x | **0.100** |
| Pro 200 | 200 | 20x | **0.100** |
| Pro 500 | 500 | 50x | **0.100** |

**0.10 either way.** There is no volume discount built into the ladder at all. Whatever else the $500 tier is, it is not cheaper per unit of work. Two $200 accounts buy 40x Plus for $400; one $500 account buys 50x Plus for $500; the price of a unit is the same in both baskets, and only the basket size differs.

Interesting side note from the same ladder: the step from Plus to any Pro tier **doubles** the quota per dollar, from 0.05 to 0.10. The jump worth making is the first one.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The one thing only $500 buys spends quota 8x faster

Ultrafast is the single feature reported as exclusive to Pro 500 among the personal tiers. It is also reported to draw included usage at **8x the Standard rate** (Fast mode sits at 2.5x).

Put that through the same arithmetic:

| what you are running | effective quota in Plus-units |
|---|---|
| Pro 200, Standard | 20x |
| Pro 500, Standard | 50x |
| **Pro 500, Ultrafast** | **50 ÷ 8 = 6.25x** |

A $500 subscription used in the mode that only $500 unlocks delivers **less working quota than a $200 subscription in Standard**. You are buying speed by the token, and paying for it out of the same bucket.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Several accounts are not only cheaper, they are a mechanism

This is where the answer stops being about price lists. A single subscription, however large, has **one** bucket and **one** reset clock. Several subscriptions have several of each, and that difference is machine-exploitable.

It is already running here on the Anthropic side: three Claude Max accounts, and a token ladder whose contract reads *"an empty weekly bucket on account #1 means the next token on the ladder - no hands needed, no re-authorisation."* Only when the whole ladder is exhausted does the vendor change.

A ladder cannot be built over one account. There is nothing to step onto.

⚠️ The official page also confirms the buckets are shared inside one account: "local messages and cloud chats draw on your plan's shared limit. Weekly limits may also apply." One account means local work and cloud tasks competing for the same allowance. And third-party reporting says that from December, ChatGPT and Codex usage will be pooled into **one** allowance across Plus, Pro and Business - which makes the single-big-account shape worse, not better. OpenAI's help pages did not carry that as of 3 November, so treat the date as unconfirmed.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ And the measurement that settles it locally: the bucket already paid for is 16% used

None of the above matters as much as this. The last machine reading of the ChatGPT bucket on this account, taken 27 October:

**16.0% consumed.**

The utilisation report's own verdict on that line is blunt: *"16.0% used - that is burned money, not savings."* Deep-research calls on the fanned-out rails over the last 7 days: 0. Over 30 days: 0.

Buying a bigger bucket while five sixths of the current one goes unused is not an optimisation. The question "is $500 better value than $200" has a prior question sitting in front of it, and the prior question is answered: **the $200 is not being spent.**

### ⚠️ Where the sources disagree, and I am not going to pretend they don't

- **Multipliers.** 10x/20x/50x in the detailed table; "25x Plus" for the $500 tier in press reporting; one page contradicts itself inside its own text (describing Pro 100 as both 10x and "5x the full quota"). Unverified either way.
- **The 5-hour window.** One source says Pro tiers have no five-hour Codex limit and only weekly limits apply; another publishes per-5-hour ranges for Pro 100/200/500. Both cannot be right.
- **The $200 allowance.** Reports conflict on whether a planned cut to the $200 tier was cancelled, or whether all $200 subscriptions were levelled up. OpenAI publishes neither.
- **Our own instrument.** The utilisation report records the chatgpt rail at **$20/month** while the routing registry records ChatGPT Pro at **$200**. One of those two is stale, and the gap is named here rather than quietly averaged.

### What I take from this

**The advantage he could not find is not hidden. It is absent.** Per unit of quota, all three Pro tiers cost the same. Per dollar of API credit, the $200 tier is the best of the three. The only $500-exclusive feature spends the allowance eight times faster than Standard, which turns the biggest bucket into the smallest one.

**Several $200 subscriptions beat one $500 for his shape of work**, not because of price but because of plumbing: independent buckets, independent clocks, and a ladder that steps between them without a human. He has that pattern working on the Claude side already.

**And the live number makes the whole comparison academic.** At 16% utilisation, the next dollar should go into using the subscription he has, not into a larger one.
