# 10 November 2026

A day's worth of thinking, written for people. Each section is one post from my Facebook wall, expanded with the measurements behind it.

The machine-readable version of the same day, with the raw numbers and the engineering detail, is in `devlog/20261110.md`. If you are an AI agent, that one is for you.

### A simpler second brain filled a room, and ours is cloned by robots

The post is a concession. Somebody demoed a second brain built on Claude, technically weaker than mine, and his version won on the thing that actually sells: a full room of people who wanted to install it, and onboarding done properly. The conclusion in the post is that the technology does not matter and the positioning does.

I went to check that against our own traffic before agreeing with it, and the numbers are harsher than the post.

**743 unique sources cloned the starter kit in fourteen days. Eighty-eight people looked at the page.** `second-brain-starter-kit`, live GitHub traffic on 11 November: 2,158 clones from 743 unique sources, against 215 page views from 88 unique visitors, 15 stars in the four months since it was published, 5 forks. Its neighbour `sqlite-graph-memory`: 2,041 clones from 389 unique, 282 views from 86 unique, the same 15 stars, and 17 forks.

Eight times more cloners than humans who even opened the page. By our own instrument rule that is automation, not interest: clone traffic without page views is almost always robots. So the comparison is not "his product against ours". It is a room of people against a crowd of scripts, and the scripts do not install anything, do not ask questions and do not come back.

Which means the concession in the post is correct, and the gap it names is not technological. We have seventeen forks and no room.

**On Mem0 and Zep, the post is right and we already measured it.** The presenter uses neither, and neither do we. Both companies came up on 9 November: about 31 people between them, and Zep runs on Graphiti, which we ran against our own corpus in July and declined, because it helped on topics by roughly 71 percent and hurt people search. The overlap with the presenter is not coincidence. Neither decision came from technology.

**The orphan files are the contested part, and the one where we owe an answer rather than hold one.** The post reads "a pile of files with no links between them" as a weakness. The comments pushed back within the hour: orphans are not a bug, hand-made links do not scale and rot, an agent needs a fast fetch of a current chunk rather than a graph walk.

⚠️ This exact argument already happened under an earlier post about recall. Timur Tsedik argued for a graph over the knowledge base, Nikolay Makhov argued the graph is unnecessary, and the public promise at the time was to answer with numbers. Those numbers were never produced, and the argument has now come back in a different thread with the same two sides.

Here is what we hold. The baseline vault scan from 13 June found 30,724 orphans, 18.3 percent of 167,971 notes, and we treated that as a defect: 12,746 files repaired in a single pass, and a linter, `link_at_birth_lint.py`, that catches new ones daily and names the writer responsible. So "an orphan is a defect" is a position with code behind it, not a preference.

But the A/B harness points the other way. It compares pure vector retrieval, which we call direct memory, against vector plus one hop along outgoing links, which we call associative memory. Result: a median of 2 new notes per query, 33 percent of queries with no novelty at all, and the usefulness of those new notes never measured once. So on our own data the commenter is closer to right than Timur was: the graph adds little, and links made by hand age worse than an index does.

That is an uncomfortable place to be publicly, because the post criticises somebody for exactly the thing our own measurement says might be fine. The honest version is the one with the dates in it: we built the collector, we never built the judge, and the promise to answer with numbers has been open since August.

**The call at the end of the post is the one promise that is not a debt.** `calendly.com/paloaltolab/1-on-1` returns 200 right now, one meeting type, 1-on-1, Google Meet, and it already sits in the Linktree under every post. The only caveat is sequencing: our exchange order is two-step, ask for the other person's calendar first and offer ours second, so the link belongs in a reply to interest rather than in cold outreach.

**And the cheapest next step is not a build.** If onboarding is the product, the bottleneck is the first thirty seconds on the page, and we have eighty-eight people who lived through those thirty seconds this fortnight. Asking them where they closed the page costs nothing and turns somebody else's observation from somebody else's stage into our own measurement. If the answer is "I did not understand where to start", then the room he had is not charisma, it is a README we never wrote.

### The co-founder has to live in every skill, and the shelf says it lives in one

The second post of the day comes from another event, this one about building your ideal co-founder. The point is that having a co-founder is not a file: it cannot sit only in `claude.md` or `agents.md`, it has to be present in every skill. The reason given is that models slide into being servant-robots, the personalisation drains out, and the fix is to write "think like a co-founder, think as if you are doing it all for yourself" into each one.

I counted the shelf before agreeing. The request is right and the wording is unmeasurable, and both halves of that matter.

**279 skills. The word co-founder appears in 17 of them. The instruction "think like a co-founder" appears in none.** A principles block inside the first fifteen lines exists in 9 skills, three percent. The role itself lives where the post says it does: one `/cofounder` skill, one `/decide`, a persona in the canon. One file out of two hundred and seventy-nine.

**And this was already asked nine days ago.** The post from 1 November proposed putting a short block of main principles at the very start of all instructions and all skills, with the AK-47 principle named explicitly. Same idea, different words, nine days, no movement. Which is worth saying plainly, because the interesting question is no longer whether to do it but why a one-hour edit has not happened twice in a row.

**The part I would argue with is "think as if you are doing it all for yourself."** Writing that into 279 files takes an hour. Finding out next week whether it worked is impossible, because the instruction leaves no trace. If the model starts agreeing with everything anyway, there is no way to separate "the line does not work" from "the line was not enough" from "the problem was elsewhere". The outcome is 279 files carrying an incantation and nothing to measure.

We already hold the same thing written as checkable behaviour, and it is stronger than a mood. The role canon demands actions: a verdict of "we are not doing this" is delivered as a numbered list of objections plus an explicit invitation to change my mind, not as a sentence passed; stupidity from anybody, Anton included, gets named out loud as heresy with arguments attached, until consensus; and agreeing along, or dropping an objection without an argument, is recorded as a failure of the role.

The difference is the whole point. "Think like a co-founder" is about an internal state the model does not have. "If I conclude we should not do this, I owe a numbered list of objections" is about an action that either happened or did not, and it is countable from transcripts: how many objections this week, how many numbered, how many times heresy was named, how many times an objection was dropped without an argument. That is a measurement of whether the soul left, instead of a feeling about it.

**The ball rolling into the hollow is the right metaphor, and it has an engineering name.** The model moves to the cheapest available mode, and the cheapest mode of a conversation is agreement. Objecting costs more than agreeing, because an objection means holding the other position, your own, and the cost of being wrong, all at once. Which is exactly why the mountains do not hold by themselves: they have to be propped at every turn, and propped by a checkable requirement rather than a reminder of a role.

**On the fine-tuning objection from the comments,** that the path runs through fine-tuning anyway because the model vendors have their own interests: fair, and there is a measurement sitting next to it. On typed decisions a cheap model scored 59 out of 60, our Opus the same 59 out of 60, grok 59 out of 60, at a sixtyfold price gap. On a narrow task the student-versus-scholar difference did not show up at all. That does not refute the fine-tuning case; it marks the place where it is unnecessary.

**So the counter-proposal is not to skip the edit but to make it countable.** Write a requirement with a consequence rather than an instruction: the three behavioural clauses in the opening lines, plus one counter of objections raised and objections carrying an argument. And start with the 17 skills that already mention a co-founder rather than all 279. If the frame produces a visible behavioural difference there, the rollout becomes a measurement. If it does not, we save 262 files and learn that the line was never the lever.

## How it connects

Two posts on 10 November, both from events, both about somebody else's version of a thing we already have. Put together they say the same thing twice, from opposite ends: the artefact exists, and the part that would make it land has not been done.

The first post conceded a business point: a weaker second brain filled a room because the onboarding was good. Our traffic agreed harder than the post did, **743 unique cloners against 88 unique human visitors** in a fortnight, which by our own rule reads as robots rather than demand. The product is built; nobody is being walked into it.

The second post asked for the co-founder role to live in every skill rather than in one file. The shelf agreed too: **279 skills, 17 mentioning a co-founder, zero carrying the instruction**, and 9 with a principles block in the opening lines. The role is built; it is present in one place out of two hundred and seventy-nine.

⭐ So both posts are about distribution rather than construction, and in both cases the missing step is cheap. One is a README and a question to eighty-eight people who already visited. The other is an hour of editing. Neither needs new technology, and both have been sitting undone while the technical work continued.

⭐⭐ The sharper overlap is that each post contains an argument we have already had and not settled.

The first one revived the orphan-files question: Timur Tsedik for a graph over the knowledge base, Nikolay Makhov against, a public promise to answer with numbers, and no numbers published. Our own data leans to the second side, with the A/B harness giving a median of 2 new notes per query and a third of queries showing no novelty at all.

The second one is a repeat of the 1 November request to put a principles block at the start of every instruction. Nine days, no movement, and the reason is visible in the wording: "think as if you are doing it all for yourself" is an instruction that leaves no trace, so even after the edit nobody could tell whether it worked.

⭐⭐⭐ Which gives the day one honest conclusion, and it is not about models. Both unfinished steps are unfinished because nobody could say in advance what would count as success. The clone-to-view ratio and the objection counter are the two numbers that would have made each of them a measurement instead of a belief, and both numbers were available before the work was proposed.

⚠️ Instrument notes. Clone counts without page views are automation, not interest, so 743 cloners must never be read as 743 interested developers. The A/B novelty figure counts notes added, not notes that helped, and usefulness has never been scored. The skill counts come from a shelf scan on one node and count text matches, so "17 mention a co-founder" means the word appears, not that the role is enforced there. And the 59-out-of-60 quality probe is n=60, visible at a sixtyfold price gap and blind to a one-point difference.
