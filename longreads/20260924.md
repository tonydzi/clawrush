### Eighteen resumes, three machines, and hedge funds

What other adventures do I have in the job hunt?

Remember I built myself a script. Right now three computers run a routine: every day, look for new openings and apply to them. I now have 18 versions of my resume for different types of work.

Among the recent additions, I started looking at hedge funds. For roughly my experience the salary there looks like 200-300k. I watched a podcast with a guy who had just joined WhatsApp, and he said none of his friends who went into hedge funds regretted it, because with bonuses you can make several million a year. The numbers hooked me, so I ran a deep research on hedge fund openings. Base of 200-300 thousand, and then the bonuses, possibly. So I will try that route as well.

Hedge funds are mostly London and New York, barely any in San Francisco. But I think remote is always possible. And if it comes to it, you can live across two countries, a week there and a week here. I am curious how to negotiate that with a future employer: even on a four-day schedule, flying home for three days is not great, so maybe the deal is a week in the office, a week at home.

Because there are four children after all. Four children make themselves felt: I am not ready to sacrifice family that much to start earning even 300 thousand a year.

## How it connects

Two posts today, and together they are the two halves of the same machine: what it runs on and what it is for.

The first lands on yesterday's open question. Yesterday the observation was that two agents sitting on the same folder still behave like different collaborators, and the folder being shared was a puzzle rather than an achievement. Today the shared folder is the achievement: one skills directory, both agents reading it, running at the same time. The two readings do not contradict each other. Shared context is an infrastructure problem and it is now solved; what each agent does with that context is a separate question and remains open.

The second says what all that infrastructure is actually pointed at, and it is the first post in this run where the binding constraint is not technical at all. Three machines apply daily against 18 role-specific resumes, and a new branch of the search opens toward hedge funds on the strength of the bonus numbers. Then the post names a limit no amount of automation moves: four children, and no four-day office schedule with flights home. That is worth reading next to yesterday's PR tactic, where the job boards were the wall. Here the wall is the shape of the offer, and the negotiation, not the pipeline, is what has to change.
