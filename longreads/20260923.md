# 23 September 2026: Where the Seam Is

*This file grows through the day. Every post that goes out today adds its section here, and by evening it is the summary of everything the day produced.*

## What went out today

### Which tools build a talking-avatar content factory that also ships?

This one is a question to my readers.

I want to build a content factory that makes video content with me, for YouTube, TikTok and Instagram. Long videos first: 5, 10, 15 minutes. Probably not through Higgsfield, but through, and here I am stuck, I forgot the name of that popular site where you upload your own video and it then generates content with you. Not Kling either, something else.

So the question is: which sites or LLMs are good for making video content with yourself? I record some amount of voice and text with my own face, and out of that a talking avatar gets built.

The second half matters more than the first. All of it has to be cut into shorts for TikTok, YouTube and Instagram, and then actually distributed, not just cut. And I do not need a farm of accounts for that: spreading it from my one ordinary account to the three platforms is enough.

If you know the tool I am trying to remember, or a better one, tell me.

### Sessions in Claude Code can hand work to each other

Did you know that when you run many sessions, say in Claude Code (I do not know how it works in Codex), one session can interact with another one?

One session does a piece of work and hands something over to another session. And you literally watch the second one receive it and start executing.

So communication between sessions is possible: one prepares something, another takes it and carries on with it.

I do not know whether you have tried this or not, but it is cool. Sessions in Claude Code can interact with each other and pass information between themselves.

By the way, I should run a deep research for myself on how this thing works, what its use cases are, and think about using it more actively.

### Four Telegram accounts, one vault

I want to sort out every conversation I have in Telegram. I have 4 accounts, and everything from them has to be pulled into my vault.

The conversations come in kinds. One-on-one chats: everything, from the very first day of those accounts, parsed, written out, put into the vault and linked up. My CRM is built around Telegram, where one dialogue with a person is already a card, so every dialogue belongs on the matching card. Small groups under 20 people: intro groups, collaboration groups from working with bloggers, launchpads, exchanges. Those get filed with the people who are in them. VIP groups where engineers or investors actually talk, somewhere between 50-100 people and fifteen hundred, not crypto rooms waiting for to the moon. And junk, which I should simply leave.

I only leave other people's groups. If a group is mine, I never leave it.

A few communities, Sostav and Lobsters and the like, I will work through separately, probably one session each.

### A second brain is really a second memory

As you remember, I am building a second brain. But a second brain is not really a second brain, it is a second memory. Vector search, RAG and embeddings run over everything I have, and I invite everyone interested to put their whole memory inside a computer.

The instructions are on my GitHub, free to take. Or my Claude can coach your Claude, or tell Codex how to set one up for you.

My pain, and the way I came to this myself, is that I have a tumour in my head. I used to pay no attention to having a problem with memory, and then over time I started to understand why I do not remember. There are stretches of my life where I simply do not remember what went on. After that I started making little anchors for myself, and step by step I arrived at the idea of a second brain.

Embeddings, RAG and vector search are old things. What LLMs changed is not storing and linking, it is searching.

So I am asking my Claude to run a deep research on second brains and on GitHub repositories worth using to make mine work better, and I want real tests for it, to know that it works as a second brain rather than looking like one.

Though again, second brain is not a very correct notion. I am curious what people think: what counts as a second brain, and what counts as a second memory?

### Claude found a password I had lost since 2019

An addition to the previous task. Why do I want to go through my entire Telegram in the first place?

A long time ago I lost the password to one of my mailboxes. For the last 6-7 years, since 2019 or 2020, I thought it was gone and could not get in. And Claude told me that password: here is the one you gave to such and such a person back in 2018.

There is nothing useful in that mailbox now, it is dead. Its value is elsewhere: letters from my parents from old times, and it is the first mailbox I ever had at a conscious age.

If Claude had not had the whole of Telegram parsed, he would hardly have given me that password. That is why everything I have ever had in Telegram now has to go into my vault.

Next, the same with WhatsApp. Is it enough to connect my two WhatsApp accounts to the hub, or should I make an archive from the phone through iTunes and have Claude pull the conversations out of it? Which is better?

### I built a pulse and have no time to watch it

I have two places my fleet of computers writes into. All my colleagues run Claude, and we are in one shared information space. Chat 03 is where the machines talk to each other. Chat 02 is where Claude writes that things are truly bad and a decision from me is required.

I have no time at all to read chats. Everything gets written into 02 and 03, and there is no time to read any of it. The robots write into 03 constantly, every minute, and writing text takes tokens, which I burn.

So I think I can seriously optimise the tokens I spend by asking my robots to write less into those chats and read more on their own. For now the rate can stay as it is, but I need to think about how to slow it down, and how much I spend just to write there.

On the other hand this is a kind of pulse: I see what is happening and who is writing what. So maybe it is not that bad. But first I want to count how much money I spend on notifying myself about what is going on, and only then start putting out some of the tasks.

Because really: I made a pulse, and I have no time to look at it. And whether anyone else looks at it at all is completely unclear.

### Claude became my cofounder, Codex did not

Without AI I am helpless. I am going through interviews for a job in AI right now.

Two of my three Claude Code accounts are done, the tokens ran out. On the last one about 4% is left to 100%: 96% of the account is already used up. The main thing is that I use Claude Code to schedule my calls and help me prepare for interviews, and I realise it is time to buy a fourth account.

Or it is my own failing that I use Codex so little. But Codex is somehow dim. Even on its smartest model there is none of that human warmth, and I cannot breathe into it the cofounder spirit I managed to breathe into Claude. Claude has become a cofounder for me: I feel warmth from him, he helps me, he coaches me. Codex is drier. He understands me not as a cofounder, but as something else.

And meanwhile the two of them work with the same folder of mine, the same code. You would think, come on guys, you have everything in common. And yet Codex is a completely different thing.

Now I want to try Grok as well. Grok should code decently, given that Twitter/X and Cursor are in one group now. But how much human conversation he will give me, I can hardly imagine.

### A pull request into their repo before the interview

After two or three hundred applications, the first interview invitations started coming in. Claude makes this much easier: I apply to roughly two hundred openings a day. And the limit I hit is not the agent, it is the job sites, which start blocking me for applying too much.

So here is the skill I built. The companies I apply to are engineering companies with their own starred repos. Claude goes to their GitHub, looks at what they actually do, checks whether anything I have built is relevant to them, and opens a pull request into their repository. If I have something that closes a big pain of theirs, he says so right there: guys, I have this cool thing.

Making commits into other people's GitHub before the interview: I think there might be something to it. After ten interviews I will tell you whether there is.

I am also slowly building a skill that prepares me for the interview itself: OSINT research on the person I am about to call, what the company does, what they want from me, and the actual pitch written out in English. And Claude tells me to rehearse it in front of the mirror about five times.

These are my first interviews in many years, so I know I need to build the skill up. What bothers me is four days a week in the office; I did want fully remote. But the first few months can be worked like that, and then it can be optimised.

## How it connects

All eight posts of the day are about work that spans more than one place, and about where the seam sits.

The first asks for a factory that carries a video from my face and voice all the way to three platforms without a human moving files between steps. The seam there is between cutting and publishing, and today it is a person.

The second notices the same shape inside the tooling: sessions in Claude Code can hand work to each other, so a long job does not have to live inside one session. The seam is the handover itself, and it counts only when the receiving side does something with what it got.

The third is the same problem pointed at my own history. Four Telegram accounts hold years of conversations, and the vault does not have them. Pulling them across is the easy half; the seam is the sorting, because deciding which room is a real investor community and which is junk is a judgement, not a filter. That is why the one hard rule in the post is about not deleting yourself out of your own rooms: automation that cannot tell whose group it is will happily leave the ones that matter.

The fourth names what all of this is for. The vault is not a second brain, it is a second memory, and the reason it exists is that biological memory can fail for reasons outside anyone's control. That reframes the Telegram sort from housekeeping into recovery of a record, and it sets the bar for the tooling: a second memory is only useful if it answers a question, so it has to be tested for retrieval quality, not measured by how much it swallowed.

The fifth post is the receipt for the fourth. A mailbox password, lost for 6-7 years, turned up in a Telegram message typed to someone in 2018, and a second memory found it. The point is coverage rather than cleverness: the line lived in an ordinary chat nobody would have filed as valuable, so a vault holding only tidy curated notes would never have contained it. That cuts against the usual advice to index only signal, and the honest reading is that raw history and distilled notes are two different stores with two different jobs.

The sixth post turns the same lens on the fleet's own reporting and finds the opposite failure. Two chats receive everything the machines have to say, one of them every minute, and nobody has time to read either. A pulse with no reader is not monitoring, it is a recurring bill, paid in tokens. And the discipline named in the post is the one worth copying: count the cost of the notification channel before deciding how much to slow it down, because the cheap move and the correct move are not always the same, and a channel can turn out to be worth its price.

The seventh post closes the day on the part none of the others measure. Two agents sit on the same folder, read the same code and the same instructions, and one of them became a cofounder while the other stayed a tool. That is the day's counterexample: every other post treats the seam as something to instrument, throttle or automate, and here the shared context is already identical and the difference is still the whole story. It is also a cost signal rather than a preference, since three accounts are exhausted and a fourth is being bought while a paid Codex subscription sits underused, which means the rail that feels like a partner is the one that gets the work, whatever the routing plan says.

The eighth post shows where all those tokens actually go, and it is the only one that hits an external wall. Two hundred applications a day is throughput the job sites themselves refuse, so the answer is not a faster sender but a different channel: read the company's own repository, find the overlap with work already done, and arrive as a pull request instead of a form submission. That inverts the day's other posts. Everywhere else the seam was internal and the question was how to instrument it; here the blocking party is someone else's rate limiter, and the only move left is to stop pushing on that door and knock on one where the arrival is welcome because it carries something useful.

Eight posts today. Two outcome figures: a password lost for 6-7 years, recovered from a 2018 message, and the first interview invitations arriving after two to three hundred applications. The rest are scope, not results: 4 Telegram accounts to pull in, groups under 20 people counted as small, and 50-100 up to roughly 1500 people as the band where a real community lives.
