## What went out today

### A skill for making skills, with one algorithm behind all of them

Anton will run a deep research into how other teams and people set up skill creation. He wants a separate skill for making skills, with the methodology inside it: how to design them properly. Skills should be ergonomic, high quality, maximally clear and understandable, without unnecessary complexity and without entropy growth. Understandable enough that anybody should be able to use them, even a very unprepared person. He wants to work out which types of skills are better to create, by what logic to design them, and how broad or how detailed the rules and descriptions should be. The outcome he wants is one clear algorithm by which all his skills get created.

The recall answered half of this with three existing skills, and the other half with a measurement that explains exactly what he is feeling.

⭐⭐ **Three skills for making skills already exist on the shelf.** `skillify` at **46KB**, last touched 22 September, the largest of the three. `skill-gap` at **12KB**, 2 October, which finds the hole where a skill is missing. `skill-forge` at **5.9KB**, 2 October, the smith itself. Plus a `skill-creator` shipped as a plugin alongside them.

So "I want a separate skill for making skills" describes a shelf that already holds three of them, built across two months, none of which has absorbed the others.

**The shelf itself, measured.** 282 directories, **277** with a `SKILL.md`, **3,683KB** in total. That is 3.6 megabytes of instructions for one operator.

⭐⭐⭐ **And here is the number that names what he is actually asking about.** The median skill body is **9,315 bytes**. The largest is `dr-fanout` at **179,104 bytes**. The smallest is `screenpipe-worklog` at **671 bytes**.

A spread of **267 to one**, with no stated rule anywhere about how big a skill should be.

That is "entropy growth" with a figure attached. His brief asks how broad or detailed the rules should be, and the honest answer is that today the question has no answer at all: the top five are `dr-fanout` 174KB, `apply` 125KB, `tt` 122KB, `retro` 94KB, `rep-reply` 52KB, while the median is nine kilobytes and the floor is under one.

⚠️ **But the discipline he wants does exist for one half, and it holds at 96 percent.** There is a standing rule that a skill description is at most 400 characters on a single quoted line, and there is a live instrument enforcing it, `skill_desc_audit.py` at 12KB.

Measured against all 277: **zero** skills have no description, and **13** exceed 400 characters, which is **4 percent**. The worst are `local-to-call` at 1,281 characters, `local-brain-onboard` at 1,133 and `local-fb-messenger` at 1,003, and every one of the 13 is a local skill rather than a fleet one.

⭐ So the shape of the gap is precise rather than general. Where a rule and a door exist, compliance is 96 percent. Where no rule exists, the spread is 267-fold. The methodology he is asking for is needed for exactly one dimension, the body, and it is needed because nobody ever wrote a sentence about it.

**Two more instruments are already standing beside the shelf.** `skill_usage_log.py` at 13KB, which counts whether a skill is actually used rather than merely called, and `codex_skills_bridge.py` at 18KB, which mounts the same file for a different agent so no second copy is made.

Which means the "one clear algorithm" has three of its four parts built: a creator, a gap-finder, a description auditor, a usage counter and a bridge. What it lacks is the one page saying how large a skill may be, what it must contain, and when it should be split instead of grown.

⚠️ **And one thing to settle before commissioning research into how other people do it.** A 3.6MB shelf of 277 skills is itself the standing context tax on every session. The question "which types of skills are better to create" has a local answer available before any external comparison: run the usage counter and see which of the 277 were used at all in the last thirty days. That is the cheapest possible input to the methodology, and it needs no research order.

## How it connects

One post so far on 6 November, and it continues a pattern that ran five times yesterday without exception.

Every brief on 5 November asked for something that already existed in part: a competitor radar from July, a dashboard inventory in the file tree, twenty content skills, a glossary from 15 July, and a definition of Antigravity he had published himself ten days earlier. Four of the five had something quietly dormant behind them, and the fifth had nothing broken at all.

This brief is the same shape again and sharper, because here the existing thing is three skills whose entire purpose is to be the thing he is asking for. `skillify`, `skill-forge` and `skill-gap` were built in September and October, and none of them answered the question he is asking in November, which is the one dimension none of them covers: size.

⭐ So the finding is now a pattern with a mechanism rather than a coincidence. What repeatedly gets built is the tool. What repeatedly does not get written is the sentence that says how the tool should be used and how big its output may be. Three skills for making skills and a 267-fold spread in their own sizes is that asymmetry stated in one line.
