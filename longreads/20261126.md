### I want to use my CRM every day again: small sends of 20-50, warmest leads first. Almost all of it was built this autumn, and the daily queue picked three people the day before I said it

The note, posted at 16:01: I need to think about how to use my CRM more again. It is silly that it exists but is not used every day; it holds thousands, even tens of thousands of contacts, and they sit idle. I want to launch at least some outreach right away, not thousands a day but 20, 30, 50. First I will sort the leads and review the filters. The first send goes to the leads I was warmest with, in Russian or English: I am deep into AI now, I vibe-code a lot, I live in San Francisco, I go to AI events and help AI projects find angels, and I want to know what they are doing now. My outreach always starts with "how are you, what are you working on"; the call comes second, and only where it makes sense. I need a recall of how I used to revive leads, and a network of routines on my machines doing it.

#### What is already there

| what he asks for | what exists |
|---|---|
| "the warmest leads" | a warmth ranking since 10 September over personal dialogs in four Telegram accounts, about 16.8 million messages, with mass mailings subtracted |
| a daily routine | an outreach queue since 5 September: 3 personal messages a day to important leads, with a call offer; last run 25 November picked three people |
| 20, 30, 50 a day | the sending engine defaults to 3-5 a day per account; repeat nudges up to 50 on Telegram, 20 on WhatsApp, 50 by email; real sending needs an explicit confirmation |

#### What was measured

On 2-4 September, 16 touches without a specific reason got 0 replies. On 8 September, 10 touches that opened with a long introduction got 0 replies. Since then every lead goes out only with a hook: a concrete, dated action of theirs.

So the recall he asks for is mostly assembled. The step back to daily work is a restart, not a rebuild: the people at the top of the ranking, one real hook each.

⚠️ The contact count was not recounted, because the main database lives on the main computer and the copy on this laptop is empty. Whether the 25 November messages went out is not verified from here.

### I want to test my fleet-wide sessions dashboard and recall how many times I built one. The catalog exists since June, it was built at least five times, and the copy I can see stopped updating in October

The note, posted at 16:30: I need to do a recall. I think I built a dashboard across many computers of my fleet, where all sessions are visible. Now I should check it: test it, break it, make sure it has the columns I need, and let my co-founder think about which columns to add. Another recall: how many times have I built dashboards like this, and what did I need in them? There should be fast links to every session, an "extract distillation" button, copy, starting a new session from the distillation. I will test it with other LLMs and want the fullest report.

#### What is already there

Since 23 June every machine drops its sessions into a shared folder, and a catalog merges them into one table. The copy on this laptop lists 6,406 sessions from six machines.

| what he asks for | state |
|---|---|
| fast links to every session | yes, a "Continue" button on every row |
| copy | yes, the summary copies in one click |
| columns | twelve: title, author, topic, last message, account, machine, folder, last activity, created, size, id, button |
| "extract distillation" | no |
| new session from a distillation | no; distillation lives separately in the "Book of chats" |

#### How many times

At least five over the summer: the shared catalog (23 June), an import health page (July, later retired), full-text search over every machine's sessions (14 July), a "what are the sessions doing now" screen (29 July) and sidebar grouping (10 September).

#### What breaks before any test

The merged table in this copy was built on 2 October; the freshest machine data arrived on 24 October. The new Mac Studio is not in it. So the order is: revive delivery from the machines, then add the two distillation buttons.

⚠️ Checked from one laptop. A newer version on the main computer would not be visible from here.

### I showed my son a Tesla and said: this is how Americans do it - reasonably reliable, great packaging. My own Tesla research from October says "reasonably" too

The note, posted at 16:58: I was showing my son a Tesla and said, look how Americans do it. You make something reasonably reliable, do great branding and packaging, and sell it. America is about cheap goods, lots of them, well advertised. Quality is not the point; it just has to not break and keep running. Or rather, comfort is not the point.

#### What the numbers say

| claim | measurement |
|---|---|
| "reasonably reliable" when new | Consumer Reports 2026: Tesla ninth on brand reliability, first time in the top 10, ~380,000 vehicles surveyed; only Cybertruck below average |
| "keeps running" with age | TÜV Report 2026, cars 2-3 years old: group average 6.5% significant defects, Model 3 13.1%, Model Y 17.3% (worst in group), brake discs and suspension; Consumer Reports 2025 used cars: last of 26 brands |
| "great packaging" | crash tests: Model Y 2025 IIHS Top Safety Pick+, Model 3 Euro NCAP 2025 five stars |

So his formula matches: new cars hold, mileage eats into them, and the visible side is strong. Comfort is not in these measurements; that part is his observation.

⚠️ Numbers come from our Tesla safety research of 30 October and the post of 4 November. German data does not transfer automatically to US cars or newer versions.

### I want my session start to run four searches in parallel, the way Hindsight does. All four kinds of search are already in my code. They run one after another, and two of the four are switched off

The note, posted at 17:10: I need to listen again to the podcasts about Hindsight, about agent memory and how it is implemented there. About parallel search. I think the session should start by running several searches in parallel; there are four kinds of them there. I need to look at how it works for me now and how Hindsight does it. I want to take them as a canary and improve my own search so that it also searches in parallel: the knowledge graph, the vector and something else. Four parallel searches, to find the context for a session as fast as possible.

#### What Hindsight does

Hindsight is an open-source agent memory from Vectorize. Its recall runs four strategies at once: vector search by meaning, BM25 keyword search, graph traversal over links, and a time filter. The results are fused into one list and re-ranked by a separate judge model.

#### What my search already has

The same four lanes:

| lane | state |
|---|---|
| vector, by meaning | always on |
| link graph (PageRank over note links) | on by default since 16 September, no regression on the benchmark |
| exact words (BM25 in SQLite) | **written, off**, waits for a flag |
| time | **written, off**, waits for a flag |

So the four kinds of search are not something to borrow. They are something already built and half switched on.

#### What is missing

Parallelism. Across the four search files there is not one thread or async call: the lanes run in turn.

And the switched-off lane has earned its place. Exact-word search alone, on a frozen set of 184 questions, found the right note 95% of the time by title, 98% by body and 100% by date, at 25 ms a query. It is weak only on "bridge" questions, where the answer sits between two notes (40%), and those are what the graph is for.

So the plan is short: switch on what is written, run the four lanes at once, and use Hindsight as the model for fusing the results.

⚠️ Hindsight's benchmark numbers (83.6% LongMemEval, 83.2% LoCoMo) are the authors' own. The time a parallel start would save is not measured: the lanes were never timed one by one.

### How the four posts connect

Three of today's notes ask to build something, and in all three the thing was already mostly built: the CRM ranking and daily queue, the fleet-wide sessions catalog, the four search lanes. The fourth note is not about building at all: it is about a car, and it says the same thing from the outside. A product that is "reasonably reliable" and well packaged wins; the numbers on Tesla show strong new cars, weaker ones with age, and a very strong window.

So today reads as one rule seen from two sides. Before building, check what is already there and only half switched on. And what exists is worth exactly as much as it still works after mileage: the CRM queue, the session catalog that stopped updating in October, and a Model Y at three years all pass or fail on the same test.
