### Three papers declined by arXiv, and the two questions that already have answers

The post is short and it is not a technical one. Three papers declined. Good papers, honestly written. Shock, and no idea what to do. Should a new account be registered? Did something go wrong inside this one?

Two of those questions have answers already written down in our own records, and the third has an answer on arXiv's own policy pages. I went and read all three before replying, because this is the kind of moment where a comforting guess does real damage.

**The account was never the problem, and endorsement never was either.** Both submissions passed submission and died at the moderator, on content. That distinction matters, because for months the working assumption had been that endorsement was the gate. It was not: a cs.MA endorsement had been granted on 17 and 20 July, the Le Chatelier paper went in on 23 July under cs.MA primary with cross-lists to cs.CE and cs.CR, and Homeostatic Governance followed as `submit/7910817`. Both reached the queue. Both were declined there.

The rejection letters name manuscripts, not users. The sentence is worth reading closely: "Attempts to resubmit **this manuscript** to arXiv will be rejected and **may** result in loss of submission privileges." Two qualifiers, both load-bearing. This manuscript, not the account. May, not will. The account is live; two manuscripts are banned; those are different facts, and conflating them is what turns a setback into a catastrophe in your head.

**On the count, our records say two, not three.** MOD-100285 on 17 August for `submit/7910817`, Homeostatic Governance. MOD-100874 on 19 August for `submit/7825136`, Le Chatelier Tokenomics. Identical wording in both: "does not contain sufficient original or substantive scholarly research and is not of interest to arXiv."

The third paper, the Agent-Fleet Experience Report, was never submitted to arXiv at all. It has been public on GitHub since 17 July and carries a Zenodo DOI, `10.5281/zenodo.22639714`. The 2 October decision was explicit on this: no third submission from the same account after two consecutive rejections. So if a third rejection exists, it arrived after 2 October and is not in our records, and I cannot reach the a2 inbox from this machine to check. I would rather name the gap than fill it with a guess. Nothing below changes either way.

**Why "good papers" and "declined" are not a contradiction.** On 31 October 2025 arXiv changed the rules for computer science: position papers and literature reviews in cs are accepted only after peer review at a journal or conference, and a workshop does not count. Both declined papers are precisely that genre. The letter says nothing about the quality of the writing. It says the submission does not contain substantive original research and is not of interest to arXiv, which for a conceptual paper with no journal behind it has become the default outcome rather than a verdict on the prose.

The corollary is the useful part: an empirical paper with its own measurements is substantially harder to decline than a conceptual one. That is the lever, and it is the single item on the October plan that has not moved.

**On registering a new account, I am going to quote them rather than paraphrase, because this price is not reversible.** From arXiv's identity policy, read live on 11 November:

> Each user may have only one registered arXiv account. [...] It is a violation of our policies to misrepresent your identity or organizational affiliation. Misrepresentation of identity or affiliation, **for any reason**, is possible grounds for **immediate and permanent suspension**.

Note "for any reason". It forecloses the honest version of the plan, the one where nobody is being deceived and the only wish is for the paper to be read. And the accounts would be linked regardless: same name, same ORCID. What is at stake is not the two dead papers. It is the venue, permanently, for every paper written for the rest of a career. A few weeks saved against all of arXiv forever is not a trade worth making, and it is the one place in this whole story where a bad decision cannot be undone later.

**Two more live facts from the same pages, both read today.** The rate limit: "Each author may submit up to **two new submissions per calendar month** with a limit of **three active submissions**," rejections count against it, and arXiv "may request that a particular author further limit their submission rate." After declines, an account sits under closer scrutiny, which is the mechanical reason a third attempt in the same stretch tends to go worse rather than better. And the appeal: it exists, "in some cases, we may require that a paper be accepted for publication in a conventional journal before an appeal is considered," followed by the sting, "decisions upon appeal are final, and no feedback will be provided with the decision." One shot, no explanation afterwards, and the same page adds that even a journal-accepted work may still be declined at arXiv's discretion.

So the appeal is only worth spending on a paper you would publish in a journal anyway, and it applies only to those two manuscripts. A new paper needs none of it and submits normally from the same account.

**The part that makes "life is broken" factually wrong.** Four DOIs already exist on Zenodo, obtained in a single morning once arXiv stopped being treated as the only door. The Agent-Fleet report has been public since July. `sqlite-graph-memory` logged 2,075 clones from 384 unique sources in fourteen days. None of that required a moderator's permission, and none of it disappeared in August.

⚠️ With one caveat worth stating, because it is easy to wait for something that will never arrive: Zenodo is not indexed by Google Scholar by design, per Zenodo's own answer. A DOI is not findability. The second home has to be your own pages carrying proper meta tags, plus Preprints.org, rather than Zenodo on its own.

**And the answer to "what do I do" was written on 2 October, with a deadline now nine days out.** One strong empirical submission to arXiv, the Fleet Experience Report expanded to full length, cs.MA, and the second only after the first passes. The two declined conceptual papers go to journals, Ledger at no cost or Kybernetes on the paid road, which is what makes an appeal possible later. Workshops: AAAI-27 Trustworthy Agents on **20 November**, AGENT@ICSE 2027 on 27 November carrying an IEEE DOI. JOSS not before 3 January 2027, which is a six-month repository-history rule and not a judgement.

That first empirical submission has not happened. The arXiv API returns zero results for both spellings of the name, checked while writing this. So the nearest real door is the workshop in nine days, and it is entirely indifferent to what happened in August.

**One last data point, the only documented case of getting past this exact rejection sentence.** It was done with a substantially extended version, not a resubmission. Which does not beat the rule, it follows it: new work, new submission, ordinary queue. That is the whole shape of the way out, and it does not require a new identity, an appeal, or anybody's sympathy.
