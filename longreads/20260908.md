The most developed story of the day begins with a small interface choice on GitHub. The same thought can be left as a comment in a discussion or submitted as a formal review. To the writer, the difference may feel cosmetic: the words can be identical. To the surrounding system, they become different objects.

That gap leads to a larger operating principle: **useful work has to be placed where its future reader will search for evidence of it.**

## A comment can help now and still disappear later

In the [English Telegram post](https://t.me/PaloAltoAi/2124), Anton describes reading other people's projects, finding mistakes, and sending advice to authors. He then notices that GitHub offers two neighboring ways to respond: a conversational comment and a review attached to the pull request.

His conclusion is personal and practical. He had been doing much of this work through comments, while believing that reviews are a more legible record of who examined a project. The lesson is not that every comment should become a review. Conversation and review serve different purposes. The lesson is to choose the object that matches the intended afterlife of the work.

A comment is optimized for the immediate thread. It asks or answers, moves a discussion forward, and can be exactly the right tool for an incomplete thought. A review is an explicit evaluation event. When the contribution is genuinely a review, recording it as one preserves that meaning for later readers.

This is a recurring systems problem. Humans judge work by its substance; software also judges it by its type, location, and metadata. If the substance is stored in the wrong object, future discovery becomes harder even when the original message was excellent.

## A health experiment should remain an experiment

The second readable story is a [Threads post about oil pulling](https://www.threads.com/@antondzi/post/DdB8x6hCkpH). Anton says he stopped using toothpaste, tried rinsing with oil, felt fine, and did not know what effect the practice actually had. He also relays claims from a ChatGPT deep-research answer.

The most important sentence is the uncertainty. A personal experience can be honestly reported without being promoted into medical guidance. Feeling fine does not establish effectiveness, and an AI-generated explanation does not replace clinical evidence or dental advice.

For the content archive, this item is therefore best classified as a personal experiment with an explicit unresolved question. Preserving that status is more useful than strengthening the claim after the fact. The archive should remember what was tried, what was felt, and what remains unproven.

## The day in one operating rule

September 8 produced a compact set of lessons:

- Put an evaluation into an evaluation-shaped object when future readers need to find it as evidence.
- Model one story and many placements instead of counting every cross-post as a new idea.
- Preserve uncertainty in personal experiments rather than upgrading it into advice.
- Record inaccessible source bodies as explicit omissions.
- Separate editorial dates from publication and repair dates.

All five are versions of the same rule: **make the record say what really happened, in the place and shape where the next reader can understand it.**
