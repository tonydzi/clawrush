# 26 September 2026: Learning in the Only Place Attention Exists

## What went out today

### Turning research into podcasts I listen to while driving

What helps me a lot right now with the job hunt and interview prep is that I taught my Claude to make podcasts for me in NotebookLM. I want to put them all on YouTube, and anyone who wants can listen: there is a lot in there that deserves attention.

I mostly want to get hired into an LLM lab, and you can apply to LLM labs, to LLM startups, and so on. The learning content is about Tensor and PyTorch, which are still scary words to me and which I am afraid of being asked about in an interview, plus things that have nothing to do with coding at all.

Learning all of it is not easy. But if it were not for NotebookLM and the endless drives to school, I would not have been able to learn any of it. When you sit in front of a computer, and I do not have one computer there but 5 computers and 9 screens, it is very hard to concentrate, especially on audio. In the car it is much easier.

By now I have many hours of audio I have not listened to yet, and every day it generates several lessons. Also, when I run a research session, a pile of text gets generated, and reading it is genuinely hard, so it is easier to pack it into audio and listen in the car.

If anyone wants the full setup for themselves, I will be glad to share. It is all on my GitHub, free to run locally or reuse. Please, help yourselves.

## How it connects

One post today, and it is the other half of the job-search thread running through this week.

The earlier posts were about output: applications per day, resume variants per role, pull requests opened before an interview. All of that is work aimed outward. This one is about input, and it names a constraint that no amount of pipeline tuning touches. The material exists, the time does not, and the only reliable slot is a drive.

The move worth copying is not the tool. It is matching the format to the place where attention actually is, rather than to the place where the work was produced. A research session ends in text because text is what a machine emits; it gets converted to audio because audio is what a driver can take in. The honest caveat sits in the post itself: hours are generated and unplayed, so production outran consumption, which is the same failure mode as a dashboard nobody reads.

## The numbers

One post today. Figures stated: 5 computers and 9 screens at the desk, several generated lessons per day, and an unplayed backlog measured in hours. All of them are the owner's own counts. No claim is made about what was retained, and none should be: generated is not learned.
