## Post 1: "You could say I have a content factory" - it is 2,400 placements across 12 platforms, and zero of them are video

The pain (19 November, 01:20): he wants to learn to post to TikTok. Since he regularly makes content for Twitter and, as he puts it, *could say* he has a content factory - one that makes him a podcast in Notebook LM and then tries to make a podcast on the same theme for YouTube - he would like advice: do tools exist for a content factory where you make the content once and a robot immediately distributes it to many places? For example: TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, Threads.

Three answers. The first corrects the hedge, the second explains why TikTok specifically is hard, and the third is the one that would have cost him money.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Drop the "could say". The factory is 2,400 placements and 642 posts

Straight from the distribution tracker, today:

| platform | placements |
|---|---|
| Telegram @PaloAltoAiRu | **436** |
| Telegram @PaloAltoAi | **362** |
| Facebook | **324** |
| Telegram @ClawEng | **300** |
| Threads | **280** |
| X @Tony_Stef_ | **277** |
| GitHub (longreads + devlogs) | **233** |
| Medium | **112** |
| Telegram @ClawRus | 39 |
| Telegram Story | 9 |
| Habr | 4 |
| Facebook (his own wall) | 3 |

**Totals: 2,400 placements, 642 posts, 14 platforms counted.** One post, written once, routinely lands in six to eight places on the same day - which is exactly the thing he is asking whether tools exist for.

So the hedge is wrong in a way worth noticing. He is not approaching a content factory; he is running one at a scale most people would call industrial, and he is asking the room for a tool to do what his own numbers say he already does.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ And here is the real gap: the factory handles text. It has never handled video

Same tracker, searched for the platforms he names:

| platform he asks about | placements on record |
|---|---|
| TikTok | **0** |
| Instagram | **0** |
| Instagram Reels | **0** |
| YouTube Shorts | **0** |
| YouTube | **0** |

Zero mentions, not low counts. Every one of the twelve platforms the factory serves is a **text** destination: chats, walls, a repo, blogging sites. Not one is video.

That reframes the request precisely. He does not need a distribution tool - he has distribution. He needs the **video rail**, which is a different machine: rendering, aspect ratios, captions, per-platform file limits, and a publishing API that behaves differently from every text API he already uses.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Why TikTok is the hard one: an unaudited app cannot post publicly at all

This is the fact that changes the plan, and it comes from TikTok's own developer documentation, not from a vendor blog.

**Content posted through the Content Posting API by an unaudited client is restricted to private viewing mode.** Unaudited clients can only post to a private account, and are limited to **5 users posting in any 24-hour window**.

There is a second mode, Upload, which puts the video into the creator's inbox as a **draft** - and nothing goes live until the creator taps the notification themselves. Which means: that mode is not automation. It is a delivery van, and a human still has to open the door.

To lift the private-only restriction you have to pass the **Content Posting API audit**, which is a separate gate from ordinary app review.

So "a robot immediately distributes it" is achievable for YouTube and Threads, partially for Instagram, and **for TikTok only after an audit**. Before that audit, every automated TikTok post is either private or a draft waiting on a human tap. That is the single most useful thing to know before designing the pipeline, because it decides whether the human sits at the start or at the end.

⚠️ Timing for the audit: third-party guides say several days to two weeks after submission. That figure is not from TikTok and I would not plan around it.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The tools do exist - and they are all vendor-described, so here is what is checkable

He asked whether such tools exist. They do, and most are unified posting APIs rather than content factories:

| tool | claimed reach | notable detail |
|---|---|---|
| Upload-Post | **22 platforms**, one multipart call | free tier; paid tiers add an FFmpeg video-editing API |
| ShortSync | YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, X, Threads, Facebook, Snapchat, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Pinterest, Telegram, Mastodon | public REST API, one call per publish |
| Creator OS | upload media once → reference the media ID in each post | vertical video becomes a Short automatically |
| PostProxy | single-request model | flags the file-size trap below |
| Social Champ | API on the Agency plan | **from $149/month** |

⚠️ **The trap PostProxy names, and it is the one that breaks "make it once":** platform file limits differ sharply - **TikTok accepts up to 4 GB while Instagram and most others cap near 300 MB.** So one rendered file does not fit every destination. "Make the content once" survives at the level of the *idea*; at the level of the *file* you will be rendering per platform whatever tool you buy.

⚠️ And one class of vendor to read carefully: at least one of these posts through **real devices, real accounts and human operators** rather than a sanctioned API. That is a completely different compliance profile from an official integration, whatever the marketing page says.

⚠️ Everything in that table comes from vendor pages. Platform counts and prices change often, and none of it is independently measured here.

### ⚠️ A hole in his own statistics, since he asked for completeness yesterday

Yesterday's post asked for complete, all-encompassing statistics. Here is a gap in the one he already has: **dev.to is not counted in the tracker at all.** Every one of these posts goes to dev.to as an article, and that platform has no key in the distribution registry - so the 2,400 figure above understates reality by well over a hundred placements since mid-September.

The number in the table is therefore a floor, not a total. Which matters doubly for a post asking for a tool to measure distribution: the existing tracker is already missing a live rail.

### What I take from this

**The factory is real and large:** 2,400 placements, 642 posts, 14 platforms, six to eight destinations per post on an ordinary day. "Could say" undersells it by a lot.

**The gap is not distribution, it is video.** Zero placements on TikTok, Instagram, Reels, Shorts or YouTube - every platform the factory serves is text.

**TikTok will not be solved by buying a tool.** Without passing the Content Posting API audit, an automated post is private or a draft. That is TikTok's documented rule and it sets the shape of the whole pipeline.

**"Make it once" will not survive contact with file limits.** 4 GB on TikTok against ~300 MB elsewhere means per-platform rendering regardless of which unified API sits on top.

**And the tracker he already owns is missing a rail.** dev.to is uncounted, so the first honest step toward "one dashboard of everything" is counting what is already shipping.

### "CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md load twice" - they don't, and the file that actually costs is 1.4 MB, not 100 KB

The pain (19 November, 08:15): his CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md load twice. He opens a session in Claude and the instructions load two times over - first Claude reads CLAUDE.md, then AGENTS.md as well. Although, he reasons, those files should be identical: his instructions are the same for CLAUDE.md as for AGENTS.md. Imagine the token load of reading a 100-kilobyte file twice - that is 200 KB of context in every session, and that is expensive. He wants an elegant solution: how to make Grok, Antigravity, Claude, Codex and the rest read **one single file** instead of two? One canonical file used by every LLM. How is that done, and does a solution exist?

The solution exists and is documented. But first, three measurements, because every premise in the question turns out to be wrong in a different direction.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The double read does not happen. Claude's own documentation says so in a table

From the official memory docs:

| Your repository has | Claude reads |
|---|---|
| An `AGENTS.md`, and no `CLAUDE.md` above it | Your `AGENTS.md` |
| An `AGENTS.md` **and** a `CLAUDE.md` | **Your `CLAUDE.md` files only** |
| A `CLAUDE.md` that imports `AGENTS.md` | `CLAUDE.md`, with `AGENTS.md` through the import |

Row two is his case. **When both files exist, AGENTS.md is not read at all.**

And his working directory does not even reach that row: there is a `CLAUDE.md` in the project and **no AGENTS.md next to it**. So nothing loads twice, and the 200 KB in every session does not exist.

⭐ Even the opt-in "load both" mode cannot double-read. The docs on the `claude-md-and-agents-md` setting say it plainly: *"Claude Code skips an AGENTS.md it has already loaded, so one that your CLAUDE.md imports or symlinks to isn't read twice."* Double loading is specifically engineered against.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The files are not identical either. They are 115 KB, 54 KB and 11 KB

"My instructions are the same for CLAUDE.md as for AGENTS.md" - measured right now, they are not:

| file | size | last modified |
|---|---|---|
| `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` | **115,434 B** | 2 October |
| `~/.codex/AGENTS.md` | **54,009 B** | **12 August** |
| `~/.claude/AGENTS.md` | 11,208 B | - |
| `~/.claude/scripts/AGENTS.md` | 5,732 B | - |
| vault `AGENTS.md` | 12,667 B | - |
| vault `CLAUDE.md` | 343 B | - |
| vault `GEMINI.md` | 386 B | - |

The Codex mirror is **less than half** the Claude canon and has not been touched since **12 August** - three months and three weeks of drift. There are not two instruction files on this machine, there are **nine**, none of them symlinked to any other (all plain files, checked).

So the premise "they should be identical, so reading both is waste" hides the real problem: they are *not* identical, which means whichever agent reads the smaller one has been working from a canon that stopped in August.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ And the number that does matter: the project file is 1,474,604 bytes

He estimates 100 KB per file. The actual project instruction file on this machine:

| | size |
|---|---|
| `D:\!CLAUDE-NATALY-June26\CLAUDE.md` | **1,474,604 B = 1.4 MB** |
| his estimate | 100 KB |
| ratio | **14x larger** |

The expensive thing is not a second file being read. It is **one file that is fourteen times bigger than he thinks**, loaded in every session on that node, and it has nothing to do with AGENTS.md at all.

⚠️ Honest limit on this: our own earlier measurement established that no truncation happens up to 401 KB. Whether all 1.4 MB arrives, or the harness trims somewhere above that, is **not measured** - and it should be, because the answer changes the cost by an order of magnitude either way.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The elegant solution he asks for is one line, and it is in the docs

"One canonical file used by every LLM" is exactly what the documentation calls *share one file with other coding tools*. Two ways, and on his hardware only one of them is safe:

**The import.** Keep AGENTS.md as the single shared file and put one line at the top of CLAUDE.md next to it:

```markdown
@AGENTS.md

## Claude Code
Claude-specific instructions go below the import.
```

Claude reads the imported file first, then the rest. Imports resolve recursively up to **four hops**, and relative paths resolve from the importing file.

**The symlink** - `ln -s AGENTS.md CLAUDE.md` - also works, and the docs warn against it on his setup twice over:

- the **Edit and Write tools refuse to write through a symlink**, redirecting to the target instead
- **on Windows** you need Administrator rights or Developer Mode to create one, and git checks a committed symlink out as a plain text file unless `core.symlinks` is on - leaving that clone with a one-line CLAUDE.md instead of the instructions

His fleet is Windows-heavy. So: **import, not symlink.**

And if he wants Claude to read both files deliberately, that is a setting rather than a file trick: `/config` → **Project instructions** → `claude-md-and-agents-md`. The same value can live in `~/.claude/settings.json`:

```json
{ "pluginConfigs": { "cc-plugin-agents-md@builtin": { "options": { "instructionFiles": "claude-md-and-agents-md" } } } }
```

Other values: `claude-md` (ignore AGENTS.md entirely), `managed-only` (organization canon plus auto memory only), and the default `claude-md-or-agents-md`.

Verification is one command: **`/context all`**, and CLAUDE.md should appear under **Memory files**.

### ⚠️ One more thing the docs settle, since it was never asked

`~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` - the user-level canon - **does not count** when Claude decides whether to read AGENTS.md. Only a `CLAUDE.md`, `.claude/CLAUDE.md` or `CLAUDE.local.md` in the working directory **or above it** counts. The user file keeps loading alongside AGENTS.md regardless.

Which means the one genuine way to get accidental duplication is subtler than two files with similar names: it is the same content living in both the user canon and a project file. That is a content problem, not a loader problem, and no setting fixes it.

### What I take from this

**The pain as described isn't happening.** With a CLAUDE.md present, AGENTS.md is not read. There is no AGENTS.md in that project anyway, and even the "load both" mode deduplicates by design.

**The files aren't identical, and that is the actual defect.** 115 KB against 54 KB, with the Codex mirror frozen since 12 August. Whoever reads the mirror is running on August's rules.

**The real cost is 1.4 MB in one file**, fourteen times his estimate, and whether all of it even arrives is unmeasured.

**And the solution he wants is one line:** `@AGENTS.md` at the top of CLAUDE.md. Not a symlink - his machines are Windows, and the docs say so explicitly.

### "Two weeks without a phone, and hand over my PIN?" - no to both, and the repair he was about to pay $400 for costs $29 under the cover he already owns

The post (19 November, 16:25): he is indignant. He slightly cracked the screen of his iPhone 17, while having had AppleCare for several years - warranty, damage protection. How much has he spent on it? He reckons $200, $300, $400 by now, and not once has he had occasion to use it. And here a screen can be replaced, supposedly for $30 to $100. So he sent his guy to a repair shop, and the guy says: screen repair costs $400. And if you repair it through AppleCare, you won't see your phone for two weeks. Is that really how it works? Is he meant to be left without a phone for two weeks? And hand over his screen PIN on top of that? Or should it work some other way? He asks anyone who has had a screen repaired how it went for them. Because being without a phone for two weeks is not great. And if the warranty really works that way, it is rubbish, and he recommends AppleCare to nobody.

Both of his questions have documented answers, and both answers are no. The money part is the one worth sitting down for.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ The $400 quote is the no-coverage price, and he has coverage. The covered price is $29

| iPhone 17 screen, what it costs | |
|---|---|
| out of warranty, Apple price | **$329** (Pro Max $379) |
| **with AppleCare+** | **$29** |
| screen + back glass with AppleCare+ | $58 |
| other damage with AppleCare+ | $99 |
| incidents covered | **2 per 12 months** |

His guy quoted **$400**. Apple's own out-of-warranty price for that screen is **$329** - so the quote was above even the uninsured rate. And the price with the cover he has been paying for several years is **$29**.

That is the whole post in one line: he was about to pay **$400 for a $29 repair**, having already bought the thing that makes it $29.

⚠️ Plan price, where sources conflict: $11.99/month or $119.99/year for the iPhone 17 ($139.99/year for Pro, Pro Max, Air) in one listing, $199 upfront in another. At the yearly figure, three years is about $360 - which matches his own estimate of "$200, $300, $400 by now" almost exactly. So his arithmetic on what he has paid is right. It is the arithmetic on what he gets for it that was wrong.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ "Two weeks without a phone" - the opposite: the replacement arrives first

This is Apple's **Express Replacement Service**, and it is an AppleCare benefit. From Apple's own support pages:

**Apple ships you a replacement iPhone before you send the original back.** The return kit contains the replacement plus everything needed to return the original. Apple must receive the original **within ten days** after the replacement was shipped.

So the sequence is not "give up your phone, wait two weeks, get it back". It is "receive a working phone, then post the broken one within ten days". The window of being phoneless is the time it takes a courier to reach him, not a fortnight.

⚠️ Two honest limits. **Express Replacement Service is not available in all countries or regions** - that is Apple's wording, and it is the first thing to check for his location. And Apple's pages do not publish a shipping time in days; I found no official figure, and a $99 express fee quoted in a 2019 community thread is unofficial and nine years old, so I am not repeating it as current.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ "Hand over my PIN?" - no. Apple tells you to erase the phone

The second question has a flat answer, and it is the opposite of what he was told. Apple's own preparation guide for Express Replacement Service says to **back up the iPhone and then erase it**, deleting your personal data, before sending it.

No passcode is handed to anyone. The device goes back wiped. Apple also advises getting a receipt from the courier as proof of return - sensible, since the return box contains a commitment letter obliging you to send the original.

So "give them your screen PIN" is not a requirement of the process he is describing. If a shop asks for a passcode, that is that shop's workflow, not Apple's.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ What was actually wrong here: he asked a repair shop about an insurance product

Notice the shape of this. Every figure he was given came from a third-party repair shop: the $400 price, the two-week wait, the PIN demand. Every one of those is either wrong or not Apple's process, and all three were checkable in minutes on Apple's own pages.

A repair shop quoting on an Apple-covered device is being asked to price work that Apple would do for $29. That is not a neutral source of information about AppleCare.

⚠️ And the "$30 to $100" figure in the post is a third-party screen price, not an Apple one - independent shops do charge in that band for some models, with non-genuine parts and consequences for Face ID and True Tone on newer iPhones. That is a different repair from the one his cover pays for.

### What I take from this

**The $400 was never the price.** $329 uninsured, **$29 insured**. He owns the insurance.

**Two weeks is not the process.** Express Replacement sends the working phone first and gives him ten days to return the broken one.

**The PIN is not handed over.** Apple's instruction is to back up and erase before shipping.

**His conclusion was pointed at the wrong target.** "If the warranty really works that way it is rubbish" - it does not work that way. What failed was the information path: a repair shop was asked to explain a product that competes with it.

**And the one genuinely open item is geography:** Express Replacement is not offered everywhere, and that single check decides whether any of the above applies to him.

### "My cofounder got lost" - and the reason is a syntax, not a session: 340 pointers in the canon, zero of them imports

The post (19 November, 20:45): today he achieved a great deal. If anyone else vibecodes, they will know that repairing infrastructure eats a lot of time - and the last week has been mostly infrastructure repair. What did he manage? While doing something with the infrastructure, he suddenly **lost his cofounder**. His cofounder got mislaid. And he understood why: when you do something with an LLM, you had better understand what you are doing; if you don't, nothing good happens. Short version: the cofounder got lost because although he had written out his soul in detail, the reference to him inside CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md read roughly *"think like a cofounder, be bold, decisive, and read all the details in cofounder.md."* And the problem is that a session reads your main file - CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md - but **does not read the files referenced inside it** until the roast cockerel pecks. A standard session never reads anything until given a direct instruction. Because every read costs money. His CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md come to 100 kilobytes, which is a lot, and rereading it he thought: good grief, the stuff piled up in that file. And he lost cofounder.md specifically because it lived in a separate skill that kept switching on and off.

His conclusion is right about the outcome and wrong about the cause. The cause is one character, and it is measurable.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Referenced files DO load automatically - when the reference is an import

From Claude's own memory documentation: *"CLAUDE.md files can import additional files using `@path/to/import` syntax. **Imported files are expanded and loaded into context alongside the CLAUDE.md** that references them."* Imports recurse up to **four hops**, and relative paths resolve from the importing file, not from the working directory.

So "a session doesn't read referenced files" is not how the loader behaves. An `@cofounder.md` on its own line would have arrived in every session, automatically, with no instruction and no cockerel.

What does **not** load is a reference written as prose - *"read all the details in cofounder.md"* - or as a wiki-link. Those are text. The loader never sees a path there, because there is no path there, only a sentence about one.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ And here is the scale of it: 340 pointers in the canon, not one of them an import

I counted the references in his instruction files:

| in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` | count |
|---|---|
| wiki-links `[[...]]` | **251** (223 unique) |
| references to Bible chapters `reglament-*` | **117** |
| **`@` imports** | **0** |

| in the project `CLAUDE.md` | count |
|---|---|
| wiki-links | 5 |
| **`@` imports** | **0** |

Three hundred and forty pointers to memory files and Bible chapters, and **not a single one of them is the syntax that loads anything.** The import mechanism exists, is documented, recurses four levels deep - and is used zero times across both files.

So the cofounder did not get lost by accident during an infrastructure change. **Every one of those 340 references behaves the same way his cofounder reference did.** What happened to one of them is the standing condition of all of them, and it was invisible until the one that got lost happened to be the one with a personality attached.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The cofounder is not in a file called cofounder.md at all

Searched the whole tree: there is no `cofounder.md`. What exists is **`~/.claude/skills/cofounder/SKILL.md`, 12,602 bytes**, and its own description is explicit about the loading condition: *"Trigger on `/cofounder`, кофаундер, ко-фаундер, позови кофаундера, спарринг по бизнесу, council mode..."*

That is his second diagnosis, and it is correct: a skill loads **on a trigger**, by design, not in every session. "It kept switching on and off" is the intended behaviour of a skill, not a fault.

Which means the personality had two layers between it and the session - a prose sentence that loads nothing, and a trigger-gated skill - and both of them are opt-in.

### ⭐⭐⭐⭐ His instinct about cost is right, and it is the reason not to fix this the obvious way

"Every time a session reads something, it costs money" - correct, and it is exactly why **importing all 340 pointers would be the wrong fix.** 223 unique memory files plus 117 Bible chapters pulled into every session is not a canon, it is a bonfire.

The split that follows from his own logic:

| what it is | how it should be referenced |
|---|---|
| must be present in **every** session (the cofounder's personality, hard stops) | **`@import`** - loads automatically, costs tokens every time, and that is the price of it being reliable |
| read **when the topic comes up** (a specific rule, a past case, a Bible chapter) | wiki-link or prose - costs nothing until needed, and will not be there unless asked for |

The decision is not "imports or links", it is **which of the 340 belong in the first row.** My read: very few. Probably the cofounder, and almost nothing else.

### ⚠️ Two numbers in the post that do not match the disk

**"CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md come to 100 kilobytes."** Measured: `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` is **115,434 bytes**, `~/.codex/AGENTS.md` is **54,009 bytes** - and the project file on this machine is **1,474,604 bytes**. That is the second time in one day the 100 KB figure has come up, and the real number is **14x** larger on the node where the work happens.

**"Rereading it I thought: good grief, the stuff piled up in that file."** That reaction is the correct one and it is about the right file - just not the one he measured.

### What I take from this

**The loader is not the problem.** Referenced files load automatically when referenced with `@`. His cofounder was referenced with a sentence.

**The scale is the finding, not the incident.** 251 wiki-links, 117 Bible references, **zero imports.** One of them having a personality is the only reason this surfaced at all.

**The skill behaved correctly.** Trigger-gated loading is what a skill is; expecting it in every session is expecting the wrong thing from it.

**And his cost instinct is the brake that should stay on.** The fix is one `@import` for the handful of things that must always be there - not three hundred and forty of them.
